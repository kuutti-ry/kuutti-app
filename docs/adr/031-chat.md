# ADR-031: The chat: own sockets and tables, the conversation as the match's record, and the retention of messages

- Status: accepted (the maintainer chose the own build on 2026-10-17, #101; the first piece, #348, is built as written here; the points under "Open, the maintainer's" are his to confirm or change), amended 2026-10-17 by §9 (#349: the live socket, the library it runs on and delivery on commit) and 2026-10-18 by §10 (#350: a photo in a message) and §11 (#103: contacts shared in a conversation)
- Date: 2026-10-17
- Follows: TD-1 (a message belongs to the conversation), TD-3 (one box, in-process state, no Redis), TD-5 (events never carry text), TD-7 (the erasure table: the counterpart's message copies are kept), TD-13 (silent matches are archived), ADR-007 §4, ADR-008 §3, ADR-011 §1 and §9, ADR-021, ADR-022 §4, ADR-024 §6 and §8, ADR-026, ADR-029 §4, ADR-030 §2, §3, §5, §6 and §8, CLAUDE.md rules 5 to 9 and Product constraints, `.claude/rules/api.md` (WebSockets), `.claude/rules/db.md`, `docs/security-checklist.md`; issues #101, #348, #349, #350, #351, #100, #102, #103, #105

## Context

Matched people need to talk to each other in the app (#101). Olli's input of 15/09/2026, as #101's comment of 10/10/2026 records it, was to build the chat on an existing messaging platform such as Matrix, "a mature server-side implementation out of the box", with a client SDK that leaves only the UI to us. The comment asked for a costed record before the milestone started. On 17/10/2026 the maintainer asked whether an in-between exists: a JavaScript or TypeScript library that does chat and reporting, lighter than a homeserver. None does. What exists is hosted services with SDKs (Stream, Sendbird, Twilio Conversations, TalkJS, CometChat), full servers in other languages (Matrix: Synapse, or Tuwunel and Continuwuity as Rust single binaries; XMPP; Tinode; Rocket.Chat; Ethora, a Node layer over ejabberd), transport libraries in TypeScript (Socket.IO, and `ws`, which `@hono/node-server` 2 upgrades to on its own), and React Native UI kits (react-native-gifted-chat in maintenance mode, Flyer Chat's React Native package unmaintained).

Nothing gives chat semantics as a module to embed, because every rule the chat has is a join against Kuutti's own tables: who may write to whom is the match (#100) less the blocks (ADR-030 §2), the sanctions (ADR-024 §6) and erasure; what a message may say is the plain-text rule (#47, #103); what erasure keeps is TD-7's kept side; what a report freezes is ADR-030 §4's copy (#351); what a push may say is #102's; what #105 counts is per account. A platform carries the bytes and holds the text; the rules stay in our handlers. So a platform saves the transport, the small part, and adds a second store of the text to keep in step with ours, the large one.

The maintainer chose the own build on 17/10/2026 and split #101 into pieces. The first, #348, needs the tables, the send and its refusals, the list, the archive of silent conversations, erasure and the export, and this record of the tables and the retention of messages. The match itself is M5's (#96, #97, #100) and not built; the conversation row comes into M2 as the match's record, which #100 later writes in the statement of the second like.

## Decision

1. **The own build.** `@hono/node-server`'s own WebSocket upgrade over `ws` in the API's one process for the live part (#349, §9), the conversation, participant and message tables in Postgres, presence in memory, every socket frame through a zod schema of `packages/schema`, and LISTEN/NOTIFY for delivery on commit (§9), which would also serve a second instance if one ever exists (rules/api.md). The three options were costed against the same rows, rules 5 to 9, TD-7, ADR-030 §2, §4 and §8, the issues that depend on the chat, the one box, a new dependency and a new recipient in the privacy policy:

   | | A: a Matrix homeserver | B: a hosted service | C: the own build |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Rule 5, no text in events or logs | a second store of the text with its own logs, outside our logger and its PII test | the vendor's logs are theirs | one logger, one PII test |
   | Rule 6, every query scoped by the session's account | who reads a room is the homeserver's membership | the vendor's channel membership | the query, as everywhere |
   | Rule 7, only matched, unblocked, unsanctioned people write | the match, the block and the sanction mirrored into room membership, with no shared transaction | the same against the vendor's API | the send's own statement |
   | Rule 8, no web surface | the client API answers any origin unless proxied | the vendor's API is a web surface by design | one socket under the session guard |
   | TD-7, the counterpart's copy under "Deleted user" | a redaction deletes for both; a deactivated user keeps what is unmeasured | the vendor's deletion | the sender's link cut, the rows kept |
   | ADR-030 §2 and §4, a block ends it; the frozen copy | a membership change after the block's commit; the copy read out of the homeserver | the vendor's freeze and export | the block's own statement; a copy of our rows |
   | #102, a push with no text | Sygnal speaks APNs and FCM, not Expo: a gateway of ours | the vendor's push or their webhook | our API, Expo |
   | One box | a second process and a RocksDB directory to back up | every message crosses the Internet twice | the one process |
   | A new recipient in the privacy policy | none | the vendor and its sub-processors; "Nobody else" goes | none |

   A's client is no shortcut on Expo either: matrix-js-sdk targets web and Node, and matrix-rust-sdk's official bindings are Swift, Kotlin, JavaScript and Node, none for React Native. B breaks "never paste user data into any external service" for every message. Socket.IO was weighed for its reconnect machinery: its connection state recovery exists on its in-memory adapter only, its documentation says it will not always succeed, and its client's buffer of sends across a reconnect doubles a message whose acknowledgement was lost. So resume and idempotency are the application's with either library (§3), and the thinner one stays.

2. **The tables** (migration 0053, `generate --name chat`; the two rows of §6 are migration 0054, `generate --custom`, since a generated file is never edited):
   - `conversation(id, opened_at, last_message_at, next_seq, archived_at, closed_at, closed_how, closed_by)`. `next_seq` is the sequence the next message takes, from 1. `closed_how` is `conversation_close`: `block`, `unmatch`, `erasure` or `sanction`; `closed_by` is the `conversation_side` whose act or account closed it. A check holds the three together, all null or all set.
   - `conversation_participant(id, conversation_id, account_id, side, read_up_to, joined_at)`: one row per side, `a` or `b`; unique `(conversation_id, side)` and `(conversation_id, account_id)`, two nulls being distinct; an index on `account_id` for the list. `read_up_to` is the highest sequence the person has read, never lowered. `account_id` goes null at erasure and the row stays.
   - `message(id, conversation_id, seq, side, client_id, kind, body, created_at)`: unique `(conversation_id, seq)` and `(conversation_id, client_id)`; `kind` is `message_kind`, `text` alone until #350 adds `photo`; a check that a text has a body.
   - `closed_by` is not in #348's first sketch. It is what lets the blocker never see a conversation again once the other person's erasure has cut the link a live block test needs (ADR-030 §6): without it the conversation would come back to the blocker as "Deleted user", and tell them the other left. #351 writes `block` with the blocker's side; a close for another reason where a block stands writes `block` too, the side of the one who stays preferred where both blocked, and an erasure or a ban rewrites an earlier close as the block's where one stands by then (a ban, then a block from an earlier card, then the erasure).

3. **The send, and the resume.** One transaction (`apps/api/src/chat/messages.ts`): the caller's account row held `FOR SHARE`, only in a state the session guard admits and with the identity in good standing (an erasure or a sanction finishes first, or waits, and then the send writes nothing), the conversation's row locked `FOR UPDATE` through the caller's participant row, then what the send decides on read in a statement of its own, so a block, a close or an erasure that committed while it waited is seen. Every message carries an id the phone generates; a `client_id` already stored answers 200 with that message and writes nothing, so a resend after a lost answer doubles nothing; one the other side used is refused. The refusals, each in words through the envelope: 404 `not_found` for a conversation not the caller's or hidden from its blocker; 409 `conversation_closed` when it is closed, when `blocked_between` holds, or when the counterpart is erased; 400 `message_too_long` over `message_max_length` characters after trimming (an empty body is the schema's 400); 400 `contact_details` where `contactDetailsIn` (#145) finds an e-mail address, a link, a phone number or a handle, the kind logged and never the text; 429 `message_budget_exceeded` with `Retry-After` over `messages_per_minute`, a limiter of ADR-026 keyed by the account whose limit is the `matching_config` row read on each request. Every statement of the send, the lookup of the phone's id and the insert included, names the caller's account. The sequence is taken in the statement that moves the conversation on (`next_seq + 1`, `last_message_at`, out of the archive), so two sends at once take consecutive numbers with no gap whatever the order. Resume is the phone asking for what is newer than the last sequence it holds (`?after=`), which #349's socket reuses at every connect: nothing lost, nothing doubled, and a restart of the process costs only the presence map. Read receipts are `read_up_to`, fanned out by #349.

4. **What each side reads.** Every statement a person's request runs starts from their own participant row (rule 6). A closed conversation reads as one of two things: "This person deleted their account", or "This conversation has ended" for every other cause, so the person blocked learns nothing of the block (ADR-030 §6) and nobody learns of the other's ban (ADR-024 §6). A block that stands closes the conversation in the reads while #351 has not yet written the close, and hides it from its blocker, who gets 404 from every route and no row in the list; the word "blocked" appears nowhere. The list groups open conversations first and the archived and the closed after (#351's "archived group"), each newest first by the last message the caller may read, else by the opening. A counterpart is a name, an age and a thumb, or `{ deleted: true }`; never an account id. The thumb is the main photo's, signed for fifteen minutes and written to the fetch log under the day's thumb budget (ADR-008); the conversation is the proof of contact, asked in the statement that finds the photo through the viewer's own participant row, as a card shown is for a report (ADR-030 §5), so it never counts as a card. There is none across a block, for somebody not active, or once the conversation has ended, so a lifted ban never shows as an ended conversation with a photo. The home screen's count asks for the list without thumbs, and the list signs them on its first read and on a pull only, a look back carrying the earlier ones, so neither spends the day's budget on every return. A shadow-banned sender's messages are stored and shown to the sender and withheld from the counterpart while the ban is open, from the list's preview and unread count too; they appear once it is lifted (#105).

5. **The retention of messages.** A message is kept as long as its conversation, and a conversation as long as one of its two people still has an account; nothing is deleted on an archive or a close (TD-1; #351's copy and its window need the rows). Erasure (`eraseAccount`, ADR-007 §4, the one place) cuts the person's link and closes their open and archived conversations as `erasure`; the counterpart keeps every message they could read, under "Deleted user" (TD-7's kept side). What the counterpart never received, the person's messages sent under a shadow ban still open, goes first, since the ban itself goes with the erasure (ADR-024 §8) and would otherwise let them appear. When the second person of a conversation is erased, nobody is left to keep the words for, and the conversation goes with its messages. The export carries, per conversation, when it opened and how it stands, the person's own messages with their words, and the counterpart's as a time and a length only, read in the database without the words: the other person's words stay theirs (Open, 3).

6. **The parameters**, rows of `matching_config` born in migration 0054 and in the seed's `MATCHING_CONFIG_V1`: `message_max_length` 2000 characters, `messages_per_minute` 30 (#101 proposed 60; nobody writes faster than one message every two seconds). `silent_match_archive_days` 7 is a row already (TD-13). The list's preview is the first 80 characters, a constant of the contract, the maintainer's to switch to the kind alone.

7. **Events, logs.** Three events in the registry, server-side and keyed by `research_id` (ADR-011), their names the research partner's to confirm: `message_sent { kind, length, firstInConversation, sinceOpened, sincePrevious }` for the sender, `conversation_archived { messages, days }` for each person, `conversation_closed { how, messages }` for the person on the other side from the one acted on, never for the erased one, and only for an erasure or (with #351) an unmatch: a block and a ban write no event (ADR-030 §9, ADR-024), and `how` takes the two values a person reads. The length, the waits, the counts of messages and the age of the conversation are bands (`1-20` to `1001+` characters, and `0` for a photo without a caption, #350; under a minute to over a week; `0` to `101+` messages; `0-6` to `90+` days), not counts or seconds: an exact value beside the event's hour would point at one message row and rebuild a deleted mapping (ADR-011 §1), and the research copy releases a value only where k people share it (ADR-029 §4). Never the text (rule 5). The logs carry the request line only; the logger redacts a field named `body`, top level and one level down, next to `note` (ADR-030 §9), and the PII test sends a message fixture through every route.

8. **The archive.** The night (`archive-silent-conversations`, `apps/api/src/chat/archive.ts`) sets `archived_at` where neither it nor `closed_at` is set and the last message, or the opening, is older than `silent_match_archive_days`, and since #103 the newest share of a contact too (§11); the next message clears it. A break stops no clock (ADR-022 §4). A ban closes the person's conversations in the ban's transaction (`closeConversationsOf`, ADR-024); a suspension closes none, and they wait for its end.

9. **The live socket** (amendment of 2026-10-17, #349). GET /ws on the API's own server: `@hono/node-server` 2 upgrades by itself over `ws` (`websocket: { server }` on `serve`, `apps/api/src/index.ts`); `@hono/node-ws`, which #101 and #349 named, is deprecated by its maintainers, and its peer range excludes the node-server the API runs. `ws` is pinned (MIT, no install scripts). A frame is at most 256 KiB, bounded before it is read (`maxPayload`, `FRAME_BYTES_MAX`).
   - **The door.** The upgrade passes every app-wide middleware: the address's limiter counts it and never gives it back, since no guard admits it (ADR-026). A browser's Origin must be the API's own host or on the CORS allowlist, or the upgrade is refused with 403: React Native names its own URL's origin, a native client or a test none (rule 8). The first frame is `auth { token }` within `WS_AUTH_TIMEOUT_MS`, checked by the session guard's own code (`authenticate`, `lib/auth-middleware.ts`), so the token never sits in a URL and the web preview, which cannot set a header on a WebSocket, runs the same code. Anything else first, a second `auth` or `resume`, or a frame outside `packages/schema/src/chat-frames.ts` closes the socket with 1008 after an `error` frame naming why. A second `auth` is refused before the first's lookup ends, no frame is read after a refusal (`ws` delivers frames until the close is answered), and a phone gone while its session is looked up, touched or asked once more registers nothing and is announced online by nobody. While the listener is off the channel, an `auth` is told `restarting` (1012) before its token is looked up, and again if the listener went while it was: a socket admitted then would miss what is committed until the listener is back. A log line names the account, the request and the code, never a frame or a token.
   - **Staying honest.** Every `WS_PING_INTERVAL_MS` the socket asks the session by its id, which a refresh keeps, while it rotates the hash: an ended session gives `session_revoked`, and an access token past its time gives `session_expired`. The app refreshes ahead while its socket is open. No `pong` within `WS_PONG_TIMEOUT_MS` closes the socket with 1001. At most `WS_SOCKETS_PER_ACCOUNT` sockets per account; the next one closes the oldest. These are configuration, not product rules (`lib/config.ts`).
   - **Delivery on commit.** Triggers on `message`, `conversation`, `conversation_participant`, `session` and `block` (migration 0055, `generate --custom`) send ids and numbers, never text, on the channel `kuutti_chat`. Postgres delivers a notification when the writing transaction commits and drops it when that transaction rolls back. One connection of the pool listens (`chat/listen.ts`). The hub (`chat/live.ts`) takes one notification at a time, in commit order, and reads what each recipient may see through their own participant row, with every rule of §4. So every write path reaches the sockets without a call of its own: today's, #350's photos and #351's block and unmatch. An ended session (a logout, a break, a sanction, an erasure) closes its sockets at once. This is rules/api.md's LISTEN/NOTIFY, taken now for the commit rather than for a second instance; a second instance would listen the same way. A lost listener closes every socket with `restarting` (1012), and the phones resume from Postgres.
   - **Presence and resume.** A person is online while a socket is open and offline when the last one closes. A shadow-banned account is never online to anybody (ADR-024 §6), and nothing is told across a close or a block. A shadow ban leaves the session live, so each ping reads it: one put on while a socket is open hides the person at the next ping, one lifted shows them again, and a socket opened since carries the newest word to the account's others. A counterpart is told how the person stands when the frame is sent, so two changes close together end on the truth. A resume names each conversation once, and one read of the list answers it: every conversation it does not name gets its presence first, since a phone resumes before its list has loaded; then, for each it names, at most one page (`MESSAGES_PAGE_MAX`) of the messages after the phone's sequence, the oldest it lacks, then the read mark, the presence and the state, or `gone` for a conversation the phone may not see. A phone further behind reads the rest over HTTP when it next opens the conversation (a `shortcut:` in `chat/live.ts`): one frame cannot make the box replay a history. The registry is the process's memory (`chat/presence.ts`); #102 asks it whether somebody has a live socket.

10. **A photo in a message** (amendment of 2026-10-18, #350). One request uploads and sends: `POST /conversations/{id}/photos`, multipart, the picture, the phone's id and an optional caption, which is the message's body under §3's text rules. The picture goes through the profile photo's pipeline (rule 4, ADR-005) as a row of `photo` with `purpose = message` and no position; one table with a purpose, not a second, so erasure, the variants, the fetch log and the signed URLs serve both. The order: what is known without a pixel is refused first (no such conversation, a resend answered with the stored message, the other side's id, closed, the caption, the day's photos); then the pipeline and the check (ADR-006), what the check is sure of refused in words and nothing stored; then the objects; then one transaction, §3's own with the sender's lock for the day's count, writing the photo row, its review row and the message (`message.kind = photo`, `message.photo_id`, migration 0056). Every path that writes no row removes the objects again unless another row shares the content. Rekognition is sent a copy padded to at least 80 px on each side, its least size, so a tiny picture is checked rather than refused as busy. The check's note for a refused photo (#253) names no photo, since none was stored, and is written only while the account is live: it waits for an erasure under way and never outlives it. At most `matching_config message_photos_per_day` (10, migration 0057) a Finnish day, and `message_photo_attempts_per_day` (30) tries, refused ones included, since a refused photo is stored nowhere and still costs the pipeline and the check. The tries are counted in the process's memory per person and end at midnight in Finland as the day's photos do; a restart forgets them (TD-3, one box). Either refusal is 429 `message_photo_budget_exceeded` with `Retry-After` until midnight. A photo counts against `messages_per_minute` too, one bucket with the texts, and writes §7's `message_sent` with the kind `photo` and the caption's length band, `0` without one. A photo no check saw is delivered only on a developer's machine or in a test (`deliverUnchecked`); anywhere else it is refused as busy, so a missing moderation parameter never lets one through. A page of the conversation, the read of one message and the send's answer to its sender (201, and 200 for a resend) sign each photo's thumb and full size for their reader until the next five-minute mark and fifteen minutes more, so a URL lives 15 to 20 minutes and, on CloudFront, a read again within the five minutes is handed the same URL. The fetch log has one row per viewer, photo and variant in each window, written by one statement per answer that skips what the window holds already, with the purpose `message`, never counted against the day's fetches (ADR-008); two reads at the same instant, in two transactions, may each write theirs, which costs nothing for that reason. A photo follows its message, so whoever reads the message sees it, and a block is not told apart from an unmatch (ADR-030 §6). A socket frame carries the photo without URLs (rules/api.md: URLs only inside rate-limited answers), and the phone reads that one message again for them. The sender's erasure takes the photo and leaves the message reading "Photo removed" (`photo_id` set null); #351's frozen copy will name the photos to keep (`erasePhotosOfAccount`'s `keep`). The export's lines carry their kind, and each of one's own lines with a photo an object: its file in the zip, null when the zip does not carry it (the object gone, or the photo not among the newest hundred sent: a `shortcut:`, since the zip is built in memory); the check's outcome as a profile photo's review is shown, where `queued` is a photo the check was unsure of, delivered with no human queue; and its automatic description, in keys and in words in the request's language. Each file the zip carries is logged as a fetch with the purpose `message`, never counted (ADR-007). The check written in 0056 names no new kind (`photo_id IS NULL OR kind <> 'text'`): a value added to an enum cannot be used in the migration's own transaction on a database that already had the enum.

11. **Contacts shared in a conversation** (amendment of 2026-10-18, #103). The one way through §3's refusal of a phone number or a handle in a message, as the maintainer decided it in chat on 18/10/2026 (#103's last two comments), replacing the offer and agreement of the plan of 17/10: a person keeps their own contacts first, and shares one stored piece at a time with one press. Nothing is asked of the other person, and nothing has to be shared back.
    - **The pieces.** Four kinds, each optional: a phone number, WhatsApp (a number), Telegram and Instagram (a username). Each is checked by its kind and kept in one form by `normaliseContact` (`packages/schema/src/contact.ts`), which the app and the API both run: a phone number and WhatsApp in E.164, with spaces, hyphens, dots and brackets dropped, a Finnish number from 0 made +358 and an international one from 00 made +; Telegram without its @, 5 to 32 letters, digits and underscores starting with a letter; Instagram without its @ and in lower case, 1 to 30 letters, digits, dots and underscores, never a dot first, last or twice. At most 64 characters are typed (`CONTACT_INPUT_MAX`). Nobody sees a stored piece until its owner shares it in a conversation.
    - **The tables** (migration 0058, generated). `contact(id, account_id, kind, value, updated_at)`: one row per account and kind (a unique index), the value 1 to 64 characters (a check), the account referenced without a cascade, since erasure keeps the tombstone (ADR-007 §2) and deletes the rows itself. `contact_share(id, conversation_id, side, kind, shared_at, ended_at, ended_how)`: one standing share per conversation, side and kind (a unique index where `ended_at` is null; an ended share may be shared again as a new row), `ended_how` of `contact_share_end` (`stopped`, `removed`, `closed`) set together with `ended_at` (a check), and the conversation's deletion takes its shares (cascade). The enums are `contact_kind` and `contact_share_end`. A share holds no value: the conversation shows the stored piece while the share stands, so a stop, a changed piece and a removed one act on the next read with nothing to copy or null, and what stays after the end is the record: which side of which conversation, which kind, when it began, when and how it ended.
    - **Delivery on commit** (migration 0059, `generate --custom`, rule 10). Two triggers on §9's channel, each sending the conversation's id and never a contact:

      ```sql
      CREATE FUNCTION chat_notify_contact_share() RETURNS trigger LANGUAGE plpgsql AS $$
      BEGIN
        PERFORM pg_notify('kuutti_chat', json_build_object('t', 'contact', 'c', NEW.conversation_id)::text);
        RETURN NULL;
      END
      $$;
      CREATE TRIGGER contact_share_notify AFTER INSERT OR UPDATE ON contact_share
        FOR EACH ROW EXECUTE FUNCTION chat_notify_contact_share();

      CREATE FUNCTION chat_notify_contact() RETURNS trigger LANGUAGE plpgsql AS $$
      BEGIN
        PERFORM pg_notify('kuutti_chat', json_build_object('t', 'contact', 'c', s.conversation_id)::text)
        FROM contact_share s
        JOIN conversation_participant p ON p.conversation_id = s.conversation_id AND p.side = s.side
        WHERE p.account_id = NEW.account_id AND s.kind = NEW.kind AND s.ended_at IS NULL;
        RETURN NULL;
      END
      $$;
      CREATE TRIGGER contact_notify AFTER UPDATE ON contact
        FOR EACH ROW WHEN (OLD.value IS DISTINCT FROM NEW.value)
        EXECUTE FUNCTION chat_notify_contact();
      ```

      The first announces a share that begins or ends, whichever path wrote it: the routes, a removal, a close. The second announces a changed piece in every conversation where it stands. A new piece stands nowhere and announces nothing; a removed one ends its shares in the same transaction, which the first announces.
    - **The routes**, behind the session guard, every statement naming the caller's account and anything about a conversation read through the caller's participant row (rule 6). `GET /contacts`: the caller's pieces in the kinds' order. `PUT /contacts/{kind}` with `{ value }`: stored in its one form, `updated_at` moved only when the value changes (the same value kept again leaves it, so it never withholds a share under a ban, below); 400 `contact_invalid` when it is not one of its kind, the detail naming the kind alone; an account erased meanwhile writes nothing (404). `DELETE /contacts/{kind}`: 204, also when there is none; in one transaction the caller's account is held first, as the keep and the share hold it, then the piece goes and its standing shares end as `removed`. The hold puts a removal and an erasure in one order: the erasure holds the account for update, ends the shares in its close and then deletes the pieces, so a removal that took the piece first while the erasure took the shares first would each wait for the other; held first, one of the two waits for the other to finish. An account erased or sanctioned meanwhile writes nothing (404), as with the keep. `GET /conversations/{id}/contacts`: the detail's `contacts` alone (below), 404 as the detail answers it. `POST /conversations/{id}/contacts/{kind}` shares and `DELETE /conversations/{id}/contacts/{kind}` stops sharing (`stopped`), each answering 200 with the caller's fresh `ConversationDetail`. The share runs §3's order: the caller's account held in a live state, the conversation's row locked through the caller's participant row, what it decides on read afresh. It answers 404 `not_found` for a conversation not the caller's or hidden from its blocker, and for a caller whose account a sanction or an erasure took out of the live states meanwhile; 409 `conversation_closed` when it is closed, the counterpart is erased or a block stands; 409 `contact_not_stored` when the caller keeps no piece of that kind (removed meanwhile on another phone). A share holds the stored piece until it commits, so a removal under way either comes first and refuses it, or waits and ends what it began. A share already standing answers 200 and writes nothing; a share in an archived conversation brings it back from the archive, as a message does (TD-13), and the night counts the newest share as it counts the last message, so a share keeps a conversation out of the archive as a message does (§8). Stopping is not refused in a closed conversation, where nothing stands, and answers 200 unchanged when nothing stands. The share, the stop, the keep and the removal count against `messages_per_minute`, one bucket with the messages (ADR-026), one press each as a message is: each of them can reach the other person's phone as a frame, so a script can neither flicker a share on and off nor rewrite a shared piece faster than it could write; `GET /contacts` and the conversation's contacts read ride the person's ordinary limit.
    - **What each side reads.** The detail (`GET /conversations/{id}`, never the list) carries `contacts`. `mine` is the caller's stored pieces, each with whether it is shared here now, so the share dialog needs no other read. `theirs` is the counterpart's stored pieces of their standing shares, and only while the conversation is open, the counterpart is linked, no block stands between the two, and the sharer is under no shadow ban that was open when the share began or when the piece was last changed: as §4 withholds a message, so that the counterpart cannot tell a withheld share from none, and the share appears at the lift (Open 5). A changed piece shows its new value wherever it stands, unless it was changed while its owner's shadow ban was open: the new value is the owner's act as a message is, so the share is withheld as one begun then, and shows again at the lift; a removed one shows nothing. In a conversation that is closed, or closed in the reads by a block or the counterpart's erasure, `mine` answers with nothing shared and `theirs` is empty; one hidden from its blocker is a 404, as from every route. `GET /conversations/{id}/contacts` answers that `contacts` alone, by the same reads through the caller's own participant row, and signs nothing and writes nothing: no thumb, no fetch-log row and nothing of the day's thumb budget (§4, ADR-008), so the read a frame asks for costs its reader nothing however often the other person acts. The app shows the counterpart's pieces under the header as selectable text, never a link, and its share dialog says that the other person sees a piece at once and that what they already wrote down elsewhere stays with them.
    - **A close and an erasure.** `closeConversationsOf` ends every standing share of the conversations it closes as `closed`, in the close's transaction and before an erased person's link is cut, by a statement after the close's own, so that a share which held the conversation's lock while the close waited for it is ended too. An erasure and a ban end them so now, and #351's block and unmatch will. Erasure (`eraseAccount`, ADR-007 §4) deletes the person's `contact` rows; their shares, ended by the close, stay as the record with no value in it, under a side whose participant row no longer names an account, as their messages do (§5), and go with the conversation when both people are erased.
    - **The export.** `AccountExport.contacts` carries the person's stored pieces with their values; each `ExportedConversation.shares` carries the person's own shares there, oldest first, as the kind, when it began, when and how it ended, never a value. Never the counterpart's pieces or shares.
    - **The frame.** The hub turns a `contact` notification into `{ type: "contacts", conversationId }` for each of the two people with a socket, read through their own participant row as §9's frames are, and only while the conversation is open between them and the other person is under no shadow ban still open (`contactsFrameReaches`): a close and a conversation hidden from its blocker have their own frames, a block is never a signal, and a frame with nothing new to read would tell the counterpart of a ban (ADR-024 §6), so a piece the shadow-banned person stops sharing meanwhile goes at the counterpart's next read. The frame carries no value: the phone answers it with `GET /conversations/{id}/contacts`, one read in flight and one trailing, so a burst of frames costs at most two reads and the last one sees the newest state, and reads the contacts so again when the socket resumes; the detail is read again when the conversation comes into focus and when the app returns to the foreground.
    - **Events.** `contact_shared { sinceOpened, messages }` for the sharer when a share begins, never on a repeat, with §7's bands; `contact_unshared {}` for the owner, one for each share they stop or a removal of the piece ends, and not at a close, which `conversation_closed` covers. Never the kind or the value (rule 5).
    - **Logs.** A contact's value is in no log line, error detail, validation message, research event or frame. The logger redacts a field named `value`, top level and one level down, beside §7's `body`; a refusal names the kind; the PII test keeps a phone number and a username, has them refused by their kind's rule and by the contract, shares them, reads them on both sides, stops and removes them, and interrupts a keep by a fault, and finds no value in a log line or the error report.

## Alternatives considered

- **A, a Matrix homeserver.** Not chosen: a second surface or a proxy of the client API, a second store of the text with its own retention and logs, no official React Native client, and every unknown (memory at five thousand people, what a deactivated user leaves behind, RocksDB backups) on its side, for a transport §3 gives in a few hundred lines.
- **B, a hosted service.** Not chosen: the text leaves the box to a paid processor, against CLAUDE.md's rule and the privacy policy's "Nobody else", and every rule is re-implemented against their API anyway.
- **Socket.IO over `ws`.** Resume and idempotency are the application's either way (§1); it adds a client dependency on the phone and rooms we do not need.
- **`@hono/node-ws`** (#349). Deprecated by its maintainers for `@hono/node-server` 2's own upgrade, which the API already runs; its peer range excludes that version.
- **Fan-out by a call from each writer, in process** (#349). Every slice that changes a conversation, a read mark or a session would call the hub after its commit: the send, the read, the archive, erasure, the ban, logout, the break, the block, and each later piece. A path that forgot would leave a phone stale. The triggers make the commit itself the signal.
- **The token in the socket's URL or a header** (#349). A URL lands in access logs and proxies; a browser cannot set a header on a WebSocket. The first frame carries it.
- **Server-sent events.** Presence and receipts need a connection that goes both ways.
- **A React Native chat UI kit.** The accessibility rules, the three languages and the theme's tokens are ours to verify in a third party's components, and a list and an input are what the screen is.
- **Ethora, XMPP, Tinode, Rocket.Chat.** A's costs in another dialect; Ethora's server licence is outside `scripts/license-policy.json`.
- **The blocker found by a live block test alone, no `closed_by`.** It needs both accounts linked, so after the other's erasure the conversation would come back to the blocker (§2).
- **Deleting a conversation's messages on a close or after a time.** TD-1 and #351's window keep them; a time limit after a close is a later choice (Open, 4).
- **The counterpart's whole thread in the export.** The other person's words are theirs; Tinder's download gives one's own messages only, Meta's the thread (Open, 3).
- **An offer and an agreement per piece** (#103's plan of 17/10, and the maintainer's first decision of 18/10/2026). Replaced the same day by one press (§11): the other person is asked nothing and owes nothing back.
- **The value copied into the share and nulled at its end** (the plan of 17/10, `contact_release.value`). A second copy to keep in step with a changed piece and to null at a stop, a removal, a close and an erasure; reading the stored piece makes each of them act at once, and the record never held a value to forget.
- **One free-text handle beside the phone number** (the plan of 17/10). Unchecked, it carries anything, a link included; the maintainer chose four kinds, each checked by its rule.
- **A contact written in a message.** A message stays for the counterpart after its sender stops sharing and after the sender's erasure (§5, TD-7), so it could never be taken back; `contactDetailsIn` keeps refusing one (§3), and the refusal points to the share.
- **The value in the `contacts` frame.** §9's notifications and frames carry ids; the phone reads the conversation's contacts, by the detail's own reads, where every rule of who sees what is applied once.
- **The whole detail read again on every `contacts` frame** (#103's first build). Each read of the detail signs the counterpart's thumb and writes a fetch-log row under the reader's day's thumb budget (§4, ADR-008), so the other person's presses would spend it; `GET /conversations/{id}/contacts` reads the same rule and signs nothing.

## Open, the maintainer's

Each is one row, one constant or one line to change.

1. `message_max_length` 2000 and `messages_per_minute` 30 (§6).
2. The list's preview: the first 80 characters of the last message, or the kind alone (§4, §6).
3. The export carries one's own words and the counterpart's as a time and a length; the alternative is the thread (§5).
4. Retention: kept while one of the two has an account, gone with the second erasure; the alternative is a time limit after a close (§5).
5. A shadow-banned sender's messages appear after a lift; the alternative keeps them withheld for good (§4). Two tells remain, accepted until #105: a conversation the sender writes in leaves the archive for the counterpart too, which shows nothing new; and a withheld message keeps its place in the sequence, so the counterpart's pages skip a number that only a shadow ban skips (the app does not show the numbers, and asks for earlier messages only after a full page). Giving a withheld message no place until the lift closes the second.
6. What the counterpart of a paused person sees: nothing special, as ADR-022 §4 leaves it; the paused person reads on return (§4).
7. A ban reads as an ended conversation to the counterpart, like a block; the alternative says that the moderators removed the person (§4).
8. Whether a Rust homeserver is worth a second costing with measured numbers once the chat exists (§1).
9. The research events' names and bands, the research partner's (§7).
10. The person blocked reads the counterpart of the ended conversation by name and age, and as "Deleted user" once the blocker erases: one bit ADR-030 §6 promises they never learn. It arises only with a conversation, a block and the blocker's erasure, so not in production before #100; #351, which designs the closed views ("This conversation has ended" as the heading), closes it by showing an ended conversation without the name before and after.

11. The socket's settings (§9): five sockets per account, five seconds for the first frame, a ping every thirty seconds and ten seconds for the pong.
12. A socket lives while its session's access token does, and the app refreshes ahead while the socket is open (§9). The alternative re-authenticates over the open socket, which #349's "a second `auth` closes the socket" rules out today.
13. The security checklist's row for the upgrade's limit cites the September audit's F31, as #349 asks. The repository holds no text of F31, so whether that row answers it is the maintainer's to confirm against the audit.
14. A photo sent in a conversation is checked on its full size, what the recipient sees; #350 named the card, the profile photo's crop of it (§10, ADR-006).
15. What the check is unsure of in a photo sent in a conversation is delivered and asks nobody; the alternative holds it for a person, against #101's "no human queue for a private photo" (§10).
16. `message_photos_per_day` 10 and `message_photo_attempts_per_day` 30, both to midnight in Finland, the tries in the process's memory so a restart forgets them (§10); the newest hundred sent photos in an export; and the length band `0` of `message_sent` for a photo without a caption, the research partner's with the rest of §7.
17. The share, the stop, the keep and the removal of a contact count against `messages_per_minute`, one bucket with the messages; the alternative is a bucket of their own (§11).
18. A share begun under a shadow ban, or one whose piece was changed under it, is withheld from the counterpart while that ban stays open and appears at the lift, as a message does (§4, §11, Open 5); one begun before the ban and left unchanged stays shown, and while the ban is open the counterpart's phone is sent no `contacts` frame, so a piece taken back or changed meanwhile goes at their next read. The notification names the conversation and not the side that acted, so that rule also holds back the frame of the counterpart's own change from their other phones, which follow at their next read (focus, foreground, resume); a side in the notification would lift it, a new migration, if a second phone's lag ever matters. A share also brings an archived conversation back for the counterpart, and keeps it out of the night's archive, as Open 5's first tell does.
19. `contact_shared` and `contact_unshared`, their names and bands, the research partner's with the rest of §7 (§11).
