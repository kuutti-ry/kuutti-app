# ADR-007: Account erasure and data export in M3

- Status: accepted, amended 2026-10-12 to 2026-10-18 (what erasure deletes and the export carries, as later changes add rows, #51, #171, #253, #296, #307, #84, #348, #350, #103; 2026-10-13: the export is a zip with the photos, the alternative once rejected, #237; see History)
- Date: 2026-09-26
- Follows: TD-7 (erasure and re-registration), TD-1 (identity apart from account), `.claude/rules/db.md` (the erasure table), issue #34 (the identity half), issue #51

## Context

The erasure table of TD-7 says what deletion removes (profile, photos, preferences, likes, matches, bookmarks, push tokens, sessions, the `research_id` mapping) and what it keeps (the identity row, the counterpart's message copies, report snapshots for twelve months, audit log entries for five years). #34 wrote the identity half: one more deletion on the identity, a thirty-day cooldown, the account row anonymised. Most of the table's rows do not exist yet in M3; what does is sessions, login attempts, photos with their review rows, the fetch log and the account's age. The right of access needs an export of the same data. This ADR fixes how those two work now, so the slices that follow join a shape rather than invent one.

## Decision

1. **Erasure is one transaction for the rows, then the objects.** `POST /account/delete` with `{ "confirm": true }` (the app asks first; a stray call without the body does nothing). In one transaction: every session and login attempt of the account is deleted; every photo row (and, by cascade, its review row) and every fetch log entry of the account is deleted; the account row is kept as a tombstone with `state = 'deleted'`, `deleted_at`, and `birth_year` and `birth_month` set to null (the two columns became nullable for this); the identity row gets `deletion_count + 1` and `reregister_after` thirty days out. After the commit the photo objects whose content key no remaining row references are deleted from the bucket, best effort and logged: an object store has no rollback, so rows first and objects second, never the other way round. The audit log, `moderator_roles` and `admin_session` are not touched: they belong to staff identities and to history, not to the account.
2. **Why a tombstone and not a deleted row.** The identity keeps counting and cooling down without it, but the account id is what the fetch log of other people (M4), the audit log and the report snapshots will name; a row that exists with nothing personal on it keeps those references meaningful and lets "one live account per identity" stay a database constraint. What is personal on the row, the age, goes.
3. **Export is one JSON document.** `GET /account/export` answers with the account (id, state, dates, age), the identity's dates and the bank's identification level (never `hetu_hmac`: it identifies nothing without the key and nothing to the person), the devices signed in, the photos with fifteen-minute URLs for their variants and their moderation outcome (decision, reason, whether a person decided, when; never the label names, which describe the machine's guess and not the person), and the person's own fetch log, newest first, at most a thousand rows. The URLs are issued through the same path as any fetch, so the export writes to the fetch log it returns. Nothing about anyone else is in it, and a test greps for another account's id.
4. **The rest of the table joins here.** Profile fields (#46, #47), preferences, likes, matches, bookmarks, push tokens and the `research_id` mapping (#50) each add a statement to `eraseAccount` and a section to the export in the change that creates the table; the counterpart's message copies and report snapshots (M4) are the kept side. `eraseAccount` in `apps/api/src/identity/erasure.ts` is the one place. The optional e-mail of #148 (TD-18) is a column on the account: set and cleared by the person (`PUT`, `DELETE /account/email`), nulled by the tombstone with the age, carried in the export as the person's own data, and never in a log line or an event.
5. **The app.** Until the profile screens exist, the home screen's account card carries "Download my data" (the export handed to the system share sheet on the phone, a download on the web preview) and "Delete my account" behind a confirmation that names the cooldown. Deletion signs the device out locally at once; every token of the account is already gone on the server.

## Consequences

- A person who deletes and comes back after thirty days gets a fresh account with nothing restored (TD-7); the tombstone row and the identity's counter are what makes the refusal inside the cooldown possible without keeping anything else.
- The export can be large in URLs but small in bytes; a client caches nothing from it (`Cache-Control: no-store`).
- Migration 0008 relaxes two NOT NULL constraints; every live account still has both values, written by the callback.
- Deleting the objects after the commit means a crash between the two leaves unreferenced objects in the bucket. M4's report and sanction work brings the orphan sweep that covers this and the upload-crash case of ADR-005.
- Which objects are orphans is decided after the commit, against committed rows, so an upload of the same content that committed during the erasure keeps its objects. A window remains between that check and the delete; the upload's failure path and `deletePhoto` have had the same one since #57. Closing it takes a per-content-key ownership protocol (an advisory lock around put + insert and around check + delete, or the row before the objects) in the media slice as a whole, to be settled with the M4 orphan sweep.

## Alternatives considered

**Deleting the account row.** Rejected as above: references from history and from other people's logs would dangle, and the constraint on one live account per identity would lose its anchor.

**Anonymising by overwriting with placeholders.** Rejected: null says "gone" without inventing a value; the columns were made nullable instead.

**A zipped export with the image bytes.** Rejected at first: the fifteen-minute URLs give the same bytes without the API assembling archives on one box; the client can fetch them. Reversed on 2026-10-13 (#237, the history below): the links died before the file was read.

## History

- 2026-10-12: the photo check's notes (#253, ADR-006 §2) join both halves. `eraseAccount` deletes them in its transaction (the tombstone keeps the account row, so no cascade would). The export carries them as `photoCheck`: what the check is, each photo it did not show with its date and its kind, in words, and until when new photos are checked more carefully, all in the request's language. They are the export's first texts in a language: a person reading their file should read what a note means, gently, not guess at a bare `hate`. The stored kind is there too, for a machine reading the file.
- 2026-10-13 (#51, the verification of 08/10): the export carries what it had left out and the API holds: `account.stateChangedAt`; the identity's `standing`, `standingChangedAt`, `refusedAttempts` and `reregisterAfter`; each device's `userAgent`, bounded as at login; and `cardsShown`, the cards served to the person (ADR-008) as photo id and last day, newest first, at most a thousand, the same trail erasure deletes. The consent rows join the erased side (ADR-010 §14). Still out, on purpose: `hetu_hmac` and the label names (§3). Login attempts (`auth_request`) were never evidence of anything: the state, nonce and code hash of a login in flight, ten minutes long, swept nightly; what a refused login leaves is `refused_attempts` on the identity, which stays, and the structured log line, which the log group keeps thirty days. The erasure of `card_shown` viewer rows and the survival of `moderator_roles` and `admin_session` have their scenarios now.
- 2026-10-13 (#170, ADR-022): a break is not an erasure. `paused` keeps every row the table names and the export shows the account as it is; the erasure table is unchanged and `eraseAccount` is still the one delete.
- 2026-10-13 (#237): the export is a zip, and the alternative rejected above is the decision now. `GET /account/export` answers `application/zip`, named `<stem>-<date>.zip` in the request's language (`account.export.fileName`, ASCII, the Finnish calendar day, nothing about the person): the document as `<stem>-<date>.json`, and `photos/<photo id>.webp`, the `full` variant of every photo whose object the store holds, rejected and queued ones too, as stored and not re-encoded; two rows that share one object get two files. In the document each photo's fifteen-minute URLs give way to `file`, the path inside the zip, null when the object is gone or there is no store; `exportedBy` names the API's version, the contract the file follows; `exports` lists the person's requests for a copy, this one included. What changed the mind: a copy whose links die in fifteen minutes is a weak copy (Article 15(3)), and a store review may ask for one file. The archive is built in memory on the one box: six photos at most, a few megabytes. Its reads bypass URL issuance, so each is written to the fetch log as a fetch of `full` by `logPhotoAccess`, counted against later fetches like any other and never refused; what bounds the exposure instead is the export itself: `matching_config exports_per_day` (3) per Finnish calendar day, counted from the new `account_export` table in the statement that writes the row (`recordExport`), the fourth answered 429 `export_budget_exceeded` with `Retry-After` until midnight. The rows are the person's trail: the export lists them and `eraseAccount` deletes them (migrations 0039 and 0040). The JSON-only route is gone; the tests open the zip. The app downloads the zip into its cache and hands the file over (the share sheet on iOS, the directory picker on Android, a download on the web preview), then deletes the cached copy.
- 2026-10-15 (#51): the research events leave the export (ADR-011 §10, the maintainer's call); the `research` section keeps the enrolment, since when and under which wording.
- 2026-10-15 (#171, ADR-023): the feedback a person sent from the app joins both halves: erased with the account in the same transaction, listed in the export with its kind, the build and whether a moderator read it.
- 2026-10-15 (#296): the export's photos carry `caption`, the key of the owner's line, as `Photo` does; the words are the app's, per language, and the key is not the person's text. It goes with the photo at erasure, as every column of the row does.
- 2026-10-15 (#296, ADR-006 §3): the export's photos carry the automatic description too, as `Photo` does (`description`, keys of a closed list) and in words in the request's language (`descriptionText`, `describePhoto` of `packages/i18n`): what the person's photo is heard with, readable in the file without the catalogue. Still never the labels behind it. It lives on the review row, so it goes with the photo and with the account.
- 2026-10-16 (#307, ADR-024 §8): the sanctions join both halves: erasure deletes the account's sanctions except the open bans and suspensions, which carry the identity's standing and stay on it; the export lists them with kind, reason, dates and whether a second member of staff looked at it, in words in the request's language too, never who and never staff's note, and not an open shadow ban. The export's `account.state` reads a shadow ban as `active`. `ErasureSummary` counts the sanctions that went.
- 2026-10-17 (#84, ADR-030 §8): reports and blocks join the kept side and the export. Erasure keeps both, whoever is erased: a report, its note with it, for twelve months from filing (TD-7's report snapshots; a nightly prune), a block on the two identities for as long as they are kept, so that it holds for a later account of either. The export lists the reports the person filed (date, reason, their own note, the outcome and its date, in words in the request's language too) and the blocks they set, by date only; never the other person's id, never a report about the person or a block against them. Both are read by the identity, past accounts included, since both are kept on it: the one place the export reaches past the account. One exception to §3's "nothing about anyone else": a filed report's outcome says whether a moderator acted on it, never which action, as the issue asks and the DSA's Art. 16(5) asks of a host if Kuutti is in its scope (#188); ADR-030 Open 9 and 18 leave both to the maintainer. `ErasureSummary` is unchanged: nothing of either goes.
- 2026-10-17 (#348, ADR-031 §5): the conversations join both halves. Erasure cuts the person's link to each conversation (`account_id` null) and closes their open and archived ones as `erasure`; the messages stay for the counterpart, under "Deleted user" (TD-7's kept side), except the ones the counterpart never received (sent under a shadow ban still open), which go before the sanctions do; a conversation whose other person was erased before goes whole. The export lists each conversation with its opening and state, the person's own messages with their words, and the counterpart's as a time and a length, never whom.
- 2026-10-18 (#350, ADR-031 §10): a person's photos sent in conversations go with them, as their profile photos do, and the message stays for the counterpart, reading "Photo removed" (`message.photo_id` set null in the same transaction). `erasePhotosOfAccount` takes the photos to keep, for #351's frozen copy of a report; nothing names one yet. The export carries every line's kind, and each of the person's own lines with a photo carries an object: the file in the zip, null when the zip does not carry it (the object is gone, or the photo is not among the newest hundred sent: the zip is built in memory, a `shortcut:` lifted by streaming it); the check's outcome, shown as a profile photo's review is, where `queued` means that the check was unsure and the photo was delivered with no human queue (ADR-031 §10); and its automatic description, in keys and in words in the request's language. Each sent photo the zip carries is written to the fetch log with the purpose `message`, never counted (ADR-008). The counterpart's lines stay a kind, a time and a length; the profile photos are as before.
- 2026-10-18 (#103, ADR-031 §11): the contacts a person keeps to share in a conversation join both halves. Erasure deletes the stored pieces (`contact`) in its transaction, and `ErasureSummary` counts them (`contacts`). The person's shares (`contact_share`) are ended by the close of their conversations, as `closed`, and stay as the record of each: which side of which conversation, which kind, when it began, when and how it ended, with no value in it, since a share reads the stored piece and never held a copy; they go with the conversation once both people are erased. The export carries the stored pieces with their values (`contacts`) and, in each conversation, the person's own shares, oldest first, never a value (`shares`); never the counterpart's pieces or shares.
- 2026-10-18 (#176, ADR-032 §5): the notification choices (`notification_preference`) and the notices in the app (`notice`) join both halves. Erasure deletes both in its transaction, the notices before the sanctions they point at, and `ErasureSummary` counts them (`notificationChoices`, `notices`). The export carries every kind with its state and every notice with when it was read (`notifications`).
