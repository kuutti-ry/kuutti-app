# ADR-010: Onboarding, versioned consents, and the activation rule

- Status: accepted; amended by §10 on 2026-10-08 (#146: the steps of the field sheet, one country-wide pond, the special-category consent as a kind of its own) and by §11 on 2026-10-09 (the one pond is the capital region)
- Date: 2026-09-26
- Follows: TD-5 (research use of events needs its own yes), TD-7 (the erasure table; what deletion keeps), TD-10 and TD-13 (the pond), TD-11 and CLAUDE.md rule 7 (seeks and the age window are hard filters), TD-17 (the Finnish text is binding), CLAUDE.md rules 3, 5 and 6, `.claude/rules/db.md` (`preferences` shape, erasure), `.claude/rules/i18n.md` (legal texts are versioned and never machine-translated), `.claude/rules/schema.md`, ADR-007 §4, ADR-009 §2 and §4; issue #46

## Context

After the bank login an account exists with a year and month of birth and nothing else. Matching needs four answers the bank cannot give, nothing may run before two consents, the research use of events needs a separate yes, and GDPR art. 7 asks the controller to show what was agreed, when, and to which wording. #46 had one line of body; this ADR fixes how those rows look, what a consent is bound to, what survives erasure, and when an account is active.

## Decision

1. **Gender is self-declared and one of a closed list.** `account.gender` (`woman`, `man`, `non_binary`), nullable until answered; nothing from the bank informs it (rule 3). The list mirrors `GENDERS` in `packages/schema`, and a test keeps the two equal.
2. **Seeks and the age window are the two hard rows.** `preferences(account_id, field, value, mode, include_unknown)` as rules/db.md describes it, one row per field, `mode = hard`: `seeks` is a non-empty set of the gender list, `age_window` is `{ min, max }` with `18 <= min <= max <= 99`. Both are written together and read as one (`readPreferences`), so the round builder of M4 joins both parties' hard rows without a special case; the profile's completeness (#47) reads the same function. The value of `seeks` never appears in a log line or an event (rule 5, checklist line 66).
3. **The pond is a choice from `ponds`**, stored as `account.pond_id`; the list comes from the API with both case forms (TD-17), and no client inflects a name.
4. **A consent is a row bound to a version, the version of the text the person read.** The app posts the `consent_version` built into it (`CONSENT_VERSIONS` from the i18n package: the text it rendered), not the API's current one; when the two differ, the app asks for an update instead of recording anything. The row's language is one the wording exists in: a phone set to a language without a legal text read the English fallback, and the row says `en`. A golden test in the i18n package ties a hash of the binding Finnish wording to its version, so a text edit without a version bump fails the build. The research opt-in is capped at ten changes a day, since every change is a row kept for good; `GET /consents` lists the newest hundred rows, while the status reads the newest active row per kind and the export carries every row, so a long history hides nothing (#65 review). `consent(account_id, kind, version, locale_shown, given_at, withdrawn_at)`, kinds `terms | privacy | research`. The binding texts live under `legal.<kind>.*` in `messages.yaml`, each with a `consent_version`; the i18n build emits `CONSENT_VERSIONS` with one version per kind and fails when a kind carries two. The API accepts a consent only for the current version (409 `agreement_outdated` otherwise), records the same kind and version once, and reads a consent for an older version as none: a changed wording is a new version and everyone is asked again. Research is the one kind a person withdraws (`withdrawn_at`; a new yes is a new row); terms and privacy end with the account.
5. **Proof of consent is kept.** The consent rows survive erasure with the tombstoned account row (#51), never rewritten, retained like the audit log (five years, TD-7's kept side), so the association can show what was agreed, when, and to which wording (GDPR art. 7(1)). They appear in the export while the account lives. TD-7's erasure table does not name them; this is the reading, for the maintainer to confirm.
6. **Activation is a rule, not a flag, and `state` is not the gate.** `active` never goes back to `registered`; an account whose terms or privacy consent is for an old version is `active` with `complete: false` and a missing step, and it is `complete`, never `state`, that a later rule (the round builder of M4) must read. `registered` becomes `active` when gender, both hard rows, the pond, and terms and privacy for the current versions are present; the research opt-in is never required. The rule runs on every read of `GET /onboarding` (`missingSteps`, pure), so no route has to remember to flip a state and a wording change that invalidates a consent shows up as a missing step, not as a stuck account. `active` never goes back to `registered`.
7. **The texts shipped are drafts, marked so.** The terms, the privacy summary and the research explanation carry `consent_version: 2026-09-draft-1`, English prefixed DRAFT and Finnish prefixed LUONNOS, no Swedish (a warning, not an error: a binding text is never machine-translated, and nobody has written it), for the maintainer and the association's counsel to replace before launch. The full pages are #53's; the summaries are what the person accepts.
8. **Erasure and export.** The preference rows go with the account, the consent rows stay, the tombstone keeps neither gender nor pond; the export lists gender, pond, both preferences and every consent row, withdrawn ones included. Every writer of these rows, the withdrawal included, takes the account row under the same lock erasure takes (ADR-009 §8), so no row lands or changes behind the deletes.
9. **The app asks one thing per screen**, every answer a button: the two consents first, on one screen showing the exact version and, in every language but Finnish, that the Finnish text is the one that counts, so nothing personal is asked before the person has read what happens to it (gender and seeks together reveal orientation, ADR-009 §2); then gender, seeks, the age window (saved with seeks), the pond, the research opt-in on its own, then home, where photos (#48) and the profile (#47) wait. The home screen sends a `registered` or incomplete account back to onboarding and shows nothing that needs a finished account until the status has been read and says complete: a status it cannot read keeps the gate closed, with a retry, never open. The research opt-in stays reachable afterwards as a switch on the account card, so "at any time" is true in the app. The API still accepts the answers in any order: an API-side gate on consent for `PUT /preferences` would need the matching slice to read consents from identity, and the boundary is not worth crossing for a client that already asks in the right order. The home screen sends a `registered` or incomplete account back to onboarding. The API does not care about order; each step is idempotent.

10. **Amendment of 2026-10-08 (#146, the field sheet, ADR-019).** The flow is the sheet's: the welcome screen carries the norms and both consents on one button; then the name (typed, never prefilled from the bank), the gender and, after non-binary, an identity label; whom one seeks, with the special-category consent on the same screen; the intent; the age window as two steppers around the person's own age (`age` in the status); three photos; two prompts or a bio; then the research opt-in as before. `GET /onboarding` lists the open steps in that order (`name`, `intent`, `photos`, `prompts_or_bio` join the enum) and `complete` means none is open, while `state` still turns `active` on the six of §6 alone: a person with a name, three photos pending with the moderator and no bio is active and incomplete, and the round builder reads completeness, never `state`. The seek answer counts only with an active `special_category` consent for the current wording (`legal.special_category.*`, one wording for seek, politics and religion, ADR-019 §4): a fourth consent kind, recorded like the others, withdrawn through `DELETE /consents/{kind}` like research, and its withdrawal takes the seek row and the profile's politics and religion with it, so onboarding asks the seek question again. §9's choice stands: the API does not gate `PUT /preferences` on the consent, the status lists the step. One pond for now: `matching_config.default_pond` names it (`suomi`, the seed's only pond), the status puts an account without a pond in it on the first read, and no pond step exists in the app; `PUT /account/pond` and the `pond` step stay for the day several ponds are live, with the sheet's postal-code ponds deferred (and a note that the counter and the gate work over one pond unchanged, ADR-013, ADR-015). The seed's three test ponds leave; tests make their own.

11. **Amendment of 2026-10-09 (the one pond is the capital region).** `default_pond` version 2 names `paakaupunkiseutu` (migration 0025, `MATCHING_CONFIG_V2` in the seed), and the seed's one pond is Pääkaupunkiseutu, locative `Pääkaupunkiseudulla`. The country-wide pond of §10 was chosen for size alone, and its row existed in seeded databases only: staging, seeded by hand before §10, has carried the capital region since its first ponds and never had `suomi`, so version 2 makes the deployed default name a pond that exists. Nothing else changes: the counter, the gate, the stories and the population work over the one pond as before, and the tree (`parent_id`) still waits for the postal-code ponds. The test ponds `espoo` and `otaniemi` that a database may still hold are empty children of it; the seed neither writes nor removes them.

## Consequences

- Two tables (`preferences`, `consent`), two columns on `account`, migration 0013; one generated module (`consent.ts`) in the i18n package.
- Six routes: `GET /onboarding`, `PUT /account/gender`, `GET /ponds`, `PUT /account/pond`, `GET`/`PUT /preferences`, `GET`/`POST /consents`, `DELETE /consents/research`; the pond and matching slices exist in the API from here.
- #50 creates the `research_id` mapping when a research consent is given and removes it on withdrawal: `giveConsent` and `withdrawResearchConsent` in `identity/onboarding.ts` are the hooks.
- The profile's completeness now waits for real rows; no profile was complete before this change, by design (ADR-009 §4).
- Open, and the maintainer's: the gender list and its texts, the age bounds, the retention of consent rows, the wording and versions of the three legal texts, whether `seeks` takes the special-category flag of ADR-009 §2 (its consent text and version would live under `legal.*` too).
