# ADR-010: Onboarding, versioned consents, and the activation rule

- Status: accepted; amended by §10 on 2026-10-08 (#146: the steps of the field sheet, one country-wide pond, the special-category consent as a kind of its own), by §11 on 2026-10-09 (the one pond is the capital region), by §12 and §13 on 2026-10-10 (#174: a second pond and the names by language; #146: whom one seeks saved on its own), by §14 on 2026-10-13 (#51: the consent rows go with the account), on 2026-10-17 (§4: one version across the app and the website, #208), by §15 on 2026-10-22 (#418: the norms, the terms, the privacy summary and the research question before the bank login; the welcome step as the fallback; onboarding's research step gone; see History) and by §16 on 2026-10-31 (#542: whom one seeks refused without the current consent, and nothing behind a session cached)
- Date: 2026-09-26
- Follows: TD-5 (research use of events needs its own yes), TD-7 (the erasure table; what deletion keeps), TD-10 and TD-13 (the pond), TD-11 and CLAUDE.md rule 7 (seeks and the age window are hard filters), TD-17 (the Finnish text is binding), CLAUDE.md rules 3, 5 and 6, `.claude/rules/db.md` (`preferences` shape, erasure), `.claude/rules/i18n.md` (legal texts are versioned and never machine-translated), `.claude/rules/schema.md`, ADR-007 §4, ADR-009 §2 and §4; issues #46, #418 (§15)

## Context

After the bank login an account exists with a year and month of birth and nothing else. Matching needs four answers the bank cannot give, nothing may run before two consents, the research use of events needs a separate yes, and GDPR art. 7 asks the controller to show what was agreed, when, and to which wording. #46 had one line of body; this ADR fixes how those rows look, what a consent is bound to, what survives erasure, and when an account is active.

## Decision

1. **Gender is self-declared and one of a closed list.** `account.gender` (`woman`, `man`, `non_binary`), nullable until answered; nothing from the bank informs it (rule 3). The list mirrors `GENDERS` in `packages/schema`, and a test keeps the two equal.
2. **Seeks and the age window are the two hard rows.** `preferences(account_id, field, value, mode, include_unknown)` as rules/db.md describes it, one row per field, `mode = hard`: `seeks` is a non-empty set of the gender list, `age_window` is `{ min, max }` with `18 <= min <= max <= 99`. Both are written together and read as one (`readPreferences`), so the round builder of M4 joins both parties' hard rows without a special case; the profile's completeness (#47) reads the same function. The value of `seeks` never appears in a log line or an event (rule 5, checklist line 66).
3. **The pond is a choice from `ponds`**, stored as `account.pond_id`; the list comes from the API with both case forms (TD-17), and no client inflects a name.
4. **A consent is a row bound to a version, the version of the text the person read.** The app posts the `consent_version` built into it (`CONSENT_VERSIONS` from the i18n package: the text it rendered), not the API's current one; when the two differ, the app asks for an update instead of recording anything. The row's language is one the wording exists in: a phone set to a language without a legal text read the English fallback, and the row says `en`. A golden test in the i18n package ties a hash of the Finnish wording to its version, so a text edit without a version bump fails the build. The research opt-in is capped at ten changes a day, since every change is a row kept for good; `GET /consents` lists the newest hundred rows, while the status reads the newest active row per kind and the export carries every row, so a long history hides nothing (#65 review). `consent(account_id, kind, version, locale_shown, given_at, withdrawn_at)`, kinds `terms | privacy | research`. The texts a person accepts in the app live under `legal.<kind>.*` in `messages.yaml`, each with a `consent_version` (amended 2026-10-17, #208: these are the summaries, and the site's full Finnish text binds, as the next paragraph says); the i18n build emits `CONSENT_VERSIONS` with one version per kind and fails when a kind carries two. The API accepts a consent only for the current version (409 `agreement_outdated` otherwise), records the same kind and version once, and reads a consent for an older version as none: a changed wording is a new version and everyone is asked again. Research is the one kind a person withdraws (`withdrawn_at`; a new yes is a new row); terms and privacy end with the account.

   **One version across the app and the website** (2026-10-17, #208). Each kind has one version and two renderings: the summary under `legal.<kind>.*`, which is what the person accepts on the screen, and the full text on the website (`kuutti-ry/kuutti-app-site`), whose Finnish binds; the research opt-in and the sensitive answers are read on its privacy page, each under its version. The version stays a person's bump, never a hash: every bump asks everybody again, so a typo fix is a new version too, or no change. Each repository pins its own text to the version by a fingerprint: the app by the golden test above, the site's build by the SHA-256 of the Finnish heading and body, with a legal text's status and binding (of a draft's one language while its Finnish is not written), refusing a text that moved without its version; the site's CI also refuses a pinned row changed, removed or moved against its `main`, and wants every pinned version's anchor on its page, since production records consents to a version until its next release and the stores' builds link to it. The research opt-in and the sensitive answers have no longer text than this app's: the site carries their `legal.<kind>.title` and summary as they are, so their two copies are held to each other as well as to the version. The two agree through one file the site's build publishes, `https://kuutti.app/legal/versions.json` (per kind: the version, the fingerprint, the language fingerprinted, the address at the anchor of the version, the day). `pnpm check:legal-versions`, in CI's `i18n` job, fails when a kind's version in `CONSENT_VERSIONS` is not the site's, when either side lacks a kind, when the app's link to the text (`legalPageUrl`) is not the address the site gives, when the published fingerprint of the research or sensitive-answers text is not the site's fingerprint of this app's Finnish title and summary, and when the file cannot be read after three tries on a network failure or a 5xx. It fails closed on every pull request, not only on one that touches `legal.*`: a site that cannot be read for a minute is retried, and one that cannot be read at all is a reason to look, not to pass. The site's CI reads this repository's `packages/i18n/src/generated/consent.ts` on `main` from GitHub and fails on a version the site never published; `src/consent.test.ts` reads that module the way the site does, so a change of the generator's shape fails here before it fails there. The site leads, since its text binds: a new version is published there first, and once it is deployed every pull request here fails until the one that carries the same version is merged, so the two pull requests are opened together and this one is merged straight after the deploy. Until then `main` goes red too if anything else merges, and a pull request that went green before the deploy runs its checks again before it merges. The first time, the site's pull request is merged and deployed before this repository's, whose check would otherwise find no file. Every summary in the app shows its version as a link to the full text at that anchor (`LegalTextLink`): a link out to the public site, no web surface of the product (rule 8). No build pulls the other repository's text, and the database is unchanged: the consent row names the version string, which is what article 7(1) asks the association to show.
5. **Proof of consent is kept while the account lives** (amended by §14: the rows go at erasure). The consent rows are never rewritten and appear in the export, so the association can show what was agreed, when, and to which wording (GDPR art. 7(1)) for as long as it processes what they cover.
6. **Activation is a rule, not a flag, and `state` is not the gate.** `active` never goes back to `registered`; an account whose terms or privacy consent is for an old version is `active` with `complete: false` and a missing step, and it is `complete`, never `state`, that a later rule (the round builder of M4) must read. `registered` becomes `active` when gender, both hard rows, the pond, and terms and privacy for the current versions are present; the research opt-in is never required. The rule runs on every read of `GET /onboarding` (`missingSteps`, pure), so no route has to remember to flip a state and a wording change that invalidates a consent shows up as a missing step, not as a stuck account. `active` never goes back to `registered`.
7. **The texts shipped are drafts, marked so.** The terms, the privacy summary and the research explanation carry `consent_version: 2026-09-draft-1`, English prefixed DRAFT and Finnish prefixed LUONNOS, no Swedish (a warning, not an error: a binding text is never machine-translated, and nobody has written it), for the maintainer and the association's counsel to replace before launch. The full pages are #53's; the summaries are what the person accepts.
8. **Erasure and export.** The preference rows go with the account, the consent rows stay, the tombstone keeps neither gender nor pond; the export lists gender, pond, both preferences and every consent row, withdrawn ones included. Every writer of these rows, the withdrawal included, takes the account row under the same lock erasure takes (ADR-009 §8), so no row lands or changes behind the deletes.
9. **The app asks one thing per screen**, every answer a button: the two consents first, on one screen showing the exact version and, in every language but Finnish, that the Finnish text is the one that counts, so nothing personal is asked before the person has read what happens to it (gender and seeks together reveal orientation, ADR-009 §2); then gender, seeks, the age window (saved with seeks), the pond, the research opt-in on its own, then home, where photos (#48) and the profile (#47) wait. The home screen sends a `registered` or incomplete account back to onboarding and shows nothing that needs a finished account until the status has been read and says complete: a status it cannot read keeps the gate closed, with a retry, never open. The research opt-in stays reachable afterwards as a switch on the account card, so "at any time" is true in the app. The API still accepts the answers in any order: an API-side gate on consent for `PUT /preferences` would need the matching slice to read consents from identity, and the boundary is not worth crossing for a client that already asks in the right order. The home screen sends a `registered` or incomplete account back to onboarding. The API does not care about order; each step is idempotent.

10. **Amendment of 2026-10-08 (#146, the field sheet, ADR-019).** The flow is the sheet's: the welcome screen carries the norms and both consents on one button; then the name (typed, never prefilled from the bank), the gender and, after non-binary, an identity label; whom one seeks, with the special-category consent on the same screen; the intent; the age window as two steppers around the person's own age (`age` in the status); three photos; two prompts or a bio; then the research opt-in as before. `GET /onboarding` lists the open steps in that order (`name`, `intent`, `photos`, `prompts_or_bio` join the enum) and `complete` means none is open, while `state` still turns `active` on the six of §6 alone: a person with a name, three photos pending with the moderator and no bio is active and incomplete, and the round builder reads completeness, never `state`. The seek answer counts only with an active `special_category` consent for the current wording (`legal.special_category.*`, one wording for seek, politics and religion, ADR-019 §4): a fourth consent kind, recorded like the others, withdrawn through `DELETE /consents/{kind}` like research, and its withdrawal takes the seek row and the profile's politics and religion with it, so onboarding asks the seek question again. §9's choice stands: the API does not gate `PUT /preferences` on the consent, the status lists the step. One pond for now: `matching_config.default_pond` names it (`suomi`, the seed's only pond), the status puts an account without a pond in it on the first read, and no pond step exists in the app; `PUT /account/pond` and the `pond` step stay for the day several ponds are live, with the sheet's postal-code ponds deferred (and a note that the counter and the gate work over one pond unchanged, ADR-013, ADR-015). The seed's three test ponds leave; tests make their own.

11. **Amendment of 2026-10-09 (the one pond is the capital region).** `default_pond` version 2 names `paakaupunkiseutu` (migration 0025, `MATCHING_CONFIG_V2` in the seed), and the seed's one pond is Pääkaupunkiseutu, locative `Pääkaupunkiseudulla`. The country-wide pond of §10 was chosen for size alone, and its row existed in seeded databases only: staging, seeded by hand before §10, has carried the capital region since its first ponds and never had `suomi`, so version 2 makes the deployed default name a pond that exists. Nothing else changes: the counter, the gate, the stories and the population work over the one pond as before, and the tree (`parent_id`) still waits for the postal-code ponds. The test ponds `espoo` and `otaniemi` that a database may still hold are empty children of it; the seed neither writes nor removes them.

12. **Amendment of 2026-10-10 (#174, a second pond and the names by language).** The seed's ponds are two siblings without a parent, Pääkaupunkiseutu and Suomi (the rest of the country), the second written by migration 0027 where the seed does not run; `default_pond` still names the capital region. With more than one pond without a parent the choice is the person's: `assignDefaultPond` assigns nothing, the status lists the `pond` step, and the app asks it after the age window with one button per pond; a child pond is no choice yet (the tree still waits for the postal-code ponds). A pond's name in the person's language is a catalogue entry by slug, `pond.name.<slug>` (en, fi, sv), read by the app with the Finnish name of the row as the fallback; the row's nominative and inessive stay as the Finnish source, and no sentence is built around a name (TD-17). The counter and the gate work per pond unchanged (ADR-013, ADR-015); the stories and the population stay in the capital region. A sibling rather than a parent, because nothing reads the tree and two counters and two gates are what the design already does.

13. **Amendment of 2026-10-10 (#146, whom one seeks saved on its own).** The app saves whom one seeks on leaving its screen and the age window on leaving its own; "written together" in §2 and "saved with seeks" in §9 no longer hold. Held in the screen's memory until the ages, the answer was lost when the app closed between the two (on the simulator, 10/10/2026), and the maintainer chose this of the three ways out: the ages straight after whom one seeks would break the sheet's order, and keeping the answer on the device would put an article 9 answer outside the database. `PUT /preferences` takes whom one seeks, the age window or both, each replacing its row and leaving the other; an update naming neither is refused. The cadence of #147 and the gate's decision anew (ADR-015) follow whom one seeks only and run when it is in the update. Nothing is active sooner: the status lists `seeks` and `age_window` apart as before, and activation waits for both. A withdrawal of the sensitive-answers consent (#204) now asks whom one seeks again and nothing else, since the window is no sensitive answer and stays; the age step is open only while no window is stored.

14. **Amendment of 2026-10-13 (#51, the consent rows go with the account).** `eraseAccount` deletes the account's consent rows in its transaction and counts them in its summary. §5 had kept them five years as proof under article 7(1); the maintainer's reading is that after erasure the proof outlives nothing it could be asked about. The terms and the privacy acceptance are the condition of use, a contract (article 6(1)(b)), not a consent; the sensitive answers and the profile the special-category consent covered go in the same transaction; and the research events keep the `consent_version` they were written under (ADR-011 §3), so an event still says which wording its person had read when nothing links it to them any more. Keeping the rows had also kept a handle on the pseudonym, the row's `given_at` beside an opt-in event's hour (ADR-011 §1); that handle goes with them. Settled by this: "the retention of consent rows" of the open list below.

15. **Amendment of 2026-10-22 (#418, the consents before the bank login).** The four norms, the terms, the privacy summary and the research question now come before the bank login, on the maintainer's answers of that day to #418's questions. What a person reads and accepts is the same as in §9 and §10; where it stands changes, and the API does not.
   - **Before the bank.** Until this phone has recorded the current versions, the sign-in screen shows "Before you sign in" where the bank button would be (`BeforeSignIn`, `apps/mobile/src/features/identity/`). First the four norms with the terms and the privacy summary, each with its version as a link to the full text (`LegalTextLink`, §4) and, outside Finnish, the line that the Finnish text is the one that counts. One button accepts both, and the bank button cannot be reached before it. Then the research question: the card with its version, and Yes and Not now, nothing chosen beforehand and neither drawn stronger. Either answer goes on to the bank, and a no costs nothing (GDPR art. 7(4)). A refusal shown on the sign-in screen of a phone that has not answered goes back to these screens, never straight to the bank. The web preview has no bank button, and keeps the welcome step.
   - **Where the answers wait** (#418's (a)). Before the exchange there is no account to bind a row to, so the app holds the answers in memory: the versions it carries (`CONSENT_VERSIONS`, §4), the research version on a yes, and the language the texts were shown in (`agreements.ts`). Straight after a successful exchange, before the status is read, it posts them through `POST /consents` as the welcome step does: the terms, then the privacy summary, then research on a yes. Nothing changes in the API or in the sign-in path. The order stays the client's, as §9 has it. The answers belong to the one bank attempt they are carried into: opening the bank binds them to that attempt's challenge (ADR-027) and starts their ten minutes, the attempt's own; only that attempt's exchange posts them. A refusal, a second attempt, answers not yet carried to the bank when the app leaves the screen, or ten minutes past drop them, and whoever signs in next answers again, so nobody's rows carry answers somebody else gave on the same phone.
   - **A phone that recorded them goes straight to the bank.** Once the terms and the privacy summary are rows, the phone remembers their versions (the preference `agreedVersions`, on the phone only), and the next sign-in on it skips the screens while those are the versions it carries. A new phone, an update that changes a wording, or a phone whose posts never landed shows them again. The welcome step remembers the versions when it records them too. The API records a consent it already has once (§4), so a person with an account who sees the screens again adds no row.
   - **A no withdraws nothing.** A no before the bank posts nothing about research: an account whose research consent is active keeps it, and withdrawing stays in Settings. A yes from an account that already has one adds no row.
   - **The welcome step is the fallback.** Onboarding still opens on it whenever the status lists the terms or the privacy summary: a wording that changed after the sign-in (Kerttu's case), a build older than the API's wording, a post that failed, or answers lost with the process. It shows the same norms and cards through the same component, and no "Step x of n" line; the steps are counted from the name, eight of them.
   - **Onboarding's research step goes.** Research is asked before the bank, and Settings keeps its switch (§9's "at any time"). A yes whose post failed is not asked again in onboarding; the switch gives it. The sensitive-answers consent stays on the seeks step, beside the answer it covers (§10, ADR-019 §4).

   What follows from it:
   - `given_at` is the time of the post right after the exchange, not of the tap: `ConsentRequest` carries no time, and the API is unchanged. The bank login lies between the two, a minute or so. The version and the language are what the person read.
   - The `research_opt_in` event (ADR-011 §4) is written at that post, so `sinceRegistrationD` will mostly read 0 and `accountActive` false. That still answers its question, when in their journey people join: most now join at the start. ADR-011 §4's aside, that activation comes before the research screen in the app's order, no longer holds; the registry is unchanged.
   - The answers live in the process's memory. Android may end the process while the browser shows the bank; the app then comes back without them, the exchange posts nothing, and the welcome step asks for the terms and the privacy summary. The research question is then Settings' alone.
   - A phone shared between two people: the second to sign in on it skips the screens before the bank, since the phone has recorded the versions, and gets the welcome step after the exchange, which asks the same. Research is then Settings' alone for them too.

16. **Amendment of 2026-10-31 (#542, the maintainer's word of that day: whom one seeks needs the consent on the server too).** §9 chose, and §10 repeated, that the API does not gate `PUT /preferences` on the consent: the matching slice would have had to read consents from identity, which was not worth it for a client that already asks in the right order. #542 will let a person change the answer at any time, so the client's order no longer decides, and the choice was never quite safe: a save queued behind a withdrawal, or sent by an old or a modified client, stored an article 9 answer with no consent on record, one that matching would not even read (ADR-019 §4). So `savePreferences` asks for the consent of the current wording under the account row's lock, the lock a withdrawal takes, as `saveProfile` has done for politics and religion since the review of 10/10/2026. `seeks` without it is 403 `consent_required` and nothing is written, a window in the same request included; the age window alone needs none. The matching slice still imports nothing of identity: the app hands the reader in, on the caller's transaction, as it hands `saveProfile` its own. Where the consent is given and withdrawn does not move: the seeks step and Settings. Onboarding reads the status again on this refusal and says it in words, so a consent taken back on another device shows as such.

    Separately, every answer behind a person's or a staff member's session now says `Cache-Control: no-store`, set once in `requireSession` and `requireAdmin`. `GET /preferences`, `GET /onboarding` and `GET /profile` carry these answers, the panel's reads carry more, and an HTTP stack keeps what it is not told to drop. `/gate`, `/ponds/seeks` and `/account/export`, which said it themselves, lose their own line.

## Consequences

- Two tables (`preferences`, `consent`), two columns on `account`, migration 0013; one generated module (`consent.ts`) in the i18n package.
- Six routes: `GET /onboarding`, `PUT /account/gender`, `GET /ponds`, `PUT /account/pond`, `GET`/`PUT /preferences`, `GET`/`POST /consents`, `DELETE /consents/research`; the pond and matching slices exist in the API from here.
- #50 creates the `research_id` mapping when a research consent is given and removes it on withdrawal: `giveConsent` and `withdrawResearchConsent` in `identity/onboarding.ts` are the hooks.
- The profile's completeness now waits for real rows; no profile was complete before this change, by design (ADR-009 §4).
- Open, and the maintainer's: the gender list and its texts, the age bounds, the wording and versions of the three legal texts, whether `seeks` takes the special-category flag of ADR-009 §2 (its consent text and version would live under `legal.*` too); whether a consent given before the bank should carry the time of the tap rather than of the post (§15: a time in `ConsentRequest`, a change of the API that #418 left out).
