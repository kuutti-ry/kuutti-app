# ADR-024: Sanctions

- Status: accepted (the parameters of §3 and the open questions at the end are the maintainer's to change)
- Date: 2026-10-16
- Follows: TD-5 (moderation, the audit log), TD-7 (standing on the identity, re-registration, the erasure table), ADR-006 §5 and §6 (staff, the audit log), ADR-007 (erasure and export), ADR-008 §3 (what others may fetch), ADR-009 §6 (the card's subject must be active), ADR-013 §2 and ADR-015 §3 (who counts, who is in a pool), ADR-014 §12 and ADR-018 §5 (the demo's ban), ADR-022 (the break and `paused_from`), CLAUDE.md rules 5 and 6; issues #307, #106, #105, #84

## Context

The states a sanction needs exist since the first migrations: `account.state` has `shadow_banned`, `suspended` and `banned`, `identity.standing` has `suspended` and `banned`, the bank login refuses a banned or suspended identity, and the session guard ends every session of either. Nothing writes them but the demo's story and the seeds. #307 asks for the record behind them: who did what to whom, why, until when, lifted by whom, looked at again by whom; the person's sessions ended; their photos no longer served; a suspension that ends by itself.

Reading the code for it (#307's map) turned up what a naive version breaks. The state an account had before a suspension cannot be derived afterwards: registered and active look the same once overwritten. Sanctions stack, so a lift that pops a stored "before" restores a shadow ban that was lifted meanwhile. A shadow ban set by overwriting `state` on a person on a break ends the break silently or loses `paused_from`. A shadow ban already shows to its owner in `GET /onboarding` and in the export. A suspension's row erased with the account leaves the identity suspended for ever. A login exchange that races a ban leaves a session that comes back to life after a lift.

## Decision

1. **One row per sanction, on the identity and the account.** `sanction`: `kind` (`warn`, `shadow_ban`, `suspend`, `ban`), `reason` from a closed list, `note` (staff's words, at most 500 characters, never in a log line, a detail or an event), `ends_at` (a suspension's end, and only a suspension's: a check constraint), `created_by`, `created_at`, `lifted_at`, `lifted_by`, `review_note`, `reviewed_by`, `reviewed_at`. `created_by` and `lifted_by` are null for what no person did: the night's end of a suspension, the demo's story, the automatic rules of #105 when they land. Visibility is still computed, never stored (rules/api.md): the row is the record, the state is derived from the rows that are open.
2. **The state is computed from the open sanctions, never popped.** Every change (an apply, a lift, a review that lifts, the night's end of a suspension) locks every account of the identity in id order and then the identity row, account rows before the identity as the erasure takes them; a ban set through an erased account binds the identity's live account at once, and two writers reaching one person through two accounts cannot overwrite each other's standing. Each of the identity's accounts is settled from what is still open (`settleSanctions`):
   - standing: `banned` while any open ban of the identity, else `suspended` while any open suspension, else `ok`; `identity.suspension_ends_at` is the latest open suspension's end, null otherwise;
   - the base, what the account would be without sanctions: `registered`, `active` or `paused`; kept in `account.sanctioned_from` while the state says `suspended` or `banned` (the precedent of `paused_from`, migration 0038), read from the state otherwise;
   - the state: `banned` or `suspended` with the standing; else from the base and whether an open shadow ban exists: `active` or `shadow_banned`; `paused` with `paused_from` set to `active` or `shadow_banned`, so the break's resume restores the right one and the person sees "Welcome back" as before; `registered` stays `registered`, and the activation (`activateAccount`) chooses `shadow_banned` over `active` while a shadow ban is open.
   - `state_changed_at` moves only when what the person can see moves: `shadow_banned` reads as `active` for that purpose. A shadow ban moves no date anywhere the person can read.
   - A tombstone's state is never written: a ban on an erased account sets the standing only, which is what keeps the person out after the thirty days (TD-7). Warn, shadow ban and suspend refuse a tombstone (409 `account_deleted`).
3. **Who may do what** (the maintainer's parameters, #106's split of 16/10/2026). Warn, shadow ban, suspend and their lift and review: moderator and admin. A ban, its lift and its review: admin only, on routes of their own (`/admin/accounts/{id}/bans`, `/admin/bans/{id}/...`), so the role is checked by the guard and never in a handler. A review is by somebody other than who applied the sanction (409 `own_sanction`, not a 403: a 403 means the role, which the panel says once in its shell, #302); a review's outcome is keep or lift; one review per sanction. Nobody lifts or looks again at a sanction on their own identity (409 `own_account`). A member of staff is not sanctioned through the panel (409 `staff_target`): the maintainer revokes the role first with the moderator tool, so no moderator can lock an admin, another moderator or themselves out of the panel. The service parses staff's note again, so no caller can hand the database a note it would refuse and have the refusal echo it into a log line. The reasons: harassment, misrepresentation, under age, commercial use, scraping, other.
4. **Sessions.** A suspension or a ban revokes every product session of the identity's live accounts (reason `sanction`) and every panel session of the identity, in the transaction that sets it. The guard already refuses both states on the next request; the revocation is what keeps a lift from bringing old devices back. A shadow ban and a warning touch no session. The exchange that turns a login into a session now takes the account row's share lock and asks for the guard's own live states and an `ok` standing (`insertSession`), so a login racing a sanction either commits first and is revoked by it, or waits and gets nothing. It does not close ADR-022 §5's window for the break: `paused` stays a live state there, for #83. A panel login takes the identity row's share lock the same way and asks for an `ok` standing (`insertAdminSession`), so a staff login racing a sanction is refused or revoked, never revived by a lift.
5. **The end of a suspension.** The panel picks a day; the suspension ends at the start of that day in Finland (`ends_at`), at most 366 calendar days after today there, and the bank login's refusal names it (`auth_suspended` gains `&until=`, as `auth_cooldown` has; the app says "You can sign in again from: {date}", the day formatted in Finland's zone so a phone abroad reads the same day). The night (`end-suspensions`, before the counter and the gate, so a lifted person is counted that night) closes every suspension past its end and settles the account. A longer exclusion is a ban. A login in the hours between the start of the day and the night's run is still refused (a `shortcut:` in `sanctions.ts`; a lift at the login would close it).
6. **What others see.** A photo of another account is served only while its owner is `active` (`findVisiblePhoto`, the card's rule of ADR-009 §6), so a sanctioned owner's photos stop at once for everyone who was shown them, and come back with a lift because nothing was deleted (the `card_shown` rows are the viewer's export trail and budget). A paused owner's photos stop too: a break hides everything (ADR-022). URLs already signed live their fifteen minutes. The card, the pool, the gate and the counter already leave out everything but `active` or count `shadow_banned` as ADR-013 and ADR-015 decided.
7. **What the person sees.** A ban or a suspension: the next request signs the device out, and the bank login says why (and until when). A warning: the row now; the notice in the app is #176's, which reads it. A shadow ban: nothing, from any route (#105): `GET /onboarding` and the export's account state read `shadow_banned` as `active`, its date does not move, and the export leaves an open shadow ban out of its sanctions until it is lifted.
8. **Erasure and export.** Erasure deletes the account's sanctions except the open bans and the open suspensions, which carry the identity's standing and stay on the identity (the kept side of TD-7): a ban keeps the person out for good, a suspension still ends at its day and the night still lifts it. Lifted rows and warnings go with the account, and a kept row goes when it is lifted later (by an admin or the night); the audit log keeps the history for five years. The export lists the person's sanctions with kind, reason, the dates and whether a person looked at it again, in the request's language like the photo check's notes, never who and never the note: staff's words may name a reporter, and GDPR Art. 15(4) keeps a copy from adversely affecting others.
9. **The audit log.** `sanction.apply`, `sanction.lift` and `sanction.review`, each with the account as the subject (so the person's page of #306 lists them) and the sanction's id, kind and reason in `detail`, written before the change in the same transaction (a review also carries its outcome). What no person did writes no audit row: the night's end of a suspension, the demo's story; #105's rules decide their own record when they land.
10. **The demo.** Tapio's story bans him through `applySanction` by nobody (`created_by` null): the same row, settling and revocation as staff's, so the panel lists it and an admin can lift it on staging. The reset and the population's removal delete sanction rows with the identities. A standing set before this change with no row behind it (staging's Tapio until its next reset) is cleared by the next settle of that account: run the staging reset after the deploy.

## Consequences

- Migration: the `sanction_kind` and `sanction_reason` enums, the `sanction` table with the check on `ends_at`, `account.sanctioned_from`, `identity.suspension_ends_at`. `tombstoneAccount` nulls `sanctioned_from`. `AUDIT_ACTIONS` gains the three actions, `subjectType` admits `account`. `ErasureSummary` counts the sanctions erased; `AccountExport` gains `sanctions`.
- `GET /onboarding`'s `state` and the export's `account.state` never say `shadow_banned`. The research event `research_opt_in.accountActive` still reads the raw state: it is keyed by research id and read by nobody the person knows.
- No research event: the taxonomy is the research partner's (TD-5).
- A banned or suspended member of staff loses the panel at once (the guard reads the standing); their role stays until the maintainer revokes it.
- The automatic rules of #105 call `applySanction` with no actor; the reports of #84 and the panel's screens of #308, #311 and #312 build on these routes.

## Alternatives considered

- **Store "the state before" on each row and pop it on a lift**: wrong as soon as two sanctions overlap (a shadow ban lifted during a suspension comes back when the suspension ends).
- **Derive the state at every read instead of writing it**: every reader of `account.state` (the guard, the card, the gate, the counter, the photos) would join the sanctions; the state column is how ADR-013 and ADR-015 already decide, and settling it on every change keeps those readers as they are.
- **The suspension's end only on the sanction row**: the bank login would read another slice's table; `suspension_ends_at` beside the standing keeps `decideRegistration` pure, as `reregister_after` does.
- **A lift at the login when a suspension has ended**: closes the hours before the night's run, at the price of the login path writing sanctions; left as a `shortcut:` until somebody is refused on their end day.
- **Keep every sanction row on erasure**: more than TD-7 keeps; the audit log already has the history.
