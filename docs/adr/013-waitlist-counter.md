## Context

The first people in a pond wait: matching opens for a person when enough others are there. The honest answer to "is anyone here?" is a number, and because every account is bank-verified the number means what it says. It is also the one figure the public site may show. But the counter is public, and a count by gender gives people away in three ways:

- **by being small**: a cell of three is three people, and a total minus two published cells is the third;
- **by being uniform**: a pond of twelve women and nobody else says of each of its members what she declared, and "no non-binary people here" says of each what they did not;
- **by moving**: a total that goes from 25 to 26 with the women from 13 to 14 says what the one person who came that day declared. At 5,000 people over many ponds, one arrival in a pond in a day is the ordinary case, so taking the figures once a day does not make a day's arrivals a crowd.

The first version of this decision (a split published when every non-zero cell is at least k, exact figures every day, a row per pond and day kept for ever) stopped only the first; the security review of the change measured the other two with the function itself, and a second review of the rework measured what is left, which the section "What stays possible" names. #54 had one line of body; this ADR fixes who is counted, what may be said, and when what is said may move.

## Decision

1. **The waitlist is the people themselves.** No e-mail list, no web form, no browser flow (rule 8). A person is on it by being a verified account with a pond; "eID-gated" is the app's own bank login.
2. **Who counts.** Accounts in state `active` or `shadow_banned`, whose identity's standing is `ok`, with a pond, which the home write sets since #514 (ADR-061 §8), and with a complete profile (§3). Not `registered`, which is not active yet, not `paused`, not suspended, not banned, not deleted. A shadow-banned account counts exactly like an active one: left out, its owner could find the ban out by taking a photo away and watching whether the figures ever follow.
3. **One figure per pond.** `finished`: everyone who counts, an account that stands as active with a profile complete by `completeness()` (ADR-009 §4), which is what `verified` minus `finishing` was. There is no total, no split by gender and no number finishing, and the night reads nobody's gender: nothing the counter says, in public or on the card, tells that an unfinished profile exists (the maintainer, 2026-11-04: "no information about incomplete profiles at all"). A finished count beside a public total would give away a small number finishing, and a second trigger beside a total would publish two states a day and one person apart, so the number is the one figure. The rule is applied in TypeScript to per-account facts, with `preferencesFrom` and `answeredPromptsOf` reading stored values exactly as the single-account readers do; it is never restated in SQL, so the counter and the profile screen cannot disagree.
4. **What may be said: `publishCounts(counts, k)`**, pure, in `packages/schema/src/pond-counter.ts`, and the only place the rule lives: `finished` when it is at least k, and otherwise null, so below k a pond says nothing. No row, a k below the floor and a count that cannot be (negative, not whole) publish nothing: a broken row or a broken configuration fails closed. Every pond is listed, a small or empty one with nulls, so the list does not say which ponds are small by leaving them out.
5. **k has a floor of 10 in code.** `matching_config.waitlist_k` (10) may raise the threshold, never lower it: `waitlistK()` holds the configured value to `WAITLIST_K_MIN` and rounds a fraction up, the side that says less; `publishCounts` publishes nothing for a k below the floor, and the response contract cannot carry a number under it. A value that is no number is an error, answered as one and never as something a cache may keep. The floor is a rule (rules/schema.md), not a tunable: one configuration row must not be able to publish cells of one.
6. **When what is said may move: `moveFigures(standing, today, k)`**, pure, in the same file. The accounts are counted once a day (the nightly job `waitlist-snapshot`, 04:00 Helsinki, with the others), but a pond's figures follow the count only in steps:
   - figures below the floor cannot have been said under any k, so the day's count replaces them at once. The floor and not the k in force: figures said under k = 10 stand while k is raised, so that lowering it again says what was said before and not the pond a person later;
   - `finished` moves only when it has moved by at least k since it was taken. Whatever the difference between the old number and the new says, it says of at least k people;
   - the bands have a step of their own (§14), and the number has no other trigger.
   One count runs at a time, under an advisory lock taken before anything is read: two containers overlap during a deploy, and two counts that read the same standing row would each find the pond moved and write numbers one person apart. And one count a Finnish calendar day, whichever process asks: every API process arms the nightly job, so a count first looks, under the lock, at the newest `taken_at` (every row carries the time of the last count, moved or not) and does nothing when that is today.
   So once a number stands, one person finishing or going changes nothing that is said, and it cannot be seen falling under k because one person went. The published `day` of a pond is the Finnish calendar day its number last moved; a pond with no number has no day.
7. **No history.** `waitlist_snapshot` holds one row per pond: the figures the counter stands on. Figures that are replaced are gone. A series of daily exact counts would say, next to a tombstone's `deleted_at`, which pond and which cell lost one that day, which is what erasure removed (TD-7). How a pond grew can be read later from what was published, if somebody keeps it; the database does not. The table is never emptied or restored by hand in a deployed environment: the first count after that is exact and may lie less than k people from what was last said. Two exceptions, named under History: migration 0095 of #514 deleted every row as the ponds merged, and migration 0109 of #581 leaves every row's `finished` at zero, which the next count replaces.
8. **No roll-up.** A parent pond publishes its own members only. Summing children into parents would let a suppressed child be computed from its siblings and the parent.
9. **One public route.** `GET /waitlist` answers without a session, `Cache-Control: public, max-age=3600` on a success and on nothing else, under the ordinary rate limit, and is the only route on the public list that carries figures. It reads one small table and the pond list; nothing in it reads a person's row, and its body carries no account, no time of day and nothing from a profile. Browser origins are answered from the allowlist only, as everywhere (the waitlist site's origin is configuration, `CORS_ALLOWED_ORIGINS`), and every answer says `Vary: Origin`. CloudFront does not cache the API today; a caching policy for this path, if one is ever set, needs `Origin` in its cache key.
10. **Where the code lives.** The public read is the pond slice's (`apps/api/src/pond/waitlist.ts`). The counting reads across accounts, profiles, photos, preferences and ponds, so it is a job (`apps/api/src/jobs/waitlist-snapshot.ts`) that imports the slices' public surfaces; putting it in the pond slice would have made pond and profile import each other (rules/layout.md). A database with no figures gets its first at boot; a failure there is logged and the API starts.
11. **In the app**, a card on the home screen says the pond's number from the same route, in one sentence under §19's heading: with the bar, "{people} people have completed their profiles and are looking for:", the bar right under it (§12); with no bar, "{people} people have completed their profiles." Below k it says only "Kuutti is just getting started.", with no number and no word of a condition. §6's steps stay and no line on the card explains them, and nothing on it says that an unfinished profile exists.
12. **Whom a pond seeks (#507).** The card says whom the people of the person's own pond seek, as one bar right under §11's sentence, which leads into it. Three bands, from finished profiles only (§3's people, with the `special_category` consent at its current version, as the rounds ask): those who seek men and not women, those who seek both, and those who seek women and not men, in that order. Non-binary in the seek set moves no band, and somebody who seeks neither women nor men stands in none. The nightly job counts the bands with the number into three more columns of `waitlist_snapshot` (migration 0089, generated, defaulting to zero), so §7's one row and §8's no roll-up hold for them. Whom one seeks is article 9 data (TD-14): no log line and no research event carries a band.

    The bar is one continuous bar, one colour per band, with no gap and no split between the portions, its outer corners rounded as one shape, and no pattern (the maintainer, 2026-11-04: "the bar, with colors, no splits between the portions, a continuous bar."). The percentages stand under it and smaller, so that they fit (the maintainer, the same day), in the bar's left-to-right order, each with its word ("35 % men") and a small swatch of its band's colour: meaning is never by colour alone, the words and the order carry it. They wrap at the largest text, nothing is animated, and the bar is one element for a screen reader, which hears the three figures in words (`pond.counter.seeks.label`). The colours are three tokens in all four sets of `apps/mobile/src/theme/tokens.css`, `--seeks-men`, `--seeks-everyone` and `--seeks-women`, and `apps/mobile/src/theme/contrast.ts` holds neighbours at least 3:1 apart. They are an agent's, tried by calculation with no pink and blue and told to the maintainer, and the designer's to change.
13. **What may be said of the bands: `seeksShares(bands, k)`**, pure, in `packages/schema/src/waitlist.ts`: percentages of the three together, never a count (the maintainer, 30/10/2026), in steps of five (`SEEKS_SHARE_STEP`, a rule and not a tunable, as k's floor is). A band below k is not said and then neither are the others, since two shares give the third, so a bar is whole or absent; §5's floor holds. A band says at least 5 %, since 0 % would say it holds nobody, and the largest band takes what the others leave, so the shares add up to 100. The step does the work a rule on the fourth cell would otherwise have to do. The people in no band are that fourth cell: the finished profiles (`finished`, on the same card) minus the bands' sum. In whole percent the shares near k are counts, and that subtraction pinned a cell under k in 8,927 of 30,294 states of ponds of 31 to 60 finished profiles with one to nine people in no band. A rule that the cell be none or at least k, as §4 once asked of the people with no declared gender, would close it and would hide the bar in every pond with a few such people, the largest ponds among them, where the shares could not be turned back into counts anyway. Steps of five leave 1 state of the 30,294, measured over `seeksShares` itself.
14. **When the bands may move.** §6's step does not carry over. The number counts everybody with a finished profile and the bands only those who stand in one, so the number can move by ten while the bands move by one, and at a pond near k the shares would then say what the one person who finished seeks; and a change of whom one seeks moves the bands and not the number. So `moveFigures` moves the bands on their own, once at least k people have come, gone or changed band since they were taken: each person adds at most one to what rose and one to what fell, so the larger of the two is how few people the difference can be of. The number stands or moves as before. A move of the bands alone leaves the pond's `day`, which is public and belongs to the public number: moved, it would say in public, with no session, that whom people seek has changed. A row from before the bands stands at zero, which says nothing, and takes them at the first count that finds k people in them.
15. **A temporary route.** #507 asked for the bands in the counter's answer, on the `GET /ponds/counter` of #500's rename, which has not landed. The maintainer, 30/10/2026: a temporary endpoint exposing only the percentages the bar needs. So `GET /ponds/seeks` answers a session with the shares of the caller's own pond or null, `no-store`, nothing else, and `GET /waitlist` carries no band: §9's one public route stays what it was, and the gender split stays in its answer though the card no longer draws it (until #581, §3). The route is a `shortcut:` in `pond/routes.ts`. It goes into `GET /ponds/counter` with that rename, and whether the shares are public there is the maintainer's call: publishing them is a disclosure this section does not make.
16. **The consent's wording.** The special-category consent says of whom one seeks that it is stored "only to match you, never shown to anyone", and the Finnish binds. Bands are an aggregate, and they are still that answer used for something other than matching. Changing the wording is a new `consent_version` and asks everybody again, so it is a person's decision and not this change's. Until the maintainer has answered, the night counts no bands in production (`countsBands`, a `shortcut:`), so nothing is counted or said there; the rest of the counter is untouched. Every other environment counts them. To lift it: a version of the consent that names the statistic, or no bar.
17. **The counter is the pond's figure, not a waitlist's (#500, ADR-060 §8).** Nobody waits at a pond any more, so nothing about the counter says so. §1 now reads: the counter counts the people themselves, and a person counts by being a verified account with a pond and a finished profile (§2, §3), on no list and in no line. §9's public route is `GET /ponds/counter`, and §11's card is `PondCard`. The rename changes nothing else: who counts, the figures, k and its floor, the steps and the one row of §2 to §8 are what they were, and nobody's answer changes but for the path. The names follow: `GET /waitlist`, `packages/schema/src/waitlist.ts`, `apps/api/src/pond/waitlist.ts`, `apps/api/src/jobs/waitlist-snapshot.ts`, the job `waitlist-snapshot`, the table `waitlist_snapshot`, the key `waitlist_k` and `WAITLIST_K_MIN` are now `GET /ponds/counter`, `packages/schema/src/pond-counter.ts`, `apps/api/src/pond/counter.ts`, `apps/api/src/jobs/pond-counter.ts`, the job `pond-counter`, the table `pond_counter` (migration 0098, generated), the key `pond_counter_k` (migration 0099, custom) and `POND_COUNTER_K_MIN`, and the card's texts are `pond.counter.*`. The key moves whole: every version keeps its value, its number and who wrote it, so a k an admin has raised stays in force and the history reads on, and this is the one way a version of a key that lives is touched (rules/db.md). The custom migration's SQL, a change of data rows and of a primary key's name that the schema DSL cannot express (rule 10):

    ```sql
    UPDATE "matching_config" SET "key" = 'pond_counter_k' WHERE "key" = 'waitlist_k';
    ALTER TABLE "pond_counter" RENAME CONSTRAINT "waitlist_snapshot_pkey" TO "pond_counter_pkey";
    ```

    This record keeps its title, its file name and its words for what was decided when it was written.
18. **The bar joins the counter, behind the session (#507, #500).** §15's `GET /ponds/seeks` is gone, and the `shortcut:` with it: the bands are in `GET /ponds/counter`, as `seeks` beside each pond's number, and `PondSeeksResponse` goes. The shares stay where §15 put them, since whether they are public is still the maintainer's call. A read with no session is §9's read: public, cacheable, and `seeks` is null on every pond. A read that offers a session goes through the same guard as any other route, so one that does not stand is a 401 and never the public answer; the one that stands is answered `no-store`, spends the person's budget and not the address's, and carries the shares on the caller's own pond alone. §9's "nothing in it reads a person's row" therefore holds for a read with no session; with one, the route reads the caller's own pond and that pond's bands and nothing else of theirs, in one statement scoped by the session's account (rule 6), as §15's route did. Every 200 says `Vary: Authorization` beside `Vary: Origin`, so a cache that kept the public answer does not hand it to somebody who asked with a session; a caching policy for this path, if one is ever set (§9), needs `Authorization` in its cache key as well, or CloudFront drops the header and the bar goes. The app now reads the route with the person's session, so its read is kept by nobody and goes out on every opening of the home screen, one read where there were two, and a failure of it costs the card its figures and its bar together. If the maintainer makes the shares public, `seeks` is filled on every pond and the session falls out of this route; the contract does not change.
19. **The card names no area (#500, ADR-061 History).** The maintainer's word of 2026-11-01: there is one pond, so §11's card stands under one heading, "People on Kuutti" (`pond.counter.title`), with no line for a name, and the sentence below k says "Kuutti is just getting started"; §12's "the person's own pond" is everybody's, and the counter, its route and the word in code stay. Following from that, the agent's and not put to him: the phone takes the first entry of the answer and needs nothing from the status; the answer keeps its shape, a list under `ponds`, so that `PondCounterResponse` stays as §18 left it; each entry's `pond` is smaller by `parentId` and `nameInessive`. §8 has nothing left to say, since no pond has a parent.

## What stays possible, and what it costs

The number says nothing of anybody's answer. What follows of an answer needs a bar that is said before and after a step, so a pond with at least ten finished profiles in every band; all of it is rare, and none of it is closed by anything short of drawing no bar.

- **People who know each other's answers.** Nine people who finish after a step of the bar and know whom they seek can subtract themselves from the next one, as far as the steps of five let them: what is left is the tenth. Ten people acting together can also bring a step about on a night of their choosing, by taking a break or coming back from one. Bank identification makes them nine real people and not nine accounts of one, which is what it costs; it is the limit of hiding a person among k.
- **When.** A step of the number, and the `day` of a pond, say that somebody finished a profile or went on or before that day. That is about who has a finished profile and when, never about an answer; the `day` saves a reader the daily polling and adds nothing to it. The first number is said on the night the tenth person finishes, since figures below the floor are replaced at once (§6).
- **The row and an erased person, for somebody who holds the database.** The standing number still counts a person erased since it was taken, until the pond's next step: the row, the live accounts and the tombstone's `deleted_at` together give back the pond erasure removed, and that the person's profile was finished. The bands do the same and give back whom the person sought, article 9 data (TD-14), until they have moved by k, which may be after the number has stepped; and a withdrawal of the `special_category` consent leaves the same residual for a live account, with `withdrawn_at` for the time. For a pond below the floor that is one night; for a published pond it lasts until the pond has moved by k, which in a quiet pond is months. Taking the person out of the row at erasure would close it and would show the departure to everybody, so the two aims pull against each other while the figures are exact. Backups hold the account as it was before erasure for as long as they are kept, which bounds what the row adds. The maintainer's call under TD-7.
- **A raised k delays a pond.** Under a k above the floor, a pond standing between 10 and k - 1 says its first number at its standing number plus k, not at k: the price of not judging "never said" by tonight's k. The card names neither a condition nor a moment for it: below a number it says only that Kuutti is just getting started (§11).
- **The number is stale by design**, by up to k - 1 people, and a shrinking pond keeps its last number until it has lost k. The card no longer says so (§11).
- **A uniform step of the bands.** If everybody who finished between two published states seeks the same, somebody who knows that a person finished in between knows what that person seeks. Steps of five blur this and do not close it.
- **Nobody in no band.** Where the shares do pin the bands' sum, as in the one state of §13, a reader can see that nobody with a finished profile there seeks only non-binary people.
- **The shares are rounded.** A band of ten in a pond of thousands reads 5 %, more than it holds, and equal bands can read 5 apart. The card is coarse on purpose.
- **The bar and the number come from different nights.** Each moves on its own sum, so the shares may be newer or older than the number beside them, by fewer than k people.
- **The middle band in the light set.** `contrast.ts` holds a band against its neighbours, not against the card. In the light set the middle band stands 1.78:1 off the card, on the bar's top and bottom edges and as a swatch, which is the colour's own and is not held; in the other three sets it stands 3.14:1 or more. The words and the order carry the meaning, and the colours are the designer's to change (§12).
- **The bar's two ends are close in lightness.** They never touch, so `contrast.ts` does not hold them: men against women stand 1.33:1 in the light set, 1.36:1 in dark, 1.08:1 in high contrast and 1.11:1 in high-contrast dark. At 320 pt or the largest text "women" can wrap under "men", with a swatch that looks much the same; the word says which is which.

## Consequences

- One table (`waitlist_snapshot`, one row per pond, migration 0016, generated), one `matching_config` row (`waitlist_k`, migration 0017, custom, with the seed carrying the same row), one nightly job, one boot step, one public route. The custom migration's SQL, a versioned data row the schema DSL cannot express (rule 10):

  ```sql
  INSERT INTO "matching_config" ("version", "key", "value", "created_by") VALUES
    (1, 'waitlist_k', '10'::jsonb, 'migration:0017')
  ON CONFLICT ("key", "version") DO NOTHING;
  ```
- Migration 0109 (#581, generated) adds `finished`, not null with a default of 0, and drops `verified`, `woman`, `man`, `non_binary` and `finishing`.
- The answer's contract is strict and requires `finished`, so a bundle older than #581 fails its parse against the new API, and the card says "The numbers could not be read." until it takes the update. A new bundle against the old API does the same during a deploy.
- The job's log line says the day, how many ponds there are and how many moved: no figure of any pond.
- The website may read `GET /ponds/counter` from its allowlisted origin, and today reads nothing of it (#581 looked); nothing else about it is decided here.
- The per-person gate ("matching opens for you when…") is M4's: it depends on the person's own hard filters, and this counter deliberately says nothing about it.
- Open, and the maintainer's: k above the floor; whether `paused` accounts count; the hour of the count; whether the bar's shares are public once they join the counter (§15). Whether the number counts only complete profiles and whether a split is said in any form were settled on 2026-11-04 (History).
