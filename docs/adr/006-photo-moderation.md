# ADR-006: Photo moderation with Rekognition and a human queue

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-09-25
- Follows: TD-8 (photos are moderated before anyone else sees them), TD-5 (audit), TD-17 (the panel is English), TD-4 (one vendor), `.claude/rules/api.md` Media and Identity, `.claude/rules/admin.md`, `.claude/rules/db.md` (audit retention), security checklist lines 10, 54 and 67, ADR-005; issue #49

## Context

A dating app without pre-publication photo moderation ships pornography and other people's faces within a day. Every upload is `pending` until something says otherwise (#48). What says otherwise, what is kept of its answer, who may overrule it, and how those people get in are retention and identity decisions, hence an ADR.

## Decision

1. **Amazon Rekognition is the automatic check, as a processor.** Three calls per photo through the instance role, on the `card` variant only, from the API: `DetectModerationLabels` (minimum confidence 30, so the low labels exist for the profile tips of #56), `DetectFaces` (BoundingBox, Quality, and Sunglasses only — never `ALL`, which would return Gender), and `DetectLabels` filtered to the scene names tips care about (`Mirror`, `Bathroom`, and `Fish`, `Fishing` since #253; sunglasses come from the face, since a pair on the head or in a hand covers no eyes). A failed `DetectLabels` leaves the tips without the scene and never holds the decision. Rekognition's detect APIs are stateless: the bytes are in the request and nothing of ours is stored on that side. AWS may use content sent to its AI services to improve them unless the account opts out; the opt-out is an Organizations policy (`aws:ai-services-opt-out`), which this account can set once it sits under the association's organisation (ADR-001, step 7), and the maintainer sets it then. The instance role may call exactly these three actions (`RekognitionDetect` in `modules/compute`); the calls are counted in CloudWatch from the `photo moderated` log line.
2. **Three outcomes; a person only where the check is unsure.** The decision is a pure function over `matching_config` rows. What the check is sure of it decides itself, and the owner reads why in words at once, in this order: a moderation label of a hard group at or above that group's reject line (the group's reason), no face found at all (`no_person`), more than one face at or above `photo_moderation_face_threshold` (default 90; `several_people`). What it is unsure of goes to the queue by name: a hard label between its group's lines, a label the check does not know at or above `photo_moderation_label_threshold` (default 60; a new taxonomy, say), or faces found but none at the face threshold (`no_face`). One sure face with nothing flagged approves the photo. The hard groups and their lines (`apps/api/src/media/label-groups.ts`, Rekognition's taxonomy version 7; the lines `photo_moderation_<group>_review` and `_reject`, 60 and 90, migration 0030): **nudity** (all of Explicit, Implied Nudity, Obstructed Intimate Parts; reason `nudity`), **violence** (all of Violence, weapons included; `violence`), **disturbing** (Visually Disturbing; `other`), **hate** (Hate Symbols; `other`). Never a reason to hold a photo back: a shirtless chest, a bare back, partial nudity, a kiss, swimwear and underwear, alcohol, drugs and tobacco (Rekognition cannot tell a joint from a cigarette), a middle finger, gambling; scene and face tip signals neither. This is a dating app, not a photo gallery: a profile photo shows its owner alone, and what the check is sure of does not load the moderators (#206, #253, the maintainer's decisions of 12/10/2026). A second face under the threshold does not count, so an uncertain face never rejects. Every other rejection is a person's. The owner may ask a person to look at the check's own rejection, once (`POST /photos/{id}/appeal`; `photo.appealable`, cleared by the appeal or a person's decision), and the photo waits in the queue again, marked as appealed; never over a nudity label at or above `photo_moderation_nudity_no_appeal` (95, migration 0033), so no moderator is sent a picture the check was that sure of. A rejection for a hard label at or above its group's usual reject line leaves a note (`photo_check_note`: the account, the photo, the group, the time; nothing of the picture). Two notes within 30 days make the check stricter for that account for 30 days from the second: every hard group rejects from 75 (`photo_closer_look_attempts`, `_window_days`, `_days`, `_reject`, migration 0035), computed from the notes and never stored. Only what the usual lines are sure of is noted, so a closer look never prolongs itself on what it alone rejected, and a person approving the photo on appeal removes its note. Nothing else follows: no ban, and the panel shows nothing of the notes. An appeal of a rejection only the closer look made reaches a moderator like any other, and its confidences, under the usual line, let a careful reader infer the closer look: the price of keeping the appeal open where the check is stricter, which is where a false positive is likelier. The notes keep twelve months (a nightly prune), go with the account, and the person reads them in their export in plain words (ADR-007). The check never overwrites a person's decision, and a photo whose check failed stays `pending` (the nightly sweep looks again from the stored card variant); nothing expires into visibility.
3. **What is retained of Rekognition's answer.** `photo_review` keeps, per photo, at most fifty labels with their confidences and parents (moderation labels first, then tip signals from the scene and face calls), the face count, what was flagged, the model version and the time, plus a person's decision with who and when. Tip signals exist only for the profile tips of #56. It is deleted with the photo (cascade) and with the account's erasure. Label names never reach the owner (they get a reason from a closed list, in words) and never a log line (counts only).
4. **Where there is no Rekognition, everything goes to a person.** `MODERATION=queue` is the default; the SSM parameter `moderation=rekognition` is what staging and production set. A misconfigured environment fills the queue instead of approving anything: loud, not unsafe. A developer's machine runs the same way, which is also how the queue is exercised locally.
5. **Staff are identities with a role.** `moderator_roles(identity_id, role)` is the allowlist, by `hetu_hmac` through the identity row, written by the maintainer with `pnpm --filter @kuutti/db moderator` and never by a route. Staff sign in through the very same bank login and callback as everyone, marked as a staff attempt (`platform = admin`): a known identity in good standing with a role gets a one-time code in the URL fragment of the panel; anyone else gets `admin_not_allowed` and nothing is created. The session is eight hours, one bearer token kept as a hash in its own table, no refresh; the role is re-read on every request, so a revoked role ends access at once. This is the "minimal admin session in M3" option the issue offered; M4's admin work extends it rather than replacing it.
6. **Every staff view and decision is an audit row, and the table is append-only.** `audit_log(actor_identity_id, action, subject_type, subject_id, detail, at)`; the row is written before the signed URL is issued or the state changed. The application role owns its tables, so a grant cannot stop it from rewriting rows; triggers can, and a drizzle-kit custom migration (0006) carries them: a row trigger refusing UPDATE and DELETE and a statement trigger refusing TRUNCATE. What a trigger cannot stop is the table's owner disabling it (`ALTER TABLE ... DISABLE TRIGGER`) or replacing its function, so the table and the function are owned by a role the API never connects as: `kuutti_audit`, set by the maintainer with `infra/scripts/db-audit-owner.sh` (a migration cannot do it: it runs as `kuutti_app`, and Postgres lets an owner hand a table only to a role it could `SET ROLE` into, which would hand the power back). `kuutti_app` keeps SELECT and INSERT and nothing else; `packages/db/src/audit-owner.test.ts` runs the script's SQL as a non-superuser in the master role's position and proves that UPDATE, DELETE, TRUNCATE, DROP, `DISABLE TRIGGER` and `CREATE OR REPLACE FUNCTION` are all refused to the application role, and that the release file hands ownership back for a deploy. The API reads the boundary at boot (`auditBoundary`, everything schema-qualified so a same-named table elsewhere cannot fool it) and warns on staging and production until the script has run; a migration that touches `audit_log` needs `release`, deploy, apply (infra/README.md). The `public` schema goes to `kuutti_audit` as well (its owner may drop any table in it, and the application role owned it as the database's owner); `kuutti_app` keeps CREATE and USAGE on it, which is all a migration needs, and the test proves it still creates and drops its own tables while `DROP TABLE audit_log` and `DROP SCHEMA public` are refused. What this guarantees is that no row of `public.audit_log` can be rewritten, removed or dropped by the application connection; the API writes to `public.audit_log` by name so a `search_path` change cannot divert rows silently, and the boot check reads the table's, the function's and the schema's owner. What remains is database ownership: `kuutti_app` owns `kuutti` (db-app-role.sh), so from a second connection a compromised credential could `DROP DATABASE` once nothing is connected, or create another schema. Handing the database to the admin role is the step that closes that, with M4's admin work. Rule 10 wants migrations generated, not typed in; the custom migration is drizzle-kit's own path for what its schema DSL cannot express, and the file is as reviewed as any other SQL in `drizzle/`. Kept five years, survives erasure (rules/db.md).
7. **The panel talks to the API from its own origin.** The queue is plain React over the generated client (ADR-003), English only. A queued photo with a hard label at or above its review line is hidden until the moderator asks to see it, and its signed URL is not fetched before, so no view is recorded that did not happen (#253). Its origin is `ADMIN_APP_URL` for the login return and one of the CORS allowlist's entries; locally Vite's server. A deployed host for the panel does not exist yet; until it does the queue is used from a developer's machine against staging, which needs that origin allowlisted there, or waits for M4's admin hosting. The maintainer's call, named in the PR.

## Consequences

- Rekognition costs about 0.001 USD per image per call in eu-central-1; three calls per upload is what the metric shows, and the billing alarm covers the rest.
- A moderator sees the card variant of every queued photo of every account: real access to people's photos, which is why each view is an audit row naming the moderator, and why the role rows are few and granted by hand.
- The owner of a rejected photo sees the reason's text and keeps the photo in their own list (never auto-deleted); the objects stay until they remove it.
- The upload request now waits for Rekognition (about a second). Should that hurt, the check moves behind the response and the photo is `pending` for that second; nothing else changes.
- `auth_request` gained an `identity_id` column for the staff path; product logins leave it null.

## Alternatives considered

**Rejecting automatically above a high threshold.** Rejected by the rules (never auto-delete) and by the false-positive cost on real people's faces; a person decides, the machine only sorts.

**A separate admin login (password, TOTP).** Rejected: a second credential system for three people, weaker than the bank login they already have, and against TD-1 (bank ID is the account).

**Rekognition on the `full` variant.** Rejected: more bytes for no better answer; the card is what people see.

**Waiting for M4's admin work before any queue.** Rejected: photos would stay `pending` for everyone until then, so nobody could test the pipeline on a phone with a real approval; the minimal session is small and M4 builds on it.

## History

- 2026-10-12: repeated hard rejections (#253, the maintainer's decisions: two in 30 days, a stricter check rather than more work for the moderators, twelve months, gone with the account). Migration 0034 adds `photo_check_note`, the custom migration 0035 its four numbers in `matching_config` (rows of data the schema DSL cannot express). §2 says what follows from the notes, and that nothing else does. Left out on purpose: a moderator's own rejection leaves no note (only the check's sure ones count), and the panel shows nothing of the notes; either is a decision of its own.
- 2026-10-12: the appeal (#253, the maintainer's decisions). The owner may ask a person to look at the check's own rejection, once, except over nudity the check was at least 95 % sure of; migration 0032 adds `photo.appealable` and `photo_review.appealed_at`, migration 0033 the line. The queue marks an appealed photo and hides a sensitive one (a hard label at its review line) until the moderator asks to see it (§7).
- 2026-10-12: §2 rewritten (#206, #253). Several faces never queued a photo and were approved, while #142's negatives and storyboard scene 5 expected the group picture in the queue; and every photo without a face went to a person, which on staging was the whole queue. The maintainer's decisions: photos with several people and photos without a face are not allowed, the check tells the owner so at once (`several_people`, `no_person`: the texts a moderator's rejection uses), and the moderators are kept out of it unless the check is very unsure (a face found but under the threshold; a moderation label beside one face). The check's first automatic rejections: `photo_review.decided_by` stays null and `reason` is set. The outline has the rows; Noa's story and scene 5 say what the check does with each of her pictures. The same day, the moderation labels (#253): the hard groups above reject at 90 and ask a person from 60, each group's two lines in `matching_config` by the custom migration 0030 (`packages/db/drizzle/0030_photo_moderation_groups.sql`, rows of data the schema DSL cannot express); the allowed labels never decide; `photo_moderation_label_threshold` now applies to a label the check does not know.
- 2026-10-09: the first uploads on staging came back from Rekognition as `InvalidImageFormatException` and stayed pending: the check sent the stored card variant, which is WebP (ADR-005 §2), and Rekognition reads JPEG and PNG only. The adapter now re-encodes the card variant to JPEG in memory for its three calls; what is stored, what the queue shows and what the sweep re-checks from stay the WebP card. Nothing of §2 changes.
