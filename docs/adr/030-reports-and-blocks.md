# ADR-030: Reports and blocks from a card

- Status: proposed (both halves are built as written here; the points under "Open, the maintainer's" are his to confirm or change)
- Date: 2026-10-17
- Follows: TD-5 (moderation, research events never carry text), TD-6 (what a verified stranger learns), TD-7 (the erasure table: report snapshots for twelve months), TD-10 (the gate counts the people who can be shown), TD-12 (who can be shown to whom), ADR-007 (erasure and export), ADR-008 §3 (the shown record), ADR-009 §6 (the card builder), ADR-015 §1 and §10 (the pool, the gate's count), ADR-024 (the reasons, the sanctions), CLAUDE.md rules 5, 6, 7 and 10 and Product constraints, `.claude/rules/db.md`; issues #84, #87, #101, #105, #106, #311, #312

## Context

#84 asks for a report and a block. The maintainer split it on 2026-10-17: the part that needs no chat now, from a card; reporting from a conversation and its frozen copy wait for the chat (#101, M2), the moderators' queue and decision are #311 and #312, and the identity-bound ban exists since #307 (ADR-024), which a decision in #312 applies.

No route shows anybody another person's card yet: `buildCard` serves the owner's preview, and the rounds, liked-you and the match are M5 (#95, #96, #97, #100). What exists of one person seen by another is the card builder, the photos a shown card opens (`card_shown`, `findVisiblePhoto`), and the pool the gate counts (`inEachOthersPool`). A block has to hold in each of them both ways (rule 7) and in everything that comes later, without the slices importing each other in a circle (`safety` already imports `profile`).

## Decision

1. **A block is between two identities, set by one person, held both ways, final.** `block(blocker_identity_id, blocked_identity_id, created_at)`, a uuid key, one row per pair and direction (`block_pair_idx`, which a second block's `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` needs), and a check that nobody blocks themselves. Keyed by identity, so it holds for a later account of either: the blocked person who deletes and comes back does not reach the blocker, and the blocker who leaves and returns is not found again by the person they blocked. It is set from a card the person was shown (§5). There is no list of one's blocks and no unblock: a list would show the people again, and unblock and block again is a harassment pattern of its own. The app says so before it is set (§11).
2. **One test, two forms.** In memory, `PoolPerson` carries `identityId` and `blocks`, the identities the person blocked, and `passesFiltersOf` refuses a candidate the viewer blocked; `inEachOthersPool` asks both directions, so a block by either excludes, symmetric by construction (a property test holds it for any two people and any blocks). In SQL, `blocked_between(a, b)`, migration 0051 (`generate --custom`):

   ```sql
   CREATE FUNCTION blocked_between(a uuid, b uuid) RETURNS boolean
   LANGUAGE sql STABLE PARALLEL SAFE
   BEGIN ATOMIC
     SELECT EXISTS (
       SELECT 1 FROM account x, account y, block bl
       WHERE x.id = a AND y.id = b
         AND ((bl.blocker_identity_id = x.identity_id AND bl.blocked_identity_id = y.identity_id)
           OR (bl.blocker_identity_id = y.identity_id AND bl.blocked_identity_id = x.identity_id)));
   END;
   ```

   A SQL-standard body (`BEGIN ATOMIC`), not a string: Postgres parses it when the function is made and records `account` and `block` as what it depends on, so no later migration drops or retypes them under it without saying so. A function, so the clause is written once and no slice reads the safety slice's table or imports it: each reader names the viewer and the subject, which is rule 6 as before. Read-only and stable; each call is two lookups on the pair index. Where it holds today: the card (`findCardSubject` reads it, `buildCard` answers the 404 of somebody outside the pool for anyone but the owner, before photos, completeness and the budget, so nothing is recorded or counted), the photos of a card shown before the block (`findVisiblePhoto`'s second branch, both ways, asked again by `insertPhotoAccess` in the statement that writes the fetch-log row, so a block that lands while a URL is issued refuses it with the same 404; URLs already signed live their fifteen minutes, as with a sanction, and the `card_shown` rows stay, the viewer's export trail and budget), and the pool of the gate's count (§3). What the later pieces owe it: the round builder (#95) takes candidates through `inEachOthersPool` and any SQL prefilter asks `blocked_between`; the round's read (#96) asks it at read time, since today's round is built at night and a block at noon removes the card from both people's rounds at once; liked-you (#97) deletes the pending likes between the two in the block's transaction, silently, as an expiry looks; a match (#100) ends for both as an unmatch looks, the word "blocked" nowhere; the conversation (#101) closes for both and nothing more is delivered, and since a block can be used to dodge a report, the blocked side keeps a way to report a conversation a block closed (#101's to design); nothing is pushed across a block (#102); a contact release is revoked by it (#103). No match exists yet, so none is ended here.
3. **The gate's count honours blocks.** The gate promises `gate_k` people who can be shown to the person (TD-10); a blocked pair cannot, so neither counts toward the other's figure. The count reads the block pairs of the pond's identities once (`blocksWithin`, `jobs/pond-gate.ts`), as it reads every other slice's rows itself (ADR-015 §10), on the night's count and on a first ask alike. A block gives nobody a power they lack: anybody can leave somebody's pool by changing their own window, and that looks the same from outside; the figure is said in tens and follows slowly (ADR-015 §6) and no gate closes (§5), so a block can neither shut a gate nor reliably be read off one. Admission, the line and the share are about the pond, not pairs, and read no block; nor does the waitlist counter (ADR-013).
4. **A report is a block and a record for the moderators.** `report(reporter_account_id, reported_account_id, source, reason, note, created_at, outcome, decided_at, decided_by, sanction_id)`, migration 0050. Made from a card the person was shown (§5). `reason` is the `sanction_reason` enum of ADR-024 §3, #106's list, so #312's "act" leads straight to a sanction with the same reason. `note` is optional, at most 500 characters after trimming (a check in the database too), with no contact-details rule: the note may need to quote "he asked for my WhatsApp". `source` is `card`; #101 adds `conversation` and the frozen copy, a table that cascades from `report`. One open report per pair (`report_open_pair_idx`, partial on `decided_at IS NULL`): a second one while it is open answers the same 204 and writes nothing, and costs nothing of the day. At most `matching_config reports_per_day` (5, migration 0052; the seed carries it) per account and Finnish calendar day, counted under an advisory lock per account, the feedback's lock (ADR-023 §2), and asked before the proof of contact (§6); the next one is 429 `report_budget_exceeded` with `Retry-After` until midnight, said in words, and nothing is written, the block neither, so the app offers the block, which has no cap: a block costs only the one who sets it. The report and its block are written in one transaction, the reporter's account row held (`FOR SHARE`) so an erasure finishes first or waits.
5. **Only from a card the person was shown, or across a block that stands.** Both routes stand on a proof of contact: a `card_shown` row of the caller's on a photo the subject still has (`wasShown`, the media slice), or a block between the two accounts, set by either while both of them existed (`blockStandsBetween`, `safety/blocks.ts`: the `registered_at` of both no later than the block's `created_at`). Whoever set a block was shown the other, so the pair has met, and the block does not go with the photos; it is asked first. Never shown, oneself, an id nobody has and a later account of somebody blocked answer the same 404 and write nothing; so does a caller erased meanwhile. A card shown to somebody else gives nobody the right. The cost: for a first block or report, with no block between the two, the proof goes with the subject's photos (`card_shown` cascades with the photo), so somebody whose shown photos were all deleted since, by a new set, an erasure or M4's sweep of old rows (ADR-008 §3), can no longer be blocked or reported from that card; a card on the screen is served again each day it is shown, so in the app this is the minute between a card served and the photos changed. The round's own record (#96), or a record of the contact that outlives the photos, is the better proof (Open, 15), and M4's sweep must not remove the proof before one of them exists.
6. **What each side learns.** The blocked or reported person learns nothing from any route: their card and photo answers to the other are the 404s of somebody outside the pool (the card's shown record and a photo's fetch-log row each ask `blocked_between` in the statement that writes them, so a block that lands meanwhile holds there too), their onboarding status and export read as before, nothing is pushed, and their gate's figure moves only at a night's count, in tens, as when anybody leaves their pool by changing a window (§3). Between two accounts a block was set between, in either direction, every answer is the same whoever is still there: a block again, a block back, a report, and a report again once one is decided answer 204 (and file it, under the day's cap); an open report answers 204; and over the day's cap the answer is the 429 whoever is named, since the cap, the open report and the block are each asked before the card. So the person blocked learns neither that the other left nor that their photos changed, and the one who blocked learns nothing more of the person they blocked. A later account of either identity is not one the block was set between: the block holds for it all the same (§1), but proves no contact, so between it and the other's accounts every answer is a stranger's 404, and an account id passed on by somebody else links no new account to an earlier one. What remains, each one bit: (a) a first block or report of somebody whose shown photos are gone, with no block between the two, answers 404 where a paused or sanctioned person's answers 204, and the first 204 writes the block, after which the answer never changes (§5); (b) a person blocked who holds the other's account id without ever having been shown their card, which only somebody passing it on can give them, gets 204 where a stranger gets 404, and so learns that a block stands. A record of the contact that outlives the photos, which would also say who was shown whom, would close both (Open, 15). Staff are outside the block: the panel reads people as staff, every view audited, and a block between a moderator's own account and somebody does not hide that person's page (#306). #311 and #312 owe the other half: a member of staff who is either party to a report is not shown it in the queue and does not decide it, so a moderator never learns who reported them (Open, 17). The reporter gets "sent" now and the outcome in their export (§8); a notice of the decision is #176's.
7. **Not a signal.** No ordering, score, exposure floor, card or count reads a block or a report. A block feeds the filters and nothing else, and a report moderation and nothing else: a test (`safety/no-signal.test.ts`) holds the statements that name either table, or `blocked_between`, to the files that may. Being blocked is not an abuse signal for #105: it is too often only being unwanted, and counting it would be a desirability signal under another name; #105's rule counts reports by distinct reporters. Staff see no block: a block is a private choice, not an accusation, and no panel route reads the table.
8. **Erasure, export, retention.** Erasure keeps both, whoever is erased: a report with its note for twelve months from filing (TD-7's "report snapshots for 12 months"; the night's `prune-reports` deletes older rows, open or decided, and leaves their blocks), a block on the identities for as long as they are kept, which TD-7 keeps for good. The export (`reports`, `blocks`) carries the reports the person filed, with the date, the reason in the words the person chose it by (the app's, `safety.report.reason.*`, not the sanction's, which state a finding) in the request's language, their own note, the outcome in words and the decision's date, and the blocks the person set by date only; never the other person's id, never a report about the person or a block against them (GDPR Art. 15(4), as ADR-024 §8 reasons). Both are the identity's, from this account or an earlier one of it: they are kept on the identity through an erasure, so the person who comes back reads what still holds on them and what they wrote (GDPR Art. 15), the one place the export reaches past the account (Open, 18). The outcome, `acted` or not, is the one thing about another person the export carries, on purpose and in two words, never which action: the issue asks for it, and the DSA's Art. 16(5) asks a host to tell whoever gave notice what it decided, if Kuutti is in its scope (#188); ADR-007 §3 names the exception (Open, 9). `.claude/rules/db.md`'s kept side now names both.
9. **Logs, events, audit.** The request line only: the caller's account, the route and the status. Whom a request names, the reason and the note reach no log line, no error detail and no Sentry event (and the logger redacts a field named `note` at the top level and one level down, a backstop), and there is no research event (the taxonomy is the research partner's, TD-5). A report's refusal by the cap logs how many and until when, never whom or why. No audit row: a person's own act is no staff action; #312 writes `report.decide` and `conversation.view`.
10. **Shaped for what follows, and nothing more.** The decision columns (`outcome` `dismissed` or `acted`, `decided_at`, `decided_by` an identity, `sanction_id` the sanction it led to, set null when erasure deletes a lifted sanction, ADR-024 §8) are written by #312 and read by the export now; a check holds them together. The only indexes are those today's queries use (the pair index, the reporter's rows by time, the open pair); #311's queue and #105's window add theirs with their queries (`.claude/rules/db.md`). The demo's removal of identities (`deleteIdentities`) deletes both tables' rows on either side of them, so `demo:reset` and the population's removal start clean although both tables outlive erasure.
11. **The app.** A "Block or report" icon button in the card's header, top right beside the name, far from the like and pass buttons at the bottom, with its label and at least 44 points, and the same as an accessibility action on the name (the heading a screen reader lands on; the card itself is no accessibility element, since that would fold its photos and words into one), on someone else's card only: `ProfileCardView` shows it when its screen passes `onGone`, and the owner's preview passes nothing (`apps/mobile/src/features/safety/SafetyActions.tsx`). One dialog: Block, or Report and block; a block is confirmed in words that say it is final, holds through either person's deletion and is not told; a report asks for one of the six reasons as a radio group and, optionally, up to 500 characters, says that it blocks too, and sends. Once either went through the dialog says so (a Done button, so that the corner's Close is the only control of that name), and closing it hands the card to `onGone`, which takes it off the screen; one that lands after the dialog was closed hands it at once and is announced on both platforms, since no live region is on the screen to say it, and a failure that lands then opens the dialog again where it was, with the words. A 404 (the card's person or their photos gone meanwhile, §5) is said as "no longer available" and hands the card back too, since trying again cannot help. The day's cap is said in words with a Block under it, which says plainly that a blocked person cannot be reported later, so nobody is left facing the card; a failure, a tap outside the dialog and the back gesture keep the reason and the words, and only Cancel or a report that went through forgets them. The note's label says its limit. Each outcome is announced to a screen reader on iOS too (Android hears the live regions), and the focus moves to each step's title. The dialog is bounded by the window, the reasons and the note scroll, a drag dismisses the keyboard and the return key closes it, and the title and the description keep the app's cap on text size; none of this is tried on a device until #96's flow (types a note, at 200% text). The button's name carries no person's name; a screen that shows more than one card at once (#96 shows one) puts the name in it, in a nominative position. A decision is a button, never a swipe. No route or deep link names another person's account. The Maestro flow waits for the round screen (#96), which gives the card a screen and passes `onGone`.

## Consequences

- Migrations 0050 (`block`, `report`, the enums `report_source` and `report_outcome`), 0051 (`blocked_between`) and 0052 (`reports_per_day`).
- Routes `POST /blocks` and `POST /reports` behind the session guard and the person's limiter; the contracts `BlockRequest`, `ReportRequest`, `ExportedReports`, `ExportedBlocks`; the error code `report_budget_exceeded`.
- `AccountExport` gains `reports` and `blocks`; `ErasureSummary` is unchanged, since nothing of either goes.
- The nightly jobs gain `prune-reports`.
- #87 keeps deal-breakers and the soft ordering; its "a block of either side excludes" is done here.
- Specs: `features/safety/blocks.feature`, `features/safety/reports.feature`, two scenarios in `features/identity/erasure.feature`.

## Alternatives considered

- **Blocks keyed by account.** A new account of either would meet the other again, the stalker case; rejected.
- **The clause inline in each reader.** The same five lines in the card, the photos and every reader to come, with the safety slice's table read from three slices; the function keeps it in one place.
- **A report without a block, or an "also block" switch.** Whoever reports should not see that person again, and the reported person should not keep seeing the reporter; it reads as the reporter leaving their pool, so it reveals nothing.
- **A frozen copy of the card in the report.** The moderator reads the card as it is now (#306); a bio edited after the report is lost to view. A copy of the words is a later choice; photos could not outlive erasure anyway.
- **Staff seeing blocks.** Rejected in §7.
- **A composite primary key on the pair.** The uniqueness is the pair index instead, so the table keeps the uuid key every table has (`.claude/rules/db.md`).

## Open, the maintainer's

Each is one function, one row or one line to change.

1. A report always blocks (§4).
2. A block is final, with no list and no unblock (§1); a mistaken block is permanent, and somebody who blocked first cannot report from a card later in the app.
3. A block outlives both erasures, for as long as the identities (§8); it goes past TD-7's "nothing restored" for the blocker. The alternatives: it goes with the blocker's erasure, or it is kept some months after it.
4. All six reasons are offered to a person, `scraping` worded as "seems automated, or collects people's details" (§4, §11).
5. The note: optional, 500 characters, kept with the report for twelve months even after the reporter's erasure (§4, §8); the alternatives are no note, a note required for `other`, or the note nulled at the reporter's erasure.
6. Five reports a day and no cap on blocks (§4).
7. One open report per pair (§4).
8. What the reported person learns: nothing, reports about them stay out of their export (§6, §8). GDPR Art. 15 against 15(4), to settle with the privacy policy and the DPIA (#190), as ADR-024's shadow ban.
9. What the reporter learns: the outcome in the export, the notice of #176 later; the DSA's Art. 16(5) asks for it if Kuutti is in its scope (#188).
10. The proof of contact is a card shown (§5).
11. The gate's count honours blocks (§3).
12. Twelve months from filing for every report, open or decided (§8); the alternative is twelve months from an erasure.
13. No research event for a block or a report (§9).
14. The button's place and the icon (§11), the designer's to move.
15. The proof of contact (§5, §6) is a card shown, or a block that stands between the accounts it was set between. A record of the contact that outlives the photos (a row per viewer and subject written with the card, kept through the subject's erasure, deleted with the viewer's) would let a first block or report reach somebody whose photos are gone, close §6's two remaining bits (it says whether the person blocked was ever shown the other), and leave M4's sweep free; it is a new table with its own retention, so it waits for the maintainer, or for #96's round record.
16. The day's cap applies to every reason (§4). Steering a person over the cap to a block buries exactly the reports the moderators most need, `under_age` first; the alternative is no cap, or a higher one, for `under_age`.
17. Staff who are a party (§6): recusal in #311 and #312, by the identity; the alternative is a written policy without a check.
18. The export reads reports and blocks by identity, past accounts included (§8); the alternative is the current account only, for both.
