# ADR-030: Reports and blocks from a card and a conversation

- Status: proposed (both halves are built as written here; the points under "Open, the maintainer's" are his to confirm or change), amended 2026-10-18 by §12 (#351: the report and the block from a conversation, its frozen copy, the unmatch), 2026-10-19 in §7, §8 and §12 (#351: the copy's own readers, the export's `source`, no frame that tells a block from an end) and 2026-10-27 by §13 (#476: a report points at the one photo it is about)
- Date: 2026-10-17
- Follows: TD-5 (moderation, research events never carry text), TD-6 (what a verified stranger learns), TD-7 (the erasure table: report snapshots for twelve months), TD-10 (the gate counts the people who can be shown), TD-12 (who can be shown to whom), ADR-007 (erasure and export), ADR-008 §3 (the shown record), ADR-009 §6 (the card builder), ADR-015 §1 and §10 (the pool, the gate's count), ADR-024 (the reasons, the sanctions), CLAUDE.md rules 5, 6, 7 and 10 and Product constraints, `.claude/rules/db.md`; issues #84, #87, #101, #105, #106, #311, #312

## Context

#84 asks for a report and a block. The maintainer split it on 2026-10-17: the part that needs no chat now, from a card; reporting from a conversation and its frozen copy wait for the chat (#101, M2), the moderators' queue and decision are #311 and #312, and the identity-bound ban exists since #307 (ADR-024), which a decision in #312 applies.

No route shows anybody another person's card yet: `buildCard` serves the owner's preview, and the rounds, liked-you and the match are M5 (#95, #96, #97, #100). What exists of one person seen by another is the card builder, the photos a shown card opens (`card_shown`, `findVisiblePhoto`), and the pool the gate counts (`inEachOthersPool`). A block has to hold in each of them both ways (rule 7) and in everything that comes later, without the slices importing each other in a circle (`safety` already imports `profile`).

## Decision

1. **A block is between two identities, set by one person, held both ways, final.** `block(blocker_identity_id, blocked_identity_id, created_at)`, a uuid key, one row per pair and direction (`block_pair_idx`, which a second block's `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` needs), and a check that nobody blocks themselves. Keyed by identity, so it holds for a later account of either: the blocked person who deletes and comes back does not reach the blocker, and the blocker who leaves and returns is not found again by the person they blocked. It is set from a card the person was shown (§5). There is no list of one's blocks and no unblock: a list would show the people again, and unblock and block again is a harassment pattern of its own. The app says so before it is set (§11).
2. **One test, two forms.** In memory, `PoolPerson` carries `identityId` and `blocks`, the identities the person blocked, and `passesFiltersOf` refuses a candidate the viewer blocked; `inEachOthersPool` asks both directions, so a block by either excludes, symmetric by construction (a property test holds it for any two people and any blocks). In SQL, `blocked_between(a, b)`, migration 0051 (`generate --custom`):

   ```sql
   CREATE FUNCTION blocked_between(a uuid, b uuid) RETURNS boolean
   LANGUAGE sql STABLE PARALLEL SAFE
   BEGIN ATOMIC
     SELECT EXISTS (
       SELECT 1 FROM account x, account y, block bl
       WHERE x.id = a AND y.id = b
         AND ((bl.blocker_identity_id = x.identity_id AND bl.blocked_identity_id = y.identity_id)
           OR (bl.blocker_identity_id = y.identity_id AND bl.blocked_identity_id = x.identity_id)));
   END;
   ```

   A SQL-standard body (`BEGIN ATOMIC`), not a string: Postgres parses it when the function is made and records `account` and `block` as what it depends on, so no later migration drops or retypes them under it without saying so. A function, so the clause is written once and no slice reads the safety slice's table or imports it: each reader names the viewer and the subject, which is rule 6 as before. Read-only and stable; each call is two lookups on the pair index. Where it holds today: the card (`findCardSubject` reads it, `buildCard` answers the 404 of somebody outside the pool for anyone but the owner, before photos, completeness and the budget, so nothing is recorded or counted), the photos of a card shown before the block (`findVisiblePhoto`'s second branch, both ways, asked again by `insertPhotoAccess` in the statement that writes the fetch-log row, so a block that lands while a URL is issued refuses it with the same 404; URLs already signed live their fifteen minutes, as with a sanction, and the `card_shown` rows stay, the viewer's export trail and budget), and the pool of the gate's count (§3). What the later pieces owe it: the round builder (#95) takes candidates through `inEachOthersPool` and any SQL prefilter asks `blocked_between`; the round's read (#96) asks it at read time, since today's round is built at night and a block at noon removes the card from both people's rounds at once; liked-you (#97) deletes the pending likes between the two in the block's transaction, silently, as an expiry looks; a match (#100) ends for both as an unmatch looks, the word "blocked" nowhere; the conversation (#101) closes for both and nothing more is delivered, and since a block can be used to dodge a report, the blocked side keeps a way to report a conversation a block closed (#101's to design); nothing is pushed across a block (#102); a contact release is revoked by it (#103). No match exists yet, so none is ended here.
3. **The gate's count honours blocks.** The gate promises `gate_k` people who can be shown to the person (TD-10); a blocked pair cannot, so neither counts toward the other's figure. The count reads the block pairs of the pond's identities once (`blocksWithin`, `jobs/pond-gate.ts`), as it reads every other slice's rows itself (ADR-015 §10), on the night's count and on a first ask alike. A block gives nobody a power they lack: anybody can leave somebody's pool by changing their own window, and that looks the same from outside; the figure is said in tens and follows slowly (ADR-015 §6) and no gate closes (§5), so a block can neither shut a gate nor reliably be read off one. Admission, the line and the share are about the pond, not pairs, and read no block; nor does the waitlist counter (ADR-013).
4. **A report is a block and a record for the moderators.** `report(reporter_account_id, reported_account_id, source, reason, note, created_at, outcome, decided_at, decided_by, sanction_id)`, migration 0050. Made from a card the person was shown (§5). `reason` is the `sanction_reason` enum of ADR-024 §3, #106's list, so #312's "act" leads straight to a sanction with a reason from the same list (the moderator chooses it, nothing is preset; the report's reason is shown beside the actions). `note` is optional, at most 500 characters after trimming (a check in the database too), with no contact-details rule: the note may need to quote "he asked for my WhatsApp". `source` is `card`; #101 adds `conversation` and the frozen copy, a table that cascades from `report`. One open report per pair (`report_open_pair_idx`, partial on `decided_at IS NULL`): a second one while it is open answers the same 204 and writes nothing, and costs nothing of the day. At most `matching_config reports_per_day` (5, migration 0052; the seed carries it) per account and Finnish calendar day, counted under an advisory lock per account, the feedback's lock (ADR-023 §2), and asked before the proof of contact (§6); the next one is 429 `report_budget_exceeded` with `Retry-After` until midnight, said in words, and nothing is written, the block neither, so the app offers the block, which has no cap: a block costs only the one who sets it. The report and its block are written in one transaction, the reporter's account row held (`FOR SHARE`) so an erasure finishes first or waits.
5. **Only from a card the person was shown, or across a block that stands.** Both routes stand on a proof of contact: a `card_shown` row of the caller's on a photo the subject still has (`wasShown`, the media slice), or a block between the two accounts, set by either while both of them existed (`blockStandsBetween`, `safety/blocks.ts`: the `registered_at` of both no later than the block's `created_at`). Whoever set a block was shown the other, so the pair has met, and the block does not go with the photos; it is asked first. Never shown, oneself, an id nobody has and a later account of somebody blocked answer the same 404 and write nothing; so does a caller erased meanwhile. A card shown to somebody else gives nobody the right. The cost: for a first block or report, with no block between the two, the proof goes with the subject's photos (`card_shown` cascades with the photo), so somebody whose shown photos were all deleted since, by a new set, an erasure or M4's sweep of old rows (ADR-008 §3), can no longer be blocked or reported from that card; a card on the screen is served again each day it is shown, so in the app this is the minute between a card served and the photos changed. The round's own record (#96), or a record of the contact that outlives the photos, is the better proof (Open, 15), and M4's sweep must not remove the proof before one of them exists.
6. **What each side learns.** The blocked or reported person learns nothing from any route: their card and photo answers to the other are the 404s of somebody outside the pool (the card's shown record and a photo's fetch-log row each ask `blocked_between` in the statement that writes them, so a block that lands meanwhile holds there too), their onboarding status and export read as before, nothing is pushed, and their gate's figure moves only at a night's count, in tens, as when anybody leaves their pool by changing a window (§3). Between two accounts a block was set between, in either direction, every answer is the same whoever is still there: a block again, a block back, a report, and a report again once one is decided answer 204 (and file it, under the day's cap); an open report answers 204; and over the day's cap the answer is the 429 whoever is named, since the cap, the open report and the block are each asked before the card. So the person blocked learns neither that the other left nor that their photos changed, and the one who blocked learns nothing more of the person they blocked. A later account of either identity is not one the block was set between: the block holds for it all the same (§1), but proves no contact, so between it and the other's accounts every answer is a stranger's 404, and an account id passed on by somebody else links no new account to an earlier one. What remains, each one bit: (a) a first block or report of somebody whose shown photos are gone, with no block between the two, answers 404 where a paused or sanctioned person's answers 204, and the first 204 writes the block, after which the answer never changes (§5); (b) a person blocked who holds the other's account id without ever having been shown their card, which only somebody passing it on can give them, gets 204 where a stranger gets 404, and so learns that a block stands. A record of the contact that outlives the photos, which would also say who was shown whom, would close both (Open, 15). Staff are outside the block: the panel reads people as staff, every view audited, and a block between a moderator's own account and somebody does not hide that person's page (#306). The other half: a member of staff who is either party to a report, by any account of their identity, past or present, is not shown it in the queue, nor does it count in any figure the queue or the front page shows them (#311), and cannot open it, see a photo of its copy or decide it (#312): each of those routes answers as for a report that does not exist and writes nothing, so a moderator never learns who reported them (Open, 17). The reporter gets "sent" now and the outcome in their export (§8); a notice of the decision is #176's.
7. **Not a signal.** No ordering, score, exposure floor, card or count reads a block or a report. A block feeds the filters and nothing else, and a report moderation and nothing else: a test (`safety/no-signal.test.ts`) holds the statements that name either table, or `blocked_between`, to the files that may, and those that name a conversation's frozen copy, `report_message`, to a list of its own: the chat slice's statement that writes it, the moderators' opened report and its photos (`report-queue.ts`, #312), and the safety slice's hold and prune of the photos it names (§12). Being blocked is not an abuse signal for #105: it is too often only being unwanted, and counting it would be a desirability signal under another name; #105's rule counts reports by distinct reporters. Staff see no block: a block is a private choice, not an accusation, and no panel route reads the table.
8. **Erasure, export, retention.** Erasure keeps both, whoever is erased: a report with its note for twelve months from filing (TD-7's "report snapshots for 12 months"; the night's `prune-reports` deletes older rows, open or decided, and leaves their blocks), a block on the identities for as long as they are kept, which TD-7 keeps for good. The export (`reports`, `blocks`) carries the reports the person filed, with the date, where it was made (`source`, a card or a conversation, #351), the reason in the words the person chose it by (the app's, `safety.report.reason.*`, not the sanction's, which state a finding) in the request's language, their own note, the outcome in words and the decision's date, and the blocks the person set by date only; never the other person's id, never a report about the person or a block against them (GDPR Art. 15(4), as ADR-024 §8 reasons), and never a conversation's frozen copy, the reporter's own lines included (§12). Both are the identity's, from this account or an earlier one of it: they are kept on the identity through an erasure, so the person who comes back reads what still holds on them and what they wrote (GDPR Art. 15), the one place the export reaches past the account (Open, 18). The outcome, `acted` or not, is the one thing about another person the export carries, on purpose and in two words, never which action: the issue asks for it, and the DSA's Art. 16(5) asks a host to tell whoever gave notice what it decided, if Kuutti is in its scope (#188); ADR-007 §3 names the exception (Open, 9). `.claude/rules/db.md`'s kept side now names both.
9. **Logs, events, audit.** The request line only: the caller's account, the route and the status. Whom a request names, the reason and the note reach no log line, no error detail and no Sentry event (and the logger redacts a field named `note` at the top level and one level down, a backstop), and there is no research event (the taxonomy is the research partner's, TD-5). A report's refusal by the cap logs how many and until when, never whom or why. No audit row: a person's own act is no staff action. The moderators' work on a report is audited about the report, never about a person, so a member of staff who is a party finds no trace of it on their own page or through the audit reader's person links (#312, §6): `conversation.view` when a report from a conversation is opened (`report.view` for a card's, whose note is a person's words too), written before the note and the copy are read; `photo.view` with the copy's line and the variant before each photo of the copy is signed; and `report.decide` with the outcome and the sanction named. The cost: a person's page no longer lists that staff read their conversation, and once a report is pruned its five-year audit rows no longer say whose it was.
10. **Shaped for what follows, and nothing more.** The decision columns (`outcome` `dismissed` or `acted`, `decided_at`, `decided_by` an identity, `sanction_id` the sanction it led to, set null when erasure deletes a lifted sanction, ADR-024 §8) are written by #312 and read by the export now; a check holds them together. The only indexes are those today's queries use (the pair index, the reporter's rows by time, the open pair); #311's queue and #105's window add theirs with their queries (`.claude/rules/db.md`). The demo's removal of identities (`deleteIdentities`) deletes both tables' rows on either side of them, so `demo:reset` and the population's removal start clean although both tables outlive erasure.
11. **The app.** A "Block or report" icon button in the card's header, top right beside the name, far from the like and pass buttons at the bottom, with its label and at least 44 points, and the same as an accessibility action on the name (the heading a screen reader lands on; the card itself is no accessibility element, since that would fold its photos and words into one), on someone else's card only: `ProfileCardView` shows it when its screen passes `onGone`, and the owner's preview passes nothing (`apps/mobile/src/features/safety/SafetyActions.tsx`). One dialog: Block, or Report and block; a block is confirmed in words that say it is final, holds through either person's deletion and is not told; a report asks for one of the six reasons as a radio group and, optionally, up to 500 characters, says that it blocks too, and sends. Once either went through the dialog says so (a Done button, so that the corner's Close is the only control of that name), and closing it hands the card to `onGone`, which takes it off the screen; one that lands after the dialog was closed hands it at once and is announced on both platforms, since no live region is on the screen to say it, and a failure that lands then opens the dialog again where it was, with the words. A 404 (the card's person or their photos gone meanwhile, §5) is said as "no longer available" and hands the card back too, since trying again cannot help. The day's cap is said in words with a Block under it, which says plainly that a blocked person cannot be reported later, so nobody is left facing the card; a failure, a tap outside the dialog and the back gesture keep the reason and the words, and only Cancel or a report that went through forgets them. The note's label says its limit. Each outcome is announced to a screen reader on iOS too (Android hears the live regions), and the focus moves to each step's title. The dialog is bounded by the window, the reasons and the note scroll, a drag dismisses the keyboard and the return key closes it, and the title and the description keep the app's cap on text size; none of this is tried on a device until #96's flow (types a note, at 200% text). The button's name carries no person's name; a screen that shows more than one card at once (#96 shows one) puts the name in it, in a nominative position. A decision is a button, never a swipe. No route or deep link names another person's account. The Maestro flow waits for the round screen (#96), which gives the card a screen and passes `onGone`.

12. **From a conversation, with its frozen copy** (amendment of 2026-10-18, #351; the parameters the maintainer confirmed that day in #351's comment). The conversation names the other person, so it is its own proof of contact, read through the caller's own participant row (rule 6); a conversation not the caller's, or hidden from the caller as its blocker, answers 404, as every chat route does (ADR-031 §4), but for the block, which answers a block that stands with 204 (below).
    - **The report.** `POST /conversations/{id}/report` with the card's `{ reason, note? }` (`ConversationReportRequest`). In one transaction, under the reporter's advisory lock and with their row held as §4 takes them: the conversation's row locked through the reporter's participant row (`lockReportedConversation`), the report with `source = 'conversation'` and `conversation_id`, the copy, and the block, which closes the conversation (below). The copy is `report_message(report_id, seq, side, kind, body, photo_id, created_at)`: every message the conversation holds when the report is filed, both people's, a shadow ban's withheld lines included, written after the lock by one `INSERT … SELECT` from `message` through the reporter's participant row (`copyConversation`, the chat slice's, which the safety slice calls through the chat's index), so its words never pass through the process (rule 5) and a send either committed first and is copied or waits for the close and is refused. `side` is `reporter` or `reported`, seen from the report rather than the conversation's `a` and `b`, so the copy says whose each line was after both people are erased and the conversation is gone (`report.conversation_id` is `ON DELETE SET NULL`, ADR-031 §5). It cascades from the report and goes with it, twelve months from filing (§8). Its words reach no log line, error detail or event (rule 5; the PII test files a report over a thread of the fixture).
    - **The window.** Taken while the conversation is open or archived, and for `matching_config report_after_close_days` (30, migration 0061) after it closed, so a block cannot be used to dodge a report (§2): the person blocked, and both people after an unmatch, read the conversation ended and can still report it. Past the window, or once the other person is erased, the answer is 409 `report_window_closed` in words and nothing is written; the detail's `reportable` says beforehand whether Report is offered. A report of the same person still open answers 204 and writes nothing, as from a card, also when the conversation is hidden from the caller because that report blocked (a resend after a lost answer), found by the conversation rather than the person, so the answer does not change when the other person is erased; the day's cap is §4's, with its 429. The two people's account and identity rows are held (`FOR KEY SHARE`, what the report's and the block's foreign keys take anyway) before the conversation's row, the order an erasure and a ban take them in, so neither waits for the other in a cycle (two connections hold it in `safety/conversation-routes.test.ts`).
    - **The block.** `POST /conversations/{id}/block`: the block from the caller's identity to the other's and the close, 204, again 204 once it stands, and 204 with nothing written once the other person is erased: a block back answers 204 whoever is still there (§6). Every block, from a card or a conversation or with a report, closes the conversations between the two accounts in its own transaction (`insertBlock`, through the chat slice's `closeConversationsBetween`): `closed_how = 'block'`, `closed_by` the blocker's side, an earlier close rewritten (ADR-031 §2). The conversation row's lock puts the block in one order with a send in flight, and a contact shared there ends with the close (#103). The other person learns nothing but that it ended, and no frame tells a block or a report from an unmatch (§6): their phones hear the one `closed` frame the close sends, as after an unmatch, and nothing when a block or a report follows an end and rewrites only its `closed_how` and `closed_by`, since the conversation's notification fires only on a change of `archived_at` or `closed_at` (migration 0063, `generate --custom`, ADR-031 §9); the block's own notification reaches the blocker's side alone (`blockerSidesBetween`), whose conversation turns `gone`, also where the blocked person's erasure cut their link before the listener read it (`chat/live.test.ts`).
    - **The unmatch** (the chat slice's, `POST /conversations/{id}/unmatch`): the caller ends the conversation for both, `closed_how = 'unmatch'`, blocking nobody; 204, again 204. It writes `conversation_closed { how: unmatch }` for the other person (ADR-031 §7); a block and a report write none (§9).
    - **What each side reads.** The blocker never sees the conversation again. The other person, and both after an unmatch, read it among the archived, read-only, as "Conversation ended": an ended conversation names nobody, no name, no age, no thumb (`counterpart: null`), before and after either person leaves, which closes ADR-031's Open 10. Nothing says which of the two ended it or how. A close stops both read marks where it found them, whoever closed it and how (`markRead` answers 204 and moves nothing), and an ended conversation counts nothing unread: the blocker can no longer reach it and the one who ended it still can, so a mark moving under the other's last message after the close would tell an end from a block.
    - **Photos in the copy.** A photo a copy names outlives its sender's erasure: erasure keeps its row and objects (`photosHeldByReports`, `erasePhotosOfAccount`'s `keep`) and takes it off its message, which reads "Photo removed" (`detachPhotosOf`); `ErasureSummary` counts them (`heldPhotos`). `report_message.photo_id` has no `ON DELETE`, so a deletion that forgot the hold fails rather than empties a copy. The night's `prune-reports` deletes the reports past twelve months with their copies, then sweeps, through the media slice (`eraseReleasedPhotos`), every photo of an erased sender that no copy names any more, and their objects after the commit: erasure deletes every photo it does not hold, so one left on an erased account is one a copy held, and an erasure that held a photo while the prune let its last copy go is caught the next night. The demo's reset deletes the objects of the photos a copy held once the deletion of the identities has taken the copies and the rows (ADR-014 §12).
    - **The moderators' reading** (#312). `GET /admin/reports/{id}` answers the report with its note and the whole copy in order, each line said to be the reporter's or the reported person's, after its `conversation.view` row (§9), in one transaction that sends nothing if the row cannot be written; a `shortcut:` answers the copy whole, as the export answers a conversation, until a long one needs paging by `seq`. A photo of the copy is never in that answer: `GET /admin/reports/{id}/copy/{seq}/photo` signs its `full` variant for fifteen minutes on request, addressed by the line so no photo id leaves the API, after a `photo.view` row; staff see what the recipient saw, where the card's crop could cut away what was reported. Decided reports stay readable for their twelve months, each look audited.
    - **What is left, one bit.** In an ended conversation the person still in it, the person blocked or, after an unmatch, either person, can tell the other's erasure: within the window by the report's answer (409 where it was 204) and the detail's `reportable`, and at any time by a photo of the other's turning into "Photo removed" in the thread. A report must name an account, and an erased person's link is cut (TD-7); a photo goes with its sender (ADR-031 §10). The alternative, a link kept for the report, would keep what erasure promises to cut. The maintainer kept the bit on 2026-10-19: the honest 409 `report_window_closed` stays, since nobody is left to report, rather than a 204 that writes nothing (Open, 19; ADR-031 Open 10).
    - **The app.** "More" in the conversation's header, after Share contact, opens §11's dialog with Block, Report and block and End conversation, each behind its confirmation in words; after any of them the person is back on the list, which says what happened. The report says that the moderators get a copy of the conversation, the person's own messages and photos included, and that it blocks, for good, and, while the conversation has not ended, that it ends it for both; End conversation's confirmation says that either person can still report it for a while, never how long, since the window is a row of `matching_config`. In an ended conversation Report alone, while `reportable`, and no More once nothing is offered. A report refused for its window has the screen read the conversation again, so More stops offering it. The dialog outlives the `gone` frame that a report or a block sends the person's own phones, which can come before the answer, so its outcome lands as it would without the frame. The dialog primitive (`components/ui/dialog.tsx`) puts away a keyboard left up when it opens, and on Android, drawn edge to edge, shrinks into the space above the keyboard, so the note and Send report stay in sight, the card's dialog (§11) included. The list's Archived group says that it holds the quiet and the ended, and that writing brings back only a quiet one. The Maestro flow `chat-report` has a persona pair of its own, Laura and Emmi, types a note, and runs once per reset (ADR-014 §4).

13. **A pointer at one photo** (amendment of 2026-10-27, #476). A report may name the one photo it is about, as a number or an id, never a copy: no new kind of report, no new reason, and nothing of anybody's photo kept.
    - **From a conversation.** `ConversationReportRequest` gains an optional `seq`, stored as `report.line_seq` (migration 0084; a check holds it to a conversation's report). It holds only for a photo the other person sent that the reporter could read, asked through the chat's own reads (`counterpartPhotoAt`, through `messageFor` and every rule of the reads), never through the copy, which carries a shadow ban's withheld lines (§12): the reporter's own line, a text, a withheld line and a photo gone are all no. It is asked after the open report, the cap and the proof, and before the block hides the conversation from the reporter.
    - **From a card.** `ReportRequest` gains an optional `photoId`, stored as `report.photo_id` with `ON DELETE SET NULL`, so it holds nothing once the photo goes, and `report.photo_pointed`, which keeps that one was named, so the moderator reads a photo deleted since as removed rather than as never pointed at. It holds only for a profile photo of the reported person that the reporter was shown (a `card_shown` row of theirs, §5's proof narrowed to one photo: `holdShownPhoto`), whatever its state now, held `FOR KEY SHARE` until the commit and taken after the reported account, in erasure's lock order. A photo deleted before the filing has no `card_shown` row left and is stored as none.
    - **A pointer that does not hold is stored as none, and the answer is the same** (§6). It is asked after everything that decides the answer, so it changes no status and writes nothing of its own, and nobody can probe a thread or a card with it. A second report while one is open still writes nothing, its pointer included (§4); the cap, the reasons and the note are unchanged.
    - **The moderators' reading** (#312). `ReportDetail` gains `lineSeq` and `pointedPhoto` (removed, or the photo's id, its state and how many approved profile photos its owner has now). The panel marks the copy's line in words ("The reporter pointed at this photo") and lists it first under the copy's heading, still hidden until Show photo. A card's pointed photo is shown on request through the report's own route, `GET /admin/reports/{id}/photo`, which signs its `card` variant for fifteen minutes after a `photo.view` row about the report (§9), and answers a party as for a report that does not exist (§6). While it is approved, ADR-006 §2's take-down sits beside it (#477): its `photo.reject` row stays about the photo, as on the person's page, so it names no report. The queue shows nothing of a pointer.
    - **Unchanged:** the export (the pointer is about the other person's photo, §8), the events (none, §9), and the logs: like the note, the filing names no pointer in a log line, error detail or event; a moderator's look at a card's pointed photo logs the photo with the report, as every signed URL's issuance is logged.
    - **The app.** The full-size screen of a photo the other person sent offers Report this photo while the conversation is `reportable` (as the bubble that opened it read it, so no new read signs a thumb), which opens §12's report step with that photo set and says so; the card's report step offers an optional "Is it about one photo?" of the card's own photos, "Not one photo" chosen first, their pictures from the phone's cache, so nothing new is signed or counted (ADR-008).
    - **Left out on purpose:** keeping a pointed profile photo after its owner deletes it or their account (a retention change, TD-7 and ADR-007; the reported person is never told of a report, so they have no cue to delete it, and if that proves wrong the hold comes next); pointing at a match's profile photo from the conversation (the conversation names a name, an age and a thumbnail, never the card, ADR-031 §4); pointing at a text line (the moderator reads the text in order anyway, and the same column can carry it later); new reasons (asked on #188).

## Alternatives considered

- **Blocks keyed by account.** A new account of either would meet the other again, the stalker case; rejected.
- **The clause inline in each reader.** The same five lines in the card, the photos and every reader to come, with the safety slice's table read from three slices; the function keeps it in one place.
- **A report without a block, or an "also block" switch.** Whoever reports should not see that person again, and the reported person should not keep seeing the reporter; it reads as the reporter leaving their pool, so it reveals nothing.
- **A frozen copy of the card in the report.** The moderator reads the card as it is now (#306); a bio edited after the report is lost to view. A copy of the words is a later choice; photos could not outlive erasure anyway.
- **Staff seeing blocks.** Rejected in §7.
- **A composite primary key on the pair.** The uniqueness is the pair index instead, so the table keeps the uuid key every table has (`.claude/rules/db.md`).

## Open, the maintainer's

Each is one function, one row or one line to change.

1. A report always blocks (§4).
2. A block is final, with no list and no unblock (§1); a mistaken block is permanent, and somebody who blocked first cannot report from a card later in the app.
3. A block outlives both erasures, for as long as the identities (§8); it goes past TD-7's "nothing restored" for the blocker. The alternatives: it goes with the blocker's erasure, or it is kept some months after it.

6. Five reports a day and no cap on blocks (§4).
7. One open report per pair (§4).

10. The proof of contact is a card shown (§5).
11. The gate's count honours blocks (§3).
12. Twelve months from filing for every report, open or decided (§8); the alternative is twelve months from an erasure.
13. No research event for a block or a report (§9).
14. The button's place and the icon (§11), the designer's to move.
15. The proof of contact (§5, §6) is a card shown, or a block that stands between the accounts it was set between. A record of the contact that outlives the photos (a row per viewer and subject written with the card, kept through the subject's erasure, deleted with the viewer's) would let a first block or report reach somebody whose photos are gone, close §6's two remaining bits (it says whether the person blocked was ever shown the other), and leave M4's sweep free; it is a new table with its own retention, so it waits for the maintainer, or for #96's round record.

18. The export reads reports and blocks by identity, past accounts included (§8); the alternative is the current account only, for both.
19. From a conversation (§12): every message is copied, a shadow ban's withheld lines too; the copy is kept twelve months from filing with its report and holds its photos through their sender's erasure; `report_after_close_days` 30 (confirmed 18/10/2026); a block and a report from a conversation close it, an unmatch blocks nobody; the export does not carry the copy (the person's own words are in their conversations' part). The one bit §12 leaves, the person still in an ended conversation telling the other's erasure (by the report's answer within the window, by a photo turning into "Photo removed" at any time), stays unless a link is kept for the report; the maintainer kept it on 2026-10-19, the honest 409 `report_window_closed` rather than a 204 that writes nothing (ADR-031 Open 10).
