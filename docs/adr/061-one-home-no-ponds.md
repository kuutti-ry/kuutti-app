# ADR-061: One home, kept no finer than a place of three thousand people, and no ponds

- Status: accepted 2026-10-31 (#514, the maintainer's word of that day: "do it, pond is not relevant anymore. the location is mandatory field. only home counts now"; §8 is the reading of "pond is not relevant anymore" told to him in chat that day, and §6's order follows from it; both are his to correct on the pull request)
- Date: 2026-10-31
- Follows: TD-1, TD-5, TD-14, TD-16, ADR-002 (slices), ADR-003 (the contract), ADR-007 §3 (the export), ADR-009 §7 (the card), ADR-010 (onboarding), ADR-013 (the counter), ADR-015 (the pool, the gate), ADR-019 §1 and §8 (fields, formats), ADR-035 (the round), ADR-042 (the short pool), CLAUDE.md rules 5, 6 and 7 and Product constraints

## Context

Nothing located a person below the pond, which was a choice between the capital region and the rest of Finland. The field sheet of 07/10/2026 had asked for a postal code ("static, not GPS", bands of 5, 15 and 50 km, one change in 30 days), and ADR-010 §12 and the register parked it.

The maintainer's concern of 31/10/2026 is the one that shapes this: outside the capital, a single gay person in a postal area "would literally out someone". Statistics Finland's postal areas of 2026 (Paavo: 3,018 areas, 5.56 million people) show how real that is and that the name on a card is not the only way:

- 1,603 areas, more than half, have under 500 inhabitants, and hold 5.5 % of the people.
- A band outs as well as a name does. Measured between area centres, 63 % of the people have no other centre within 2 km, and 28 % none within 5 km; in areas under 1,000 inhabitants it is 70 %. For them "nearby" says "your own postal code".
- People move, and a band read from a home that changes freely is an oracle: move, read the same card again, and the circles close in on somebody (ADR-015's Context refuses the same pattern for the gate's figure).

Measuring only from the town a card shows was weighed and dropped: it puts about half of the capital's pairs in the wrong band, and reads somebody in Ivalo as nearby from Rovaniemi, 255 km away. Keeping the real code needed a rule for small areas and a limit on changes, and the maintainer said people may change location.

## Decision

1. **Home is mandatory, and there is one.** Onboarding asks the home postal code after the age window, in the pond step's place and in its place among `ACTIVATION_STEPS`. Five digits, checked against the list of §4. One home, whoever commutes: several places were proposed and dropped by the maintainer's word ("only home counts now"). It changes whenever the person likes, with no cadence; only the time of the latest move is stored, for §5. It is a step, not a part of `completeness()`, as `intent` is: an account active without it stays active, the status lists the step, the app sends the person to it, and the pool leaves them out until they answer.

2. **What is kept is a place of at least `home_min_inhabitants` people (3,000).** The digits go in through the body of `PUT /account/home` and are looked up in memory. Kept, in `account.home_place`: the postal area if it has that many inhabitants and that many are left behind its municipality's key without it; else its municipality; else the nearest municipality that has, on its own side of the sea while that side has one. The condition on who is left makes the number true of a key and not only of a row of the list: Aura's larger area has 3,455 people, and keeping it would leave municipality 019 standing for 547, one small area under another name. So where too few are left, counted with the small municipalities that fall to the key, the smallest large areas go back in until enough are. At 3,000 that is 34 areas, and 34 municipal keys would otherwise have stood for 66,070 people in groups under the floor. So 71 % of the people keep their own area (96 % in the capital region), 26 % their municipality and 3 % a neighbouring one, in 750 places, the smallest of 3,009 people. Where the area is kept the key is the postal code itself: the digits are gone only where the area is too small, and no text may say Kuutti never keeps a postal code. A municipality's key is its three-digit number. Keys are official codes, so they outlive a yearly list. A stored place is read through the same function, so a raised value coarsens every read at once, while the row keeps what it held until the person next writes: lowering the value again gives back an area a row still holds, and refines nobody whose key has no digits left. A key the list no longer knows reads as no home and the step opens again, with a warn line that names no key.

   This is the one number of the design: no name on a card, no band, no probing and no leak of the table narrows anybody below it.

3. **The card shows a name, never digits, and the person chooses which.** By default the nearest town: the kept place's municipality if it has at least `home_town_min_inhabitants` people (20,000), else the nearest that has, by centre; Maarianhamina always counts, so that Åland is not sent across the sea. Where an area is kept the person may show its name instead (`account.home_shows_area`). A new home starts from the town again. The area's name is the only thing the switch can show, so a person in a municipality-level place has no choice and the screen says what the card will say. The home is not a field of the registry and #515's Hide does not apply to it. Names are in Finnish and Swedish as the list has them; English reads Finnish, and no sentence is built around a name (TD-17).

4. **The list is the repository's, from Statistics Finland.** `pnpm places` reads Paavo's `pno_tilasto_<year>` and the municipality names over the network and writes two generated files: `packages/schema/src/postal-areas.generated.ts` (code and names, for the phone's autocomplete, behind the subpath `@kuutti/schema/postal-areas`, never the package root) and `apps/api/src/pond/places.generated.ts` (centres in ETRS-TM35FIN metres, inhabitants, municipalities). No table: it is public, read-only, needed in memory by the pool, and a diff in a pull request is the right review. Autocomplete runs on the phone, so a half-typed home never reaches the server. Paavo is CC BY 4.0: the source, the date and what Kuutti changed are said beside the data, in the app's licences screen and in the API image's notices. Posti's own file also holds post boxes and companies, which are nobody's home. A code the list lacks is refused with a text that offers the nearest areas.

5. **Distance is said in bands, and the band is frozen with the round.** The straight line between two kept places' centres, `distanceBand(km, edges)`: nearby under `distance_nearby_km` (5), close under `distance_close_km` (15), around under `distance_around_km` (50), far beyond. Never kilometres (ADR-019 §8). The band is computed when a round's entry is written and stored there (`round_entries.distance`), and the card's line reads it, so moving home and reading a card again says nothing new. The short pool (ADR-042) counts from the pool as it stands, so it would answer afresh from every place typed, and a relaxation undone would bring its cards back with a new band. A move therefore closes the day's short pool: while `account.home_moved_at` is later than the round's `built_at`, nothing is offered and a relaxation tapped is refused, until the next round is built. A move is one new reading a night, the price ADR-015 §17 accepts, and the freedom of §1 needs no limit. The time is the latest move's alone, overwritten, and says nothing of where. Only a round's entries carry a band: the owner's preview has none, and likes, matches and the chat build no card.

6. **Distance orders a round, before the soft count.** `roundOrder` sorts by band, nearest first, then by the soft count, then by the day's hash (`RoundOrdering` `band_count`). With no ponds nothing else keeps people local: 94 % of random pairs outside the capital are over 50 km apart, and the soft count first would put a high fit in Helsinki ahead of a neighbour in Oulu. This reverses the maintainer's listed "inside a soft-fit band" because of §8, and is his to reverse back; it is one line. `likedYouPlaces` and `comingBackOrder` keep their orders: what waits on a person is not moved by distance, and ADR-042 §1 has its own order.

7. **A distance filter is the seventh deal-breaker.** `distance` is a deal-breaker with no field: it accepts the bands the person takes (`nearby`, `close`, `around`; `far` accepted is no filter), held both ways by `inEachOthersPool` (rule 7) against the pair's band. It counts against `deal_breakers_max`, now 7, and `DEAL_BREAKERS_CAP` follows. Since everybody has a home it is never paused, stores `include_unknown` true, offers only `aside` on a short round (ADR-042 §3) and never says "doesn't say" on a card. ADR-042 §8's "every deal-breaker field is a card field" ends here.

8. **There is one pond.** "Pond is not relevant anymore" is read as one country-wide pool. `suomi` survives, being the one row every database has (migration 0027), and the rest are deleted; accounts and gate rows move to it without emptying, `waitlist_snapshot` is emptied and the boot counts it afresh, and `default_pond` version 3 names it. The pond step, `GET /ponds`, `PUT /account/pond`, `PondList`, `PondChoice` and `ProfileCard.pond` go. `account.pond_id`, the counter, the gate and `events.pond` stay and work on the one row, because #500, #502 and #503 own what becomes of them; the home write sets the pond, in the statement that sets the home, so that the counter's `verified` still starts after a gender is declared.

9. **Where it travels.** `home_place`, `home_shows_area` and `home_moved_at` live on `account` and go at erasure with the tombstone (`personal-data.test.ts`). A change overwrites, so no earlier place is held; what stays of one is the band on the entries of its days, which goes when either person's account does. Retention is the account's. The export says the place, what the card shows and when it last moved, in words and never a key (`account.home`), and each entry of a round its band. No log line, error detail, event, frame or push carries a home or a postal code: the code travels in a body the request logger does not read, a code refused or an area too small to keep never reaches SQL, a kept area's code is the stored key, and the logger redacts its names as a net. Research events carry the pond and no home (rule 5). Moderators see no home. The privacy notice must stop saying Kuutti never keeps a location, and `docs/runbooks/police-disclosure.md` must say what is held.

## Consequences

- `packages/schema`: `distance.ts`, the postal list and its subpath, `HomeUpdate`, `HomeSummary`, `PlaceName`, the steps, `ProfileCard.place`, `RoundEntry.distance`, `RoundOrdering`, the wider `DealBreakerField`; `pnpm openapi`.
- `packages/db`: migrations `0093_one_pond`, `0094_account_home`, `0095_home_distance_config`, `0096_home_moved`; the seed has one pond and the new keys.
- `apps/api`: the pond slice owns the home (`pond/places.ts`, `PUT /account/home`); `PoolPerson.home` is required, carrying the centre and the band edges so that the pure functions stay pure; `subjectFrom` answers null without a home.
- `apps/mobile`: the home step with autocomplete, the choice as a local follow-up step as the identity label is, a profile row, the card's two lines, a distance row among the deal-breakers.
- The demo personas type a code through the real route, the population gets homes from the list by inhabitants, and staging needs `demo-reset` and `demo-population` after the deploy, or everybody reads as having no home.
- One pond means `subjectsOfPond` loads everybody on each round build: no new path, within the 5,000 people of TD-3's box, and `docs/runbooks/capacity.md` should be measured again.

## Not decided here

- Whether the pond, the counter's words and "Your area" on the home screen go: #500, which also loses its per-pond switch as a list of one, and #502, whose "also people elsewhere in Finland" closes.
- Whether the band orders people coming back (ADR-042 §1), and whether #502's queue keeps §6's order: the queue is not built.
- The final words of the bands and the privacy text: English is this change's, Finnish and Swedish are flagged machine text, and `legal.*` is the maintainer's.
