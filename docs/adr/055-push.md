# ADR-055: Push through Expo: that there is something, never what or from whom

- Status: proposed (#102, built as written here on the maintainer's answers of 2026-10-25 to the issue's four questions: the native module now, outside #184's batch; the message, the round and the match pushes in one change; the parameters as proposed; the site's privacy policy drafted here, its version his; and to the review's two of the same day: no conversation in a push, and no round push to somebody who stopped opening rounds; the points under "Open, the maintainer's" are his to confirm or change)
- Date: 2026-10-25
- Follows: TD-3 (one box; push through Expo), TD-7 (what erasure deletes), TD-18 (no mail and no notice without a reason), ADR-007 §3 and §4 (export and erasure), ADR-011 §8 (a vendor's box and what it receives, the model for §8 here), ADR-022 §2 (a break revokes every session), ADR-024 (a sanction revokes the sessions), ADR-031 §5 and §9 (the live socket and who may read a message), ADR-032 (the switches this reads, and the permission line it left here), ADR-034 and ADR-035 (the round and its builder), ADR-038 (the match; its Open 1), ADR-045 (the app lock; its Open 1), CLAUDE.md rules 5 and 6, Product constraints (no real-time engagement push) and "Security and privacy defaults" (a new dependency); issues #102, #176, #184, #348, #349, #351, #384, #391, #409, #422

## Context

Nothing told a person that something had happened while the app was closed: a message, today's round, a match the other person made. ADR-032 built the switches per kind and left the push, and the line saying whether the phone lets Kuutti notify at all, to #102, which needs `expo-notifications`, a native module. The maintainer had put it with #184's native batch on 18/10/2026; on 25/10/2026 he chose to ship it now, as #422, #444 and #446 shipped theirs. He also chose that this change carries all three pushes (the message, the round, and the match for the person who did not make it), and confirmed the parameters the issue proposed.

A phone's lock screen is read by others, and a push travels through Expo, then Apple or Google. Whatever the payload carries, all of them learn.

## Decision

1. **The payload says that there is something, never what or from whom.** A push carries one line of the app's own words in the device's language (`push.message` "You have a new message", `push.match` "You have a new match", `push.round` "Today's round is ready"), no title, and `data` naming its kind alone: `PushData` in `packages/schema/src/notifications.ts`, `{ kind: "message" | "match" | "round" }`. Never a message's text, a name, an account's id, anything of a profile (rule 5's reasoning, applied to a vendor), nor the conversation: its id is the same on both people's phones, so it would tie the two devices together for Expo, Apple and Google, with the times of their pushes (the maintainer's of 25/10/2026). `push.test.ts` fails on a payload with any of them, with a fixture for each. No badge, ever (ADR-032 §4: no count). A push replaces an earlier one of its kind on the phone (`collapseId` and Android's `tag` are the kind). Each lives at most until the quiet hours begin and at most until the Finnish day ends (its `ttl`), so a phone that was off is not told in the night, nor days later after a block (§7).
2. **A token per signed-in device.** `push_token(id, account_id, session_id, token, platform, locale, created_at)`, one generated migration (0081): `session_id` references `session` `ON DELETE CASCADE` and is unique, so a device has one token and it goes when its session row goes; `token` is unique, so a token belongs to one session. `platform` is `ios | android`; `locale` is the language the phone asked in when it registered, one of those the environment serves (`servedLocales`), which the push is worded in. `PUT /push-token` with `{ token, platform }` registers the caller's session's token and answers 204: it replaces the session's earlier token, and takes the token from any other session it was registered to, whichever account's, since the device now holds the caller's session (an app reinstalled without signing out keeps its token on iOS, and somebody else's pushes must not reach the next person to sign in on it). That one statement is the exception to rule 6's scoping, and it says why in place. The app registers on every start while the phone allows notifications, when the token changes, and when the person changes the app's language.
3. **A token goes when its session ends, whatever ends it.** A trigger deletes a session's token the moment its `revoked_at` is set (migration 0082, below): a sign-out, "sign out everywhere", the app lock's way out (ADR-045 §6), a sanction (ADR-024), a break (ADR-022 §2) and a refresh token's reuse all revoke, in three statements of the identity slice today and any later one. A session deleted (the night's prune, erasure) takes it by the cascade. A session that only expired keeps its row until the night's prune, and the sender reads tokens of live sessions only, so it gets nothing either. `eraseAccount` deletes the person's tokens itself and counts them (`ErasureSummary.pushTokens`). A paused, suspended or banned person therefore has no token, by construction.
4. **The message push.** After a message (text or photo) commits, the chat hands its conversation and sequence to the notifications slice through a callback `app.ts` gives `chatRoutes`, as the first-message check is handed (ADR-033), so the chat never imports notifications. The push goes to the other participant if, read fresh: they may read the message through their own participant row, by the socket's own rule (`messageReaders`, the chat's: nothing across a block or a close, nothing from a shadow-banned sender's withheld rows, nothing to an erased account); they have no live socket (`live.online`, #349), since the socket already delivers it to somebody in the app; their message switch is on (ADR-032); it is not the quiet hours; and no push of that conversation went to them within `message_push_min_interval_minutes`. The interval is kept in the process's memory, per recipient and conversation (`shortcut:` a restart forgets it, so the first message after one may push again early; a column on the token would lift it, if that is ever seen). The push runs after the send's answer, which never waits for it: an answer that took longer when somebody was pushed would tell the sender whether the other person has a phone, their switch on, a hidden socket, or whether the sender's own messages are withheld by a shadow ban. A failure is logged by its kind. The process's one push (`createPush`, made at boot) is waited for at shutdown (`settled`).
5. **The match push.** A like that makes a match (ADR-038) hands the person to tell to the notifications slice after it commits, through `Matches.made`, which `app.ts` gives the round's routes, and the push runs after the like's answer, as a message's does (§4). The push goes to the person whose earlier like the match met, never to the one who just liked, who sees the match in the answer: if their match switch is on and it is not the quiet hours, whether or not they are in the app, since no socket frame tells of a new match (#389). This is the one push besides the round's and a message's, the maintainer's of 25/10/2026; `rules/mobile.md` says so. It answers ADR-038 Open 1 for the push; a notice in the app (ADR-032's `match` kind) stays open.
6. **The round push.** Once a day, at `round_push_hour` in Helsinki, everybody whose round for the Finnish day was built and not yet opened (`rounds.opened_at`), with a card that can still be shown, whose round switch is on, gets one push; a shadow-banned person's round too, as it is built, since a shadow ban tells nobody anything (ADR-024 §7). Nobody else: not a person whose round is empty, nor one who opened it already, nor one with no round (production builds none yet, ADR-035's `shortcut:`), nor one who opened no round in the last `round_push_idle_days` Finnish days, counted from their first round if they never opened one (the maintainer's of 25/10/2026: the push is for those who use the app, not a daily call to come back). A run that fails is tried again three times, five minutes apart, the same day, then left to the next (`shortcut:` a process that starts after the hour arms for tomorrow, so a deploy across it skips that day; a row of the last day told would lift it). It lives with the jobs (`jobs/round-push.ts`), since it reads rounds, their entries, the switches and the tokens, on a timer of its own armed as the night's is, the hour read from `matching_config` at each arming and the wait counted to that hour on the day's own clock, so a change of clocks moves neither (`msUntilFinnishHour`, which the night's timer shares). Its time to live ends with the day, or with the quiet hours if they come first (§7).
7. **The quiet hours.** No push of any kind from `push_quiet_from_hour` to `push_quiet_to_hour`, in Helsinki; equal hours mean none. None is sent then, and none sent before waits into them for a phone that was off: every push's time to live ends where the quiet hours begin, and where the Finnish day ends. Nothing is kept to send later: the person sees it all when they open the app (Open 1). An hour outside 0 to 23 in any of the three hour rows is an error, logged, never a timer for an hour there is not.
8. **Expo is a new recipient.** The API sends to Expo's push service (`https://exp.host/--/api/v2/push/send`), which hands each push to Apple's or Google's service. Expo receives, per push: the token, the line, `data`, and when. A token names an installation of the app on a device to Expo, Apple and Google, and nothing about the person to them; the API keeps it only to send. The Expo client is a seam (`lib/push.ts`, `Deps.pusher`): Expo's, a log of what would go (development and test only, as mail's), or none where `PUSH` is off, and a memory double in the tests. An access token for Expo's enhanced push security, once the maintainer switches it on in EAS, is the SecureString `/kuutti/<env>/expo-access-token`, made with the CLI and never a resource (ADR-001), read as `EXPO_ACCESS_TOKEN`; without it the requests go unauthenticated, which Expo accepts until the switch is on. Up to a hundred pushes a request, one request at a time and at least 200 ms apart (Expo takes 600 a second for a project), so a request refused loses its own pushes alone and the tickets of those before it are read; no retry (Open 4). A request Expo refuses for tokens of more than one project (a build of another project, or a token registered by hand) keeps the project most of its tokens are of, deletes the others, and is sent again without them, so one stray token cannot cost the rest of its request every day. A ticket or, read once fifteen minutes later from the process's memory, a receipt that says `DeviceNotRegistered` deletes its token; any other error is logged by its code alone (`shortcut:` a restart loses the receipts it was waiting for; a table of tickets would lift it). `docs/vendors/expo.md` says what Expo receives and keeps; the privacy policy's "Who else handles your data" gains the push in Expo's row and in Apple and Google's, and section 7 its line (the site, `kuutti-ry/kuutti-app-site`, under the version the maintainer gives it, ADR-010 §4). ADR-011 §8 is the model for the reasoning: a vendor gets what it needs to do its one job, and the association names it.
9. **The app asks once, when there is something to tell.** Never at the first start: the home screen offers it once the person's matching is open (the round's way in is there, and every match comes through a round), with the reason in words and two buttons of equal weight, "Allow notifications", which shows the phone's own question, and "No thanks"; either answer is remembered on the phone (the preference `pushAsked`), so the app never offers it again, and both buttons wait while the question is open. The phone's own refusal is remembered by the phone. Everything works without it. On Android the channel `default` is made before the phone is asked (Android 13 shows no question without one) and again at each registration, in the app's language and with no launcher badge. The app registers its token with the app's language as `Accept-Language`, since the API words the pushes in the request's language, one registration at a time, skipping one identical to the last it sent, and never after a sign-out. A tap is acted on once; nothing while signed out, and a sign-out clears the pushes still in the phone's list, so the next person to sign in cannot tap the previous one's. While the app is in front a push is shown as a banner and in the list, with no sound and no badge. A tap on a message's or a match's push opens the conversations, where the new one is first, and a round's today's round; while the app lock is shown, the tap waits for it to open. The app registers again whenever it comes back to the front, so a permission given meanwhile in the phone's own settings, or a token fetch that failed for want of network, is not left to the next start. A sign-out that never reached the API (no network, or the lock's way out offline) also unregisters the phone from Apple's or Google's push, since the server's session, and its token, live on until they expire; the next ticket says the device is gone and its token is deleted. On the web target nothing asks, registers or opens, and the Settings line says that the device cannot show notifications. Settings › Notifications says in words whether the phone lets Kuutti notify: allowed, not allowed with the way to the phone's settings (`Linking.openSettings`, on both platforms), or not asked yet, with a button that shows the phone's question, which on iOS is the only way the phone's settings ever get a Notifications row for Kuutti; it reads the phone again when the app comes back to the front, and a change is announced to a screen reader. No dialog of ours asks. The opening line no longer says that nothing is sent.
10. **The parameters**, version 1 rows of `matching_config` (migration 0082 and the seed's `MATCHING_CONFIG_V1`, held equal by `migrate.test.ts`): `message_push_min_interval_minutes` 10, `push_quiet_from_hour` 22, `push_quiet_to_hour` 8, `round_push_hour` 9, the issue's proposals that the maintainer confirmed on 25/10/2026; and `round_push_idle_days` 7 (migration 0083), his of the same day.
11. **Erasure and export** (ADR-007 §3, §4). Erasure deletes the tokens (§3). The export lists the person's sessions as before, each with whether it receives pushes and, if it does, its platform, language and since when; never the token itself, which names the installation to Expo, Apple and Google and nothing to the person, and which a copy of the export in the wrong hands could send to.

## Consequences

- `expo-notifications` in the app and its config plugin in `app.json`, with the Android notification icon from the monochrome icon (#409's placeholder): the native fingerprint moves for both platforms, every installed development build is orphaned a fourth time since #184 was written, and the maintainer dispatches a development build after the merge. The push needs Expo's credentials for Apple (an APNs key) and Google (an FCM V1 service account key) in EAS, the maintainer's (`docs/vendors/expo.md`). **Android gets no push token at all** until the Firebase project's `google-services.json` is in the native build (`android.googleServicesFile`): until then every Android phone that allows notifications reports a failed token fetch at each start, and adding the file moves the fingerprint again.
- Routes: `PUT /push-token`. Contracts: `PushPlatform`, `PushTokenRequest`, `PushData`; `AccountExport.sessions[].push`. In the API, `ErasureSummary.pushTokens`.
- The parameter `push` (`expo`) in staging's configuration (`infra/envs/staging/main.tf`), so staging and its previews send. Production has none yet and sends nothing: it gets `push=expo` with its access token and enhanced push security on, so that nobody holding a stored token can send to its people's phones. `PUSH` defaults to `log` in development and test and `off` elsewhere.
- Specs: `features/notifications/push.feature` (the notifications slice is not a rules slice, but who may be told of a message is the chat's rule, so its scenarios are specified), and a scenario in `features/identity/erasure.feature`.
- `rules/mobile.md`'s Notifications line, ADR-032 §4, ADR-038 Open 1 and ADR-045 Open 1 are amended to point here.

## Not done, and what would make it needed

- A push for a moderator's decision (ADR-032's `moderation` kind): #312's, when its decisions exist.
- A notice in the app for a match: ADR-038 Open 1's other half.
- Retrying a push Expo refused for the moment: if pushes are seen lost (Open 4).
- Pushes kept through the quiet hours and sent at their end: if the maintainer wants them (Open 1).

## Alternatives considered

- **The token on the account, not the session.** A sign-out on one phone would have to find that phone's token by something it sends; the session is the device already (`session.platform`), and its end is the end of its pushes.
- **Deleting the token in each revoking statement.** Three statements in the identity slice today, and the next one written would forget; a trigger is the database saying it once, as 0055 already says a revoked session's sockets close.
- **The words from the phone (a data-only push the app words itself).** iOS shows nothing for a data-only push while the app is not running, and the words would still have to be the phone's language; the server has it from the registration.
- **A title as well as a line.** The phone already shows the app's name above the line; a title "Kuutti" says it twice.
- **The interval and the receipts in tables.** One box (TD-3); what a restart forgets is a push sent early or a dead token kept until its next refusal.
- **The conversation in the push** (as written first, and the 17/10 plan's): a tap would open the conversation itself, but the one id on both phones pairs them for the vendors. A per-person id (the recipient's participant row) would keep the direct tap and cost a mapping in the list and the tap; the conversations list is one tap from it.
- **Waiting for the push in the send's answer** (as written first): simpler to test, but the answer's time then says whether anybody was pushed.
- **Asking at the first conversation**, as the 17/10 plan had it for the message alone. With the round's push in the same change the first use is the first round, and every match comes through one.

## Open, the maintainer's

1. Nothing pushed in the quiet hours is sent later; the person sees it on opening the app.
2. A push makes the phone's default sound, as the person's phone settings allow; in front, the app shows it silently.
3. While the app lock is on (ADR-045 Open 1), a push says what it says without the lock: its words name nobody and nothing, so the lock has nothing to take out.
4. No retry of a push Expo refused for a moment (a 429, a 5xx, a timeout).
5. The match push goes whether or not the person is in the app, since nothing else tells them.
