# ADR-015: The pond gate: admission, the pool, and what a person is told

- Status: proposed; amended by §10 on 2026-10-08 (#147: admission by contests, intent in the pool, the cadence of a change) (the rules are built as written here; the points under "For the maintainer to confirm" are his, and the status changes when he has answered them)
- Date: 2026-09-28
- Follows: TD-10 (pond gate and admission), TD-11 (rounds for admitted people with a complete profile), TD-13 (the imbalanced pond; nobody is un-admitted), TD-14 (gender-neutral rules, the three genders, `seeks` is article 9 data), TD-7 (states, erasure), TD-16 (completeness), CLAUDE.md rules 5, 6 and 7 and Product constraints, `.claude/rules/layout.md`, ADR-007 §4 (a new table brings its erasure statement and its export section), ADR-009 §4 and §6 (completeness, the card's age), ADR-010 §6 (it is `complete`, never `state`, that matching reads), ADR-013 (the counter's step rule); issue #94

## Context

M3 ends with a person who has a finished profile and a pond, and a public counter that says how the pond fills. M4 begins with the question that person asks next: when does matching open for me? TD-10 answers per person, not per pond: when the people who could be shown to them number K. TD-10 and TD-13 add admission: where one gender is the larger group, its newcomers wait, visibly. TD-14 asks that none of it names a gender.

The round builder (#95) and everything after it stand on two things decided here: who is in whose pool, and who is inside the pond at all.

What makes the gate harder than the counter is that the person chooses what is counted. The counter's figures are of a pond; a pool is of a pond as one person's preferences cut it, and the person can change those preferences, their gender and their pond whenever they like. A figure that answers every question put to it says what one other person seeks: with a window of one year of birth, "one more is needed" against "two more are needed" is that person's `seeks`. The security review of this change showed it by doing it, and §6, §7 and §9 are what came of it.

## Decision

1. **One function says who can be shown to whom, and it is symmetric.** `inEachOthersPool(a, b)` in `apps/api/src/matching/pool.ts`: each one's gender is among those the other seeks, and each one's age is inside the other's window. It is the hard filters of onboarding in both directions (rule 7); blocks and deal-breakers join in the same function with #87, so no caller can show somebody a filter excludes. A property test holds the symmetry for any two people. The age is the card's (`ageInYears`), so a card never shows an age a window excludes; which day of the birth month the age turns on is one function's business (noted on #47).
2. **A pool is within one pond.** The parent ponds exist for widening (TD-13 §6), which is a later decision; today a pond's people are those who chose it.
3. **Who is counted.** A person has a gate when their account is `active` or `shadow_banned`, their identity is in good standing, their profile is complete (#47) and onboarding is (ADR-010 §6: gender, both hard rows, the pond, and terms and privacy in their current wording; a consent for an older wording reads as none). A person is in others' pools when, beside that, they are let into the pond and `active`: a shadow-banned person has a gate like anybody, stands in the line like anybody and is in nobody's pool, so that the ban shows to nobody and least of all to them (TD-7). Paused, suspended, banned and deleted accounts are in no pool and have no gate. Activity within ninety days (TD-7) joins when the account has a `last_active_at`, with the round builder.
4. **Admission keeps the larger group to its share, and names no group.** Two groups exist for the rule: people who seek another gender than their own, by their own gender. Everybody else, people who seek their own gender too and non-binary people, is let in at once (TD-13, TD-14). Of the two groups, the smaller one, and either while they are equal, is always let in; the larger one while it is at most `majority_share_max` of both. Within a group the order is the order of registration; between the groups the earlier registration goes first, so a newcomer of the smaller group opens the way for those who waited. The share is counted over the people of the two groups who are let in, not over the whole pond: those who wait with nobody do not make room for anybody.
5. **No count lets anybody out, and no gate closes.** `admitted_at` and `opened_at` are written once by the count. A pond whose ratio drifts is throttled by the impression cap of the rounds (TD-13 §2), and a pool that shrinks below K makes shorter rounds (TD-11), never a closed gate: a person who had rounds yesterday and has none today without having done anything is the experience this product exists to avoid. What takes an admission back is the person's own doing, and only two things: another pond (§8) and joining a group that waits (§9).
6. **What a person is told is said in steps, and nothing finer is kept.** The size of the pool is said rounded down to whole steps (`sayPool`), the first figure like every later one, and the place in the line as the end of its step, "among the next ten" (`sayPlace`). The step is the counter's `waitlist_k`, floor ten. On top of the rounding the pool's figure follows slowly: it moves when the pool is a whole step away from what was said, so a pool that goes back and forth over a ten does not show each crossing. What was said stays with the row through an emptying (§8, §9) and through a wait in the line: otherwise a person would leave their pond and come back on one day, have a first figure every night, and see every crossing after all. The step can be changed after a figure was said under another one. A figure is then read in the step of today first, rounded down (`saidInStep`, and `sayPlace` for a place), by the route before the next count and by the count, which follows from there; the export shows the row as it was written until then. A raise says nothing anew of the pool: the figure read that way is a function of what was said, and the pool is within a step of it while the new step is a multiple of the old, so it stands until the pool moves. Lowered again, the figure of the coarser step stands until the pool is a step away from it, what was said and not the pool a person later, as the counter keeps its figures through a raise (ADR-013 §6). The app says "about" and "among", which is what is true. The exact pool and the exact place exist in memory while a count runs; the row holds what is said (`pool_said`, `place_said`), so the route, the export and a copy of the table all say the same and none of them more. The gate itself opens at exactly K: that is the product (TD-10).
7. **Counted at night, and once when a person first asks.** `countGates` runs with the nightly jobs for every pond, under an advisory lock of its own; counting twice changes nothing, no date either: a row that would say what it says already is not written, and `counted_at` is when a count last wrote it. A person who is complete and was never counted is counted alone when they first ask (`gateOf`), so nobody waits for the night to learn where they stand for the first time; nobody else's row is written then, so asking moves nobody's figure. Because only one row is written, nothing in it rests on what is not written: the person is let in then and there only when that needs nobody else's admission (`admitsAlone`: they wait with nobody, or they are the first of their group who is not let in and the group may enter as the written admissions stand), and their pool is of the people whose admission is written. A place in the line is said as the written admissions stand, too (`placeAlone`): behind everybody of the group who is not let in, whoever the count of everybody would let in before them. It can say "among the next twenty" where the night finds "among the next ten", never the other way round. The pond is read for the asker only while they live in it, and the statement names them (rule 6). Whoever would be let in only together with others is told that the night will say; otherwise a person who registered later could ask first, be let in on the strength of admissions that then never come about, and stand inside before the one who came first. It is once in an account's life: every later change, of preferences, of gender or of pond, shows in what the person is told after the next count and never on asking, so nobody can put one question after another to the figure. A first ask that finds a count running does not wait for it and is told that the night will say; of several first asks at once, one counts. The count is O(n²) in the people of a pond, in memory: at the five thousand people the project plans for it is some tens of millions of comparisons a night, a third of a second for one person, and no index or queue is built for a scale that is not coming.
8. **The row is the person's.** `gate(account_id, pond_id, admitted_at, place_said, pool_said, opened_at, counted_at)`, with checks that a place is at least one, that somebody let in has no place in the line, and that a gate is open only for somebody let in. It goes with the account at erasure (TD-7), in the erasure's transaction, and it is in the export as it is held (ADR-007 §4). When the person chooses another pond the row is emptied in the statement that changes the pond: a place is a place in one pond, and nobody carries an admission along. Emptied, not removed, so that the first ask stays the first (§7). The count locks every account that may have a row before it reads anything about it (`FOR SHARE`, in a statement of its own), and whoever changes a declaration, a pond or erases an account takes the account's lock first: a change finishes before the count reads, or waits until it has written. Read without the lock, a declaration taken back while the count ran would have had its emptied row written over with what the old declaration earned. The accounts are locked before any row of the gate is touched, so a count and a writer cannot wait for each other. A first ask locks the asker's account the same way, and writes a row only for an account it finds live in that pond at the moment of writing. A change of pond that runs while a count writes the person's first row can miss that row; it is then of a pond the person left, reads as "the night will say", and the next count empties it into the pond the person lives in.
9. **Admission is decided anew for a person who joins a group that waits.** A change of gender or of whom one seeks that brings a person into one of the two groups of §4, from outside them or from the other one, empties the row in the transaction that writes the change (`admissionAnew`). Without it a person of the larger group declares that they seek their own gender too, is let in at once, takes the declaration back, and stands inside: past everybody who waited, and counted into the share they wait behind. Leaving such a group takes nothing back, nor does a change within it (somebody who seeks one gender more, another window of ages). At the next count the person stands in the line by their registration like everybody of the group. What empties the row, so that it can be judged: any change of pond, there and back included; taking one's own gender out of whom one seeks; a change of gender to woman or man while one does not seek that gender. What does not: the same preferences saved again, another window of ages, one more gender sought, one's own gender added, becoming non-binary. It names the caller's row only, so it cannot be turned on anybody else.
10. **The count lives with the jobs, the rules with the slices.** The count reads accounts, consents, profiles, photos, preferences and ponds, so it is in `apps/api/src/jobs/pond-gate.ts`, like the counter's (ADR-013). The rules are pure functions of the slices they belong to: `inEachOthersPool` of matching, `admit`, `admitsAlone`, `placeAlone`, `sayPool`, `sayPlace` and `joinsAGroupThatWaits` of the pond, `completeness` of the profile. The pond's route is handed the reader by the app, so the pond slice imports nothing from the jobs. Matching and identity call the pond's `admissionAnew` where they write `seeks` and gender; the pond imports from neither.
11. **Nothing of it is logged or measured by person.** The night's log line has how many ponds, people, admissions and openings, never who, where or what anybody seeks. The line of a first ask says that one happened and nothing of its outcome: beside the request's line, which names the account, "one waits" would say that this person does not seek their own gender. No research event is added: the taxonomy is agreed with the research partner (TD-5), and the gate is not in it yet.

10. **Amendment of 2026-10-08 (#147, the field sheet's gender model, ADR-019).** Three changes. *Intent is a hard filter both ways:* `inEachOthersPool` also asks that the two want the same thing, or that one of them is open to either (`intentsFit`); the gate's count reads the intent from the profile, and a person without one is not done. *Admission is by contest, not by label.* §4's two groups were people who seek another gender than their own, by their own gender, and that let a person who declared non-binary and sought women past the men's line, and a person who added their own gender to whom they seek past everybody (§9's exploit). Now a person competes for every gender they seek but their own (`contestsOf`; seeking one's own gender is no contest), and each contest is kept to its ratio: the people competing for a gender may be at most `majority_share_max / (1 - majority_share_max)` times the people of that gender who seek somebody else (0.6 is three to two, the same line as before for the two groups of §4), or at most as many, whichever lets more in; an empty pond has room for the first of anybody. A person whose every contest has room is let in, in the order of registration, and the line is read again from its head after each one; a person who competes for nobody is let in at once. The place in the line is among those who compete alike. `admitsAlone` and `placeAlone` read the same way as the recorded admissions stand. *A change is possible once in the cadence.* A change of gender, or of whom one seeks, from an earlier answer is refused for `change_cadence_days` (30, a row of `matching_config` since version 1, migration 0022) after the last one, with the date in the onboarding status (`nextChange`) and never a reason or a hostile word: somebody transitioning must not hit a wall. The first answer, the same answer again and the window of ages are free; the times live on the account (`gender_changed_at`, `seeks_changed_at`, migration 0021), which a withdrawn special-category consent does not clear; and an answer given after a withdrawal counts as a change while a change is on record, so that path is no way round (a person who changed whom they seek and then withdrew the consent waits out the cadence before answering again, which is the one cost of keeping the lever shut). A withdrawal with no consent to withdraw changes nothing. The gate's count, like the status, reads the seek answer only with that consent in its current wording. Migration 0022, custom, carries one row: `INSERT INTO "matching_config" ("version", "key", "value", "created_by") VALUES (1, 'change_cadence_days', '30'::jsonb, 'migration:0022') ON CONFLICT ("key", "version") DO NOTHING;`. §9 reads with contests: what empties the row is joining a contest one was not in, from none or from another; leaving one, seeking one's own gender too or no longer, a change of gender that keeps the contests, another window of ages, take nothing back. Nothing is derived from whom one seeks, ever, and a test holds the apps and the catalogue to it. Point 5 of "For the maintainer to confirm" stands in its new shape; points 1 and 2 are answered by the sheet.

## Parameters

`gate_k` = 30 and `majority_share_max` = 0.6 are rows of `matching_config` since version 1, and `change_cadence_days` = 30 since #147 (migration 0022). The step is `waitlist_k` (10, migration 0017). The step is raised only to a multiple of the step in force (10 to 20, never to 15): a figure read in a step that is no multiple of the one it was said under can be a whole new step from the pool, and the next count then says one crossing anew (§6).

## For the maintainer to confirm

These are decided here so that the code could be written, and each is one function or one line to change:

1. **The share is kept between the two groups only** (§4), not over the pond's whole population, and the smaller group is always let in. At small numbers the share can stand above sixty per cent (two of three).
2. **What a person is told is said in tens** (§6): "about 20 more people are needed", "you are among the next 10", where TD-10 says "N more" and a place. The exact figures are the alternative, and with them what is described under Context.
3. **No gate closes again** (§5).
4. **A person is counted once when they first ask, and the night says everything after** (§7), where TD-10 names the nightly job only. After a change of pond a person reads "where you stand is counted tonight" until the next count.
5. **Joining a group that waits takes the admission back** (§9), and an open gate with it, until a count lets the person in again. In a pond with room that is the next night. In a pond at its share it is not: a person who was inside, adds their own gender to whom they seek by a slip and takes it out again within the minute, stands in the line, for as long as the line takes. TD-13's "nobody is un-admitted" is read as being about the pond's drift, not about what a person changes about themselves; where the log and this file differ, the log wins, so this point and points 2 and 4 want an answer before the change is merged. A milder rule is possible: the row records in which group the admission was given, and only joining another group than that empties it. It costs a column that says something `seeks` says, and a warning in the app before such a change is saved would go with either.
6. **How often a person's own changes may move their figure.** Once a night, as built. See "What stays possible": a limit would be a row of `matching_config`, for instance that the figure follows a change of the hard preferences only after some days.
7. **Shadow-banned people stand in the line and count toward the share** (§3), so a person can wait behind somebody nobody sees. It keeps the ban from showing; the cost is a place.
8. **Activity within ninety days is not asked yet** (§3): the account has no `last_active_at`.
9. **The app says nothing about a round when the gate is open**, only that matching is open: the rounds are #95.

## Consequences

- The round builder (#95) takes its candidates through `inEachOthersPool` and builds for people whose gate is open. The gate's count runs before it in the night.
- The app reads the gate when the home screen comes to the front and when the app comes back to the foreground, and shows what it knew until the answer is there: the screen stays mounted while the person adds photos on another one, and while the night's count runs.
- The synthetic population (#73) has no photos yet, so nobody in it has a complete profile and none of its people has a gate. The gate is seen locally with the personas once they have photos, and in the tests, which make finished profiles directly.
- The referral that moves a person up the line (#107) needs an order that is not only the registration's: a column beside `registered_at`, with that issue. It will also need §9 and the Product constraints read again.
- Erasure's table (rules/db.md) has one more row that goes: the place at the gate.

## What stays possible

Said in tens, once a night, a figure still answers questions, slowly. What a person can learn, as far as the two security reviews of this change could find:

- When the pool crosses K the gate opens. That is the product. While `gate_k` is a multiple of the step it is one of the tens; if it is ever set between them it is one threshold more.
- A person whose figure moves learns that their pool crossed a ten, or came a whole step away from what was said. When they know that one person came to the pond that night and nobody else, they learn that this person is in their pool. It is one night in about ten, and it is the same for the public counter (ADR-013).
- A question about somebody who is there already: two windows of ages that differ by that person's year of birth, asked on two nights. About one such pair in ten stands at the edge of a ten, so it takes some twenty nights to a yes and about six weeks to a fairly sure no, blurred by everybody else who comes and goes. Each question costs a night. Point 6 above is about making it slower.
- People who act together are faster. Nine acquaintances of the right age bring a pool to the edge of a ten on purpose, and the tenth person's answer is then had in one night. The same in the line: acquaintances who registered earlier and join or leave it place a watcher at the tenth place.
- A shadow ban shows to a friend who sets out to find it: nine others and the banned person are said as none, nine others and somebody who is seen as ten. It takes the same staging as the point before.
- Admission itself is exact. The person at the head of the line learns from being let in that somebody of the other group came or one of their own left, and a person asking for the first time learns from "counted tonight" against a place in the line whether they would pass somebody.
- The first ask is a question at an hour of the asker's choosing, once per account; an erased account can be followed by a new one after the cooldown of thirty days.
- Two people who compare their figures learn in which tens their pools are.
- A person in the line who knows that an acquaintance of their gender, registered before them, came to the pond learns from a place that moved from "the next ten" to "the next twenty" that the acquaintance waits with them, which is to say does not seek their own gender. Nine times in ten the place does not move.
