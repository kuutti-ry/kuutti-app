# Security checklist

OWASP Top 10 (2025) mapped to this codebase. A diff that touches identity, auth, uploads, signed URLs, research events, or infrastructure is checked against every line here before it opens as a pull request. The `security-reviewer` agent in `.claude/agents/` runs this list; humans use it the same way.

The four surfaces that matter most, in order: the OIDC exchange with Telia, the hetu HMAC, session token issuance and validation, signed URL issuance for media. Everything else is CRUD with rate limits.

## Broken access control

- [ ] Every query that touches user data is scoped by the session's `account_id` inside the query, never by a check after the fetch.
- [x] Admin routes check role (moderator / admin / researcher) server-side, and the admin allowlist is by `hetu_hmac`. (#49: `requireAdmin` in `apps/api/src/lib/admin-middleware.ts` re-reads `moderator_roles` on every request; the row is keyed by the identity the `hetu_hmac` resolves to.)
- [x] No endpoint returns another user's data beyond what a profile card shows; no bulk export endpoint exists. (#52: another account's photo is issued a URL only against a `card_shown` row the card route writes for the viewer, one photo at a time and counted against the day's budget in the same statement, and never in the full size, which the app asks for only of one's own (ADR-056 §3), `findVisiblePhoto` in `apps/api/src/media/repo.ts`; `/account/export` is one person's own data, #51, as one zip with the photos inside, at most `matching_config exports_per_day` per Finnish day counted by `recordExport` in `apps/api/src/identity/repo.ts`, #237. The card: `buildCard` in `apps/api/src/profile/card.ts` serves another account's card only when the subject is `active` and complete, writes the shown records and counts the card in `recordCardServed`, and no route takes a subject id in M3, ADR-009 §6; today's round (`GET /rounds/today`, #96, ADR-034) serves only the caller's own round's cards, each through the card builder, and no round route takes another person's id.)
- [x] An account that takes in other people faster than a person does is taken out of everyone else's sight for a moderator to judge, without its answers changing (#86, ADR-041, on ADR-033's seam): URLs of other people's photos in the card and full sizes from the fetch log (`othersPhotoFetchesSince`, never a thumb, the person's own or a conversation's photo), different people's cards from `card_shown` (`cardsServedSince`, off while the round bounds them) and the reads of today's round, which live in memory only (`roundReadRule`); each check is scoped by the account in its query, runs after what its request wrote has committed and before it answers, is measured without the lock first with the person's standing, so that only the request that applies a ban takes `lockAccountForSanction` and a ban shows in no later answer's time, applies one shadow ban by nobody for the reason `scraping` with counts and times only, and writes a line with the rule and its count, never a person (`apps/api/src/safety/automatic.ts`, `velocity.test.ts` interleaves two checks at the threshold, the PII test sends the fixtures through both rules).
- [x] Hard filters are enforced in the round builder, in both directions. (#95, ADR-035 §2: `inEachOthersPool` decides every card, in `roundOrder` and `likedYouPlaces`, over the hard rows and blocks `subjectsOfPond` reads as the gate's count does; asserted both ways over every card of a 300-person synthetic population in `jobs/round-builder.test.ts` and by property tests in `matching/round-order.test.ts`.)
- [x] A block holds both ways wherever one person is shown to another, and only somebody whose card the caller was shown can be blocked or reported (#84, ADR-030): `blocked_between` (migration 0051) in `findCardSubject` (`apps/api/src/profile/repo.ts`) and `findVisiblePhoto` (`apps/api/src/media/repo.ts`), the blocks in `inEachOthersPool` and the gate's count; `POST /blocks` and `POST /reports` act only on a proof of contact, a `card_shown` row of the caller's (`wasShown`) or a block that stands between the two accounts it was set between, in either direction (`blockStandsBetween`: both accounts registered no later than the block), answer the same 404 for never shown, oneself, nobody and a later account of somebody blocked, and the same 204 whatever stood before; the day's cap, an open report and a standing block answer before the card is asked about, so neither side of a block learns from either route whether the other is still there, and an account id passed on links no later account to an earlier one (the bits that remain are ADR-030 §6's, Open 15); the card's shown record and a photo's fetch-log row ask for a block in the statement that writes them; `blocked_between` has a SQL-standard body, so Postgres tracks the tables it reads; a report's note reaches no log line, error detail or event, and the logger redacts `note` by name; the export lists the person's own reports and blocks, never the other's id, and nothing about them (`apps/api/src/safety/`, `no-signal.test.ts` holds who reads the tables).

- [x] A stored contact reaches only its owner and the person they share it with, in that conversation, while the share stands (#103, ADR-031 §11): every contacts route reads and writes the caller's own pieces by the session's account, and the share routes act through the caller's own participant row after §3's hold of the account, lock of the conversation and fresh read, so a block, a close, a sanction or an erasure that committed meanwhile refuses a share; ## Security misconfiguration

- [x] CORS: product API routes refuse browser origins; only the admin SPA, the waitlist site, and a pull-request preview's own web origin are allowlisted. (#52: `corsAllowlist` answers only the configured origins and production refuses http ones, `apps/api/src/lib/config.ts`; `app.test.ts` asks every route with an unknown browser origin, direct and preflight, and finds no Access-Control-Allow-Origin. #313, ADR-025 §10: staging's allowlist is exactly the panel's origin, `https://admin.staging.kuutti.app`, in `/kuutti/staging/cors-allowed-origins` from `infra/envs/staging`; the panel's previews under `/pr-<n>/` share that origin and need no entry of their own, and `build.yml` asks the API's CORS for that origin after each deploy of the panel.)
- [ ] No debug or introspection routes outside `NODE_ENV=development`.
- [ ] Deployed configuration comes only from SSM Parameter Store via the instance role; `.env` is local-only.
- [x] Mail leaves only through SES, from `noreply@kuutti.app` (#385, ADR-043 §4 and §5): the instance role may call `ses:SendEmail` on the identity `kuutti.app` alone, under the condition `ses:FromAddress`, and staging's only to its testers, under the condition `ses:Recipients` (`MAIL_TESTERS`, ADR-043 §6: anybody can sign in there as Telia's public test persons, and staging shares production's account, reputation and suppression list); the account-level suppression list holds bounces and complaints; `MAIL=log`, which writes a message and its code to the log, is refused at boot outside development and test (`apps/api/src/lib/config.ts`), so the parameter store's `mail` is `ses` or absent, never `log`. (Ticked 2026-10-25, #385: the bootstrap's identity applied, a code from staging passed DKIM, SPF and DMARC, and two sends there to addresses the list did not match yet were answered 503.)
- [x] A fresh box routes nothing public to Dokploy before its owner exists (#437, ADR-048): first boot writes Dokploy's `dokploy.yml` with the service alone, so Dokploy adds no `dokploy.docker.localhost` router, and no control-plane router; `dokploy-setup.sh` writes those once the owner's API key exists and requires `INSTANCE_ID` to do it. Verified at production's first boot on 2026-10-24: before the owner existed, `dokploy.docker.localhost`, `dokploy.kuutti.app`, the old `dokploy.prod.kuutti.app`, `api.kuutti.app` and a request by the bare address (Host: the Elastic IP) all answered 404 on 80 and 443, and 3000 and 8080 were closed (#25).
- [ ] CloudFront is the only public entry; nothing listens on plain HTTP. (ADR-005: the distribution fronts the API once `media_enabled` is set; the box still admits 443 from anywhere because Dokploy and the previews are served there directly, and 80 for the ACME challenge. `cloudfront_only_ingress` is the switch; the follow-up that lets it flip is in ADR-005.)
- [x] The panel's host serves from a private bucket that only its distribution reads, and every answer carries the panel's headers (#313, ADR-025, `infra/modules/admin`): origin access control signs every request, public access is blocked and the bucket owner enforced; the response-headers policy sends a CSP of `default-src 'none'` with `script-src`, `style-src` and `font-src 'self'` (Saiga, from the bucket's `fonts/`, ADR-052), the API's origin alone in `connect-src` and with `'self'` in `img-src`, and `worker-src`, `base-uri`, `form-action` and `frame-ancestors 'none'`, with HSTS (preload), `nosniff`, `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, a closed `Permissions-Policy`, COOP and CORP `same-origin` and `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow`, and `build.yml` reads them back after each deploy of the panel. The root is written by the `staging` environment's role; the plan role may put and delete `pr-*` objects and nothing else (`admin-previews`, `infra/bootstrap`), so a pull request can replace another's panel preview, never the root. A preview runs on the panel's origin and can read a staging staff session in the tab it is opened in: the trust is write access (ADR-004 §3), and staging holds invented people only (ADR-018).

## Cryptographic failures

- [ ] HMAC-SHA256 with the permanent SSM key is used for the hetu and for nothing else.
- [x] The e-mail's confirmation code (#385, ADR-043 §3) is six digits from the operating system's generator (`randomInt`), stored only as the SHA-256 of the account id and the code (`email_code`), compared in constant time, never an HMAC with the hetu key. It works for `matching_config email_code_minutes` (30) and `email_code_attempts` (3) wrong tries, and an account is sent at most `email_codes_per_day` (5) a Finnish day, counted under the account row's lock (`apps/api/src/identity/email.ts`): fifteen guesses a day at a million codes, from the account's own session.
- [x] An invitation's code (#107, ADR-044 §2 and §6) is ten characters of Crockford's base32, fifty bits from the operating system's generator drawn without modulo bias, stored only as the SHA-256 of the normalised code (`invitation.code_hash`), never an HMAC with the hetu key, and shown once, in the answer that made it: it reaches no log line, error detail, event or export. Redeeming checks the caller before it looks the code up, so it answers nothing about a code to somebody who could not use one; it answers one 404 for a code unknown, used or one's own (ADR-027 §3's precedent), runs under the caller's account row's lock, and a person may try `matching_config invitation_tries_per_day` (5) codes a Finnish day, counted by the route's limiter, whose key is logged nowhere (`apps/api/src/rewards/`).
- [x] The app lock's passcode (#418, ADR-045 §3) is six digits stored only as `{ salt, hash }`, the SHA-256 of a salt of its own and the digits, never the digits, in this phone's keychain or keystore as `WHEN_UNLOCKED_THIS_DEVICE_ONLY`, written in place so a failed write never turns it off, compared in constant time and never an HMAC with the hetu key. It is not stretched, on purpose: a million codes are tried in seconds whatever stretching a phone can afford at each unlock, so the guard is the keychain, which holds the session's tokens as well, and five wrong tries counted before the check (`apps/mobile/src/features/identity/lock/passcode.ts`, `lock/store.ts`; tested). The salt is ADR-027 §5's native UUID until `expo-crypto` at #184.
- [ ] Session tokens are random 256-bit values (a refresh token two of them, ADR-059), stored hashed, compared in constant time.
- [ ] The hetu HMAC key and the Telia signing key are never in the database, the repo, logs, or the Dokploy control plane.
- [ ] TLS terminates at CloudFront; internal hops stay inside the VPC. (ADR-005: viewer TLS at CloudFront after the cutover; the hop to the box is HTTPS over the public address to `origin.api.<env>`, not inside the VPC, until the ingress switch above flips.)

## Injection

- [ ] Drizzle query builder only. Any raw `sql` template carries a reviewer comment explaining why.
- [ ] All input passes through a zod schema from `packages/schema` at the route boundary, including query strings, headers used for logic, and WebSocket messages. (#349: every frame of the chat's socket, both ways, through `packages/schema/src/chat-frames.ts`, and the database's notifications through the hub's own schema; the row stays open for the routes as a whole.)
- [x] Uploaded images are re-encoded by sharp with `limitInputPixels`; the original bytes are never stored or served. (#48: `apps/api/src/media/pipeline.ts`; the pixel cap is applied to the file header before the decoder, the route tests read the stored bytes back. #350: a photo sent in a conversation goes through the same `processPhoto`, behind the session guard and its own 10 MB cap, and the check reads it before anything is stored; `apps/api/src/chat/photos.test.ts` reads all three stored variants back, each WebP without the fixture's EXIF and GPS.)
- [x] The translator (`pnpm i18n:translate`) sends only message text (and approved translations as examples), the glossary and the tone guide to the Claude API; its output is validated and stays machine text until a native review (#55).

## Insecure design

- [ ] The change cites the TD entry or ADR it follows, or proposes a new ADR in `docs/adr/`.
- [ ] It does not add a compatibility score, desirability signal, infinite feed, real-time engagement push, or purchasable visibility (see the Product constraints section of `CLAUDE.md`).
- [ ] It does not widen what is stored about identity: no hetu, no legal sex, no full date of birth, no name.

## Authentication failures

- [ ] OIDC callback verifies `state`, `nonce`, the ID token signature against the issuer JWKS, `iss`, `aud`, `exp`, and the `acr` expected for FTN.
- [x] A login attempt's state works once (#324, ADR-026 §8): the callback claims the attempt in the statement that finds it, before the token call; a replay, concurrent or later, is `auth_state_mismatch` without a broker call, and a staff attempt's replay lands on the panel's page (#250); a refusal or a broker failure spends it (`apps/api/src/identity/login.ts`, `repo.ts` `claimAuthRequest`, `findAuthRequestPlatform`).
- [x] The one-time code works only with its attempt's verifier (#186, ADR-027): `/auth/start` requires the SHA-256 of the app's verifier and stores it with the attempt; `/auth/exchange` spends the code in one statement only with the matching verifier, and a wrong one leaves it unspent and answers `auth_code_used`, as an unknown, used or expired code does (`apps/api/src/identity/repo.ts` `consumeAppCode`, `routes.test.ts`). The staff panel's code is not bound: it returns to the panel's own https origin, which only an Android 11-and-older chooser can offer to another app, accepted for now (ADR-027 §4).
- [ ] Telia broker keys are fetched from discovery and rotate on their schedule; nothing is pinned.
- [ ] The app never holds a client secret; the exchange is backend-driven and the app receives a one-time code by deep link that only the device holding the attempt's verifier can exchange.
- [x] Sessions expire; a device change re-authenticates through the bank; admin sessions are 8 hours with no refresh token. (#35 for the product sessions; #49: `admin_session`, `ADMIN_SESSION_TTL_MS`, no refresh route, tested.) A phone's session ends ninety days after its last refresh and never later than a year after its bank login, both enforced in the statement that rotates the tokens and read from the row's own `created_at`, and the deviation from NIST SP 800-63B's AAL2 timeouts is written down (#472, ADR-058: `REFRESH_TOKEN_TTL_MS`, `SESSION_CEILING_MS`, `rotateSession`; the boundaries tested to the millisecond). Any earlier refresh token of a session presented again ends the session for every holder, however many refreshes back it is: the refresh token carries the session's own secret, fixed for its life, whose SHA-256 the row keeps (`family_hash`), so the owner's next refresh ends a lifted session however often the taker refreshed, and a made-up token needs that secret, which only a holder of a genuine token of the session knows (#490, ADR-059: `refreshSession`, `findSessionByFamilyHash`; a token from before two refreshes tested).
- [ ] Re-registration honours identity standing: banned and suspended are refused, cooldown is enforced, nothing is restored.
- [x] A sanction ends what it says at once and leaves nothing to come back (#307, ADR-024): a suspension or a ban revokes every product session of the account and every panel session of the identity in the transaction that sets it; `insertSession` takes the account row's share lock and asks for the guard's live states and an `ok` standing, so a login racing a sanction never leaves a session that a lift would revive; a sanctioned or paused owner's photos are refused to others in `findVisiblePhoto`; a ban, its lift and its review are behind an admin-only guard on paths of their own; staff's note reaches no log line, error detail, event or export.
- [x] The app's lock gates the screens of a signed-in phone, never the session (#418, ADR-045): off until the person turns it on; at a cold start, after the person's grace time in the background and on a clock set back, the lock screen lies over everything (on iOS in a view of the window's own, above a presented picker or share sheet; on Android in a modal), and a cover hides the app in the switcher; the phone's face or fingerprint with no fallback to the phone's own code (`disableDeviceFallback`), Android's weak class counting; five wrong passcodes in a row, counted before the check and kept across restarts, sign the phone out, spent tries are never checked again, and the sign-out waits for the API five seconds at most; a lock that cannot be read stays locked with the bank as the only way out; the bank is always offered, and drops this phone's session as Log out does; any sign-out removes the lock, and nothing of it reaches the API (`apps/mobile/src/features/identity/lock/LockProvider.tsx`, `biometrics.ts`, `store.ts`; tested with the module mocked; the face and the finger are checked by hand on phones once the native build is installed, `docs/runbooks/devices.md`).
