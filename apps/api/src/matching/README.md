# matching

The queue, filters, likes, budgets, recycling. TD-11, TD-12, TD-14, ADR-062.

#94 (ADR-015): `pool.ts` is who can be shown to whom, `inEachOthersPool`: every hard filter in both directions, symmetric by construction. The queue's builder (#95, #502) draws candidates through it. A block of either side excludes there (#84, ADR-030 §2): each person carries the identities they blocked. So does a deal-breaker of either side (#87, ADR-015 §15): each person carries their own answers and their deal-breakers, and one on a field its holder has not answered is paused. One on height (#508) compares the two people's answers: `accepts` reads the holder's own beside the other's. `deal-breakers.ts` (#149) stores the deal-breakers as hard rows on the registry's whitelist, each only on a field the person answered themselves, and reads the rows back (`dealBreakersFrom`) for the person and for the queue's builder alike. `soft.ts` (#87, ADR-019) is `softMatches`, the count of soft fields on which two people are similar from both sides: the order of new people, never a filter, and nothing else reads it.

#96 (ADR-034): `rounds.ts` is the queue and the day's log of it: the two tables' statements, `addPlaces` (the queue's one writer, #502), the read through the card builder `app.ts` hands in (`CardOf`), the seen report and the decision (a like, a pass, a fish or a skip, changeable until the Finnish day ends, under the round row's lock), erasure's and the export's halves, the night's `pruneRounds` (a year, ADR-062 §13), and the research events (`profile_viewed`, `like`, `pass`, and `match` since #100). Every write asks the block and the candidate's state in the statement that writes (ADR-030 §2). `round-routes.ts` is the app's routes, the day in the path of each write.

#95 (ADR-035), #502 (ADR-062 §2): `round-order.ts` is the queue's order past the likes waiting. `roundOrder` is new people: everybody in each other's pool both ways, minus whom a hidden field keeps apart (`hiddenByField`, ADR-019 §5), the nearest band first, then the most soft matches (#514, ADR-061 §6), ties in an order of the day's and the person's. `comingBackOrder` is people seen before. An entry keeps the band it was written with (`round_entries.distance`, ADR-061 §5), and a move works on the next read, as every change of filters does. The builder itself reads across slices and lives with the jobs (`jobs/round-builder.ts`): who comes next (`fillQueue`: the ladder's clocks, the conversations, the inbox stop) and whether two people fit now (`fitsNow`), handed into `round-routes.ts` by `app.ts` (`Queue`).

#98's budget half (ADR-036): `decide` counts the day's likes under the round's lock, `like_budget_balanced` for everybody; a like back on a `liked_you` or `fish` card counts like any other (#503), and a pass or a skip is never counted; over the budget, 429 `like_budget_exceeded` with the wait until midnight.

#97 (ADR-037): `likedYouPlaces` is the queue's first places, the people whose like waits on the person in the order the builder reads them (the oldest like first), however many, each one somebody the person may meet. The builder reads the waiting likes from the round record itself (`waitingOn` in `jobs/round-builder.ts`): likes of ended days within `like_expiry_days`, not answered by a like or a pass. A shadow-banned person's queue holds none of these places (ADR-037 §7).

#100 (ADR-038): `decide` makes a match of a like meeting a standing like, under a lock on the pair, opening the conversation through the chat slice's `openConversation` (handed in by `app.ts` as `Matches`), and answers with it; a like that is part of a match is final (409 `match_made`). After the commit, `Matches.made` hands the match to the push, which tells the person whose earlier like it met (#102, ADR-055 §5). A second match of the same two is a new conversation (ADR-062 §7), so `Matches.between` names one that is open or was opened this Finnish day.

#378 (ADR-039): a fish is a like with a mark (`round_entries.fish`), chosen as `fish` on a card and read back so; `decide` counts the person's fish of the last `fish_window_days` under the round's lock (429 `fish_budget_exceeded`), and on a `liked_you` or `fish` card it is a like. The builder puts the waiting fish ahead of the waiting likes, labelled `fish`. #475: `fish_window_days` is counted in periods from a Monday, so its 7 is the calendar week, Monday to Sunday, and the round and each decision's answer carry `fishToSend`, whether the caller can send a fish today, from the same count as the refusal (`fishSent`) and an earned fish waiting; the phone offers the fish only while it is true, and on the card that holds the fish. #524 (ADR-036 §6): the round and each decision's answer carry `likesLeft`, the budget less the same count as the refusal (`likesSent`), a fish included, zero at the least; the phone says it in words.

#107 (ADR-044 §8): a fish past the window's budget uses one an invitation earned the person instead of being refused, oldest first, in `decide`'s transaction under the round's lock; the window's count leaves out what a unit paid for, so a banked unit never fills the budget it was used past; a fish changed to a like or a pass gives its unit back. Earned likes are gone (#503), and a fish needs one of the day's likes, an earned one too. The reward table is the rewards slice's: `decide` reaches it through `rewards/index.ts` (`spendEarned`, `releaseEarned`, `entriesPaidBy`, and `earnedFishWaiting` for `fishToSend`).

#99 (ADR-042, ADR-062 §10): the short pool. New people come first and earlier ones after them (`comingBackOrder`), counting only what was displayed or decided. Once no new person is left a round is short, and it offers to relax one of the person's own deal-breakers for the day (`GET /rounds/{day}/relaxations`, counted on asking, in the counter's steps); a tap records it on the day's round (`round_relaxation`) and adds a page of whom it lets in after the last place (`POST`, `appendRelaxed`, reason `relaxed` with `relaxed_field`), the queue going on to the rest once those are read, and one tap undoes it with the cards still undecided (`DELETE`, `undoRelaxation`). The pond is read by the builder (`relaxationOffers`, `relaxedCandidates` in `jobs/round-builder.ts`, handed in as `ShortPool`); `relaxedBy` (`pool.ts`) loosens only the person's side, so the other side's filters always hold. A like on a relaxed card waives the liker's deal-breakers that card came in under (`relaxed_with`) for its person, each time their queue asks. Every card names the caller's own deal-breakers its person did not answer (`notAnswered`).

#502 (ADR-062): one queue to the end of the pool. `readQueue` answers at most `QUEUE_PAGE` undecided cards that fit now, asking both people's filters on each read (`Queue.fits`), and with none left adds the next people (`Queue.fill`); entries are null before matching is open for the person, an empty list at the queue's end. A skip is the decision `skip`, spends nothing and starts no clock; a second pass is marked on the entry (`passedBefore`, `second_pass`). A new like asks the your-turn limit first (`Matches.yourTurn`, 409 `your_turn_limit`), before anything is read of the other person, then the filters, in `decide`'s transaction; a pass or a skip asks neither, so that a like can always be taken back.
