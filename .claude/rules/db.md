# Database (Drizzle + RDS PostgreSQL) rules

Consult `https://orm.drizzle.team/llms.txt` for API specifics.

- The schema is TypeScript in this package. Migrations come from `drizzle-kit generate`, are committed as SQL, reviewed as SQL, and never hand-edited. One migration per change. What the schema DSL cannot express (a trigger, a function, a versioned data row) goes through `drizzle-kit generate --custom --name <what>`: the tool creates the numbered file and the journal entry, the SQL inside is written for it, cited in an ADR, and reviewed like any other; a generated file is never edited afterwards. The API entrypoint applies them under an advisory lock; a manual RDS snapshot precedes a release-tag migration.
- Naming: snake_case tables and columns, `timestamptz` for every timestamp with an `_at` suffix, uuid primary keys unless there is a stated reason.
- `identity` and `account` are separate tables from the first migration. Standing and bans live on `identity`. `ponds` has `parent_id` from the first migration.
- Never add a column for: hetu, legal sex, full date of birth, name from the bank, message text in `events`, email in `events`.
- `preferences(account_id, field, value, mode hard|soft, include_unknown)`; deal-breakers are `mode = hard` rows. The round builder reads both parties' hard rows (`subjectsOfPond`, `jobs/pool.ts`) and applies them both ways (`inEachOthersPool`, ADR-035 §2).
- `events` is append-only with monthly partitions and 90-day retention, keyed by `research_id`, never `account_id`.
- `matching_config` is versioned. Parameters are rows, never constants in code. A new key is born in a migration (a `--custom` insert, its value also in the seed's `MATCHING_CONFIG_V1`); a new value is a new version, written by an admin in the panel with a reason (`reason`, #305) and the admin's identity id in `created_by`, or by a migration naming itself there. A version of a key that lives is never deleted or rewritten; a key nothing reads goes whole, by a migration that names it (ADR-060 §6).
- Erasure per TD-7: deletion removes profile, photos, preferences, the optional e-mail with its confirmation, the code waiting for it and the day's sends (`email_confirmed_at`, `email_code`, `email_send`, #385, ADR-043 §7), the photo check's notes (#253; twelve months otherwise), the consent rows (ADR-010 §14), the export requests (#237), the feedback (#171, ADR-023), the sanctions except the open bans and suspensions (#307, ADR-024 §8), likes, matches, bookmarks, push tokens, sessions, the person's participant link in conversations (#348, ADR-031: `account_id` goes null and the conversation closes; once both people are erased, the conversation goes with its messages), the person's stored contacts (#103, ADR-031 §11), the notification choices and the notices in the app (#176, ADR-032), the person's open invitations and the units of the reward ledger their invitations earned (`invitation`, `reward`, #107, ADR-044 §10), an invitation the person redeemed that has not counted, and an invitation of either side that would name nobody, the person's rounds with their entries and their day's relaxations (#99, ADR-042), and the entries in which the person is somebody's card (#96, ADR-034), whether the person told Kuutti they found someone (`found_them_answered`, #83, ADR-040), and the `research_id` mapping row; it keeps the identity row with its standing and the open bans and suspensions that carry it, the messages for the counterpart, the person's own among them (TD-1, ADR-031), the person's contact shares, ended by the close and kept as the record with no value in it until the conversation goes (#103), the reports filed and received, their notes and a conversation's frozen copy with them (`report_message`, #351, ADR-030 §12), for 12 months from filing (#84, ADR-030 §8), and the photos such a copy holds, taken off their messages all the same (which read "Photo removed") and counted as `heldPhotos`, until the night's prune lets them go with the last copy that names them, the blocks on the identity, whoever is erased, audit log entries for 5 years, the day's count of those who found someone (`found_them_count`, ADR-040), which names nobody, an invitation the person made that somebody redeemed, its inviter cut to null, so its redeemer has still redeemed one, ever, and keeps it in their export, and an invitation the person redeemed that has counted, its redeemer cut to null, so its inviter keeps what it earned (#107, ADR-044 §10).
- Indexes: 7-day exposure count per candidate, `last_active_at`, and the eligible-pool lookup. Add the index in the same migration as the query that needs it.
- The seed creates a few hundred fake verified users with valid-format hetus. Previews and local dev are always seeded, never a copy of production.
- RDS specifics: one connection pool, no RDS Proxy. LISTEN/NOTIFY and advisory locks are fine on the ordinary connection. Do not propose a pooler.
